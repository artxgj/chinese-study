**三、詩意詳細解析**

**（一）寫作背景**

〈望廬山瀑布〉是李白描繪廬山瀑布壯麗景色的著名七言絕句，作於唐玄宗開元十三年（725年）前後，時李白初出巴蜀，遊歷江南 [1]。廬山位於今江西省九江市，是中國著名的道教聖地與風景名勝區。此詩描寫廬山香爐峰瀑布的雄奇景象，以誇張的想像與生動的比喻，將瀑布的壯觀氣勢與詩人的浪漫情懷融為一體。

**（二）字詞釋義**

- **廬山**：位於今江西省九江市，中國著名風景名勝區。
- **香爐**：指廬山香爐峰，因山峰形狀像香爐而得名。
- **紫煙**：紫色的煙霧。陽光照耀下的水汽，呈現出紫色雲霧的景象。
- **遙看**：遠遠地望去。
- **瀑布**：從山壁上直瀉而下的水流。
- **掛前川**：像一條巨大的白練掛在山前的河流上。
- **飛流**：飛瀉而下的水流。
- **直下**：筆直地傾瀉而下。
- **三千尺**：形容瀑布極高（古代一尺約合今0.23公尺）。
- **疑是**：好像是，彷彿是。
- **銀河**：天上的銀河。
- **九天**：天空的最高處，傳說天有九重。

**（三）逐句解析**

- **首句「日照香爐生紫煙」**：詩人從遠處著筆，描寫陽光照耀香爐峰，峰頂雲霧繚繞，在陽光的映射下呈現出紫色的煙霞。這句為瀑布的出現鋪墊了背景，營造出神秘而壯麗的氛圍 [1]。

- **次句「遙看瀑布掛前川」**：詩人將視線轉向瀑布，遠遠望去，巨大的瀑布像一條白色的綢緞懸掛在山前的河流之上。「掛」字用得非常巧妙，化動為靜，將奔騰的瀑布寫得像一幅靜態的畫卷，突出了瀑布的壯觀與詩人的驚嘆 [2]。

- **第三句「飛流直下三千尺」**：詩人進一步描寫瀑布的動態，「飛流」寫出其速度之快，「直下」寫出其氣勢之猛，「三千尺」則極言其高度。這句詩將瀑布的壯觀氣勢展現得淋漓盡致。

- **末句「疑是銀河落九天」**：詩人以神奇的想像作結，將瀑布比作從天上傾瀉而下的銀河。「疑是」二字將寫實與想像融為一體，使整首詩的境界得到了升華，充滿了浪漫主義色彩 [1]。

**（四）整體意境與主題**

這首詩以極度誇張的手法與豐富的想像力，描繪了廬山瀑布的壯麗景象，展現了李白對大自然奇觀的驚嘆與讚美 [1]。全詩由遠及近，由靜到動，最後以「銀河落九天」的奇思妙想將全詩推向高潮。

詩中的瀑布不僅是自然景觀的寫照，更象徵著詩人胸中奔騰不息的激情與豪情壯志。整首詩氣勢磅礡，想像奇特，語言奔放，充分體現了李白浪漫主義詩歌的藝術魅力。

**（五）藝術特色**

- **誇張手法**：「三千尺」極言瀑布之高，「落九天」極言瀑布之源，將自然景物無限放大，增強了詩的感染力。
- **神奇想像**：將瀑布比作銀河，化平凡為神奇，體現了李白超乎尋常的想像力。
- **煉字精妙**：「生」寫出煙霧的動態美，「掛」寫出瀑布的靜態美，「飛」與「直」寫出瀑布的速度與氣勢，字字珠璣。
- **情景交融**：壯麗的瀑布與詩人的浪漫情懷融為一體，達到了「一切景語皆情語」的境界。

**III. Detailed Analysis**

**(1) Historical Background**

"Wang Lushan Pubu" is a famous seven-character quatrain written by Li Bai around 725 AD, during his travels through Jiangnan shortly after leaving Shu (Sichuan) [1]. Mount Lu is located in present-day Jiujiang, Jiangxi Province, and is a famous Taoist site and scenic area. This poem describes the magnificent scenery of the waterfall at Xianglu Peak on Mount Lu, using grand exaggeration and vivid metaphor to blend the waterfall's spectacular power with the poet's romantic spirit.

**(2) Vocabulary Explanations**

- **廬山 (Lú Shān)**: Mount Lu, located in present-day Jiujiang, Jiangxi, a famous Chinese scenic area.
- **香爐 (Xiāng Lú)**: Incense Burner Peak on Mount Lu, named for its resemblance to a censer.
- **紫煙 (Zǐ Yān)**: Purple mist; the mist and clouds illuminated by sunlight take on a purple hue.
- **遙看 (Yáo Kàn)**: Viewing from afar.
- **瀑布 (Pù Bù)**: A waterfall.
- **掛前川 (Guà Qián Chuān)**: The waterfall hangs like a white silk banner in front of the mountain stream.
- **飛流 (Fēi Liú)**: The rushing, flying water.
- **直下 (Zhí Xià)**: Plunging straight down.
- **三千尺 (Sān Qiān Chǐ)**: Three thousand feet; an exaggerated height (one ancient "chi" ≈ 0.23 meters).
- **疑是 (Yí Shì)**: As if it were; it seems.
- **銀河 (Yín Hé)**: The Milky Way.
- **九天 (Jiǔ Tiān)**: The highest heaven (traditional belief has nine layers of heaven).

**(3) Line-by-Line Interpretation**

- **First Line: "The sun shines on Incense Burner Peak, giving rise to purple mist."**  
  The poet begins with a distant view: sunlight illuminates the peak, and the mist and clouds take on a purple hue, creating a mysterious and magnificent atmosphere [1]. This line sets the stage for the appearance of the waterfall.

- **Second Line: "From afar, I see the waterfall hanging before the mountain stream."**  
  The poet focuses on the waterfall, which appears from a distance like a white silk banner suspended in front of the mountain river. The word "hanging" (掛) is particularly effective, turning the dynamic waterfall into a static, painting-like image, emphasizing its grandeur and the poet's awe [2].

- **Third Line: "Its flying water plunges straight down three thousand feet."**  
  The poet uses the verbs "plunges" and "straight down" to capture the waterfall's overwhelming power and speed, while "three thousand feet" exaggerates its immense height. This line vividly conveys the waterfall's spectacular force.

- **Fourth Line: "I suspect it is the Milky Way falling from the highest heaven."**  
  The poem ends with a dazzling imaginative leap: the waterfall is not merely water, but the Milky Way itself, cascading from the sky. The phrase "I suspect" blends reality with imagination, elevating the poem to a realm of pure wonder and romanticism [1].

**(4) Overall Mood and Themes**

This poem uses hyperbole and vivid imagination to depict the magnificent scenery of the Lu Mountain waterfall, expressing Li Bai's profound awe and admiration for the wonders of nature [1]. The poem moves from a distant view to a close-up, from stillness to motion, culminating in the breathtaking image of the "Milky Way falling from the sky."

The waterfall is not just a natural phenomenon; it also symbolizes the poet's own surging passion and boundless ambition. The poem's grand scale, fantastic imagery, and unrestrained language are hallmarks of Li Bai's romantic style.

**(5) Artistic Features**

- **Exaggeration**: "Three thousand feet" and "falling from the highest heaven" amplify the natural scene, enhancing its emotional impact.
- **Fantastic Imagination**: The comparison of the waterfall to the Milky Way transforms the ordinary into the miraculous, showcasing Li Bai's extraordinary creativity.
- **Precise Word Choice**: "Gives rise to" (生) captures the dynamic mist; "hanging" (掛) captures the static waterfall; "plunges" and "straight down" capture its speed and power.
- **Fusion of Scene and Emotion**: The majestic waterfall is seamlessly merged with the poet's romantic spirit, achieving a state where "all scenery is emotional language."
