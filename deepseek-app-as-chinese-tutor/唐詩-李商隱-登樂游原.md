**三、詩意詳細解析**

**（一）寫作背景**

李商隱（約813–858）是晚唐最具代表性的詩人之一，身處牛李黨爭的政治漩渦中，一生抑鬱不得志。〈登樂遊原〉作於晚年，樂遊原地處長安城南，地勢高敞，是唐代文人雅士登高望遠的著名勝地。詩人於暮色中驅車前往，面對即將落下的夕陽，寫下這首千古絕唱。

**（二）字詞釋義**

- **向晚**：接近傍晚。「向」為將近、接近之意。
- **意不適**：心情不愉快、情緒不佳。
- **驅車**：駕車。此處指乘車前往。
- **古原**：指樂遊原。稱「古」是因為此地自西漢以來即為遊覽勝地，歷史悠久。
- **無限好**：無窮無盡的美好。
- **只是**：只可惜、只不過是。
- **近黃昏**：接近日落之時。

**（三）逐句解析**

- **首句「向晚意不適」**  
  詩人開篇直接點出時間與心境——傍晚時分，心情鬱悶。這句為全詩定下惆悵、低落的基調。李商隱並未說明「意不適」的具體原因，留給讀者想像空間，可能是仕途失意、人生感慨，或僅是暮色引發的莫名愁緒。

- **次句「驅車登古原」**  
  為了排遣心中的不快，詩人駕車登上樂遊原。這句是行動描寫，「驅車」表現急切或無奈的心情，「古原」則暗示此處承載著歷史厚重感，詩人登高望遠，企圖在開闊視野中尋求慰藉。

- **第三句「夕陽無限好」**  
  登上古原後，詩人看見壯麗的夕陽景色——晚霞滿天，光芒萬丈，美得無窮無盡。這句是全詩的轉折，情緒從低落轉為對自然之美的讚嘆，語氣高昂，充滿驚豔之感。

- **末句「只是近黃昏」**  
  然而，這份美好轉瞬即逝，因為夕陽已接近地平線，夜色將至。一個「只是」急轉直下，將前面的讚嘆瞬間拉回現實的無奈。這句既是寫景，也是抒情，道出詩人對美好事物難以長久的深刻惋惜。

**（四）整體意境與主題**

這首詩僅二十字，卻蘊含極深的人生哲理與時代感懷。從表面看，它是一首寫景詩，記錄詩人登高所見的夕陽美景；但更深層看，它是李商隱對**人生、時代與命運**的隱喻。

- **時間的無情**：夕陽雖美，卻無法阻止黑夜降臨。這象徵一切美好事物——青春、盛事、理想——終將逝去的必然規律。
- **個人的感慨**：「意不適」的開篇與「近黃昏」的結尾形成呼應，暗示詩人對自身暮年、仕途無望的哀嘆。李商隱一生懷才不遇，這首詩正是他對生命晚景的自我寫照。
- **時代的縮影**：晚唐國勢日衰，曾經輝煌的大唐帝國正如那「無限好」的夕陽，燦爛卻已近尾聲。詩人對夕陽的惋惜，某種程度上也是對一個時代的告別。

**（五）藝術特色**

- **對比強烈**：「無限好」與「近黃昏」形成巨大張力，美好與消逝並存，讚嘆與惋惜交織。
- **以小見大**：短短二十字，從個人一時的心情出發，昇華到對生命、歷史、時代的普遍性思考。
- **語言凝練**：全詩無一贅字，卻層層遞進，情感轉折自然流暢，是唐詩中「以景寓情」的典範之作。

這首詩之所以千古傳誦，正是因為它用最簡潔的語言，捕捉了人類面對美好事物消逝時那份共通的、深沉的無奈與感傷。

**III. Detailed Analysis**

**(1) Historical Background**

Li Shangyin (c. 813–858) was one of the most representative poets of the late Tang Dynasty. He lived amidst the fierce Niu-Li factional struggles and suffered from political frustration throughout his life. "Deng Le You Yuan" was written in his later years. Leyouyuan (Happy Touring Plain) was a famous elevated site south of Chang'an, beloved by Tang literati for its panoramic views. At dusk, the poet drove there and, facing the setting sun, composed this timeless masterpiece.

**(2) Key Word Definitions**

- **向晚 (Xiàng wǎn)** : Approaching evening. "向" means "nearing" or "about to".
- **意不適 (Yì bù shì)** : Feeling displeased or emotionally unsettled.
- **驅車 (Qū chē)** : To drive a chariot/carriage. Here, it means to ride in a carriage to a destination.
- **古原 (Gǔ yuán)** : Refers to Leyouyuan itself. "Ancient" because it had been a scenic spot since the Western Han Dynasty, carrying centuries of historical weight.
- **無限好 (Wú xiàn hǎo)** : Boundlessly beautiful; splendid beyond measure.
- **只是 (Zhǐ shì)** : Unfortunately; it is just that...
- **近黃昏 (Jìn huáng hūn)** : Approaching dusk; nearing sunset.

**(3) Line-by-Line Interpretation**

- **First line, "Xiàng wǎn yì bù shì" (At dusk, my heart is not at ease):**  
  The poet opens by directly stating the time and mood – it is evening, and he feels melancholy. This sets a somber, dejected tone for the entire poem. Li Shangyin does not specify the cause of his unease, leaving it open to interpretation: perhaps frustrated ambition, existential reflection, or simply the vague sadness that dusk evokes.

- **Second line, "Qū chē dēng gǔ yuán" (I drive my chariot up to the ancient plain):**  
  To escape his gloom, the poet rides to the high plain of Leyouyuan. This describes action – "driving" conveys a sense of urgency or resignation, while "ancient plain" evokes historical depth, suggesting the poet seeks solace in vastness and perspective.

- **Third line, "Xī yáng wú xiàn hǎo" (The setting sun is infinitely beautiful):**  
  Once atop the plain, the poet beholds a magnificent sunset – radiant clouds, brilliant light, beauty beyond measure. This line marks the poem's emotional turning point: the mood lifts from depression to awe and admiration, with a tone of exclamation and wonder.

- **Fourth line, "Zhǐ shì jìn huáng hūn" (Unfortunately, it is nearly dusk):**  
  Yet, this beauty is fleeting, for the sun is already dipping below the horizon, and night will soon fall. The word "unfortunately" (只是) creates a sharp reversal, yanking the previous admiration back to the bitter reality of transience. This line serves both as a scenic description and a lyrical sigh, giving voice to the poet's profound regret that beautiful things cannot endure.

**(4) Overall Mood and Theme**

Though just twenty characters long, this poem carries immense philosophical weight and historical poignancy. On the surface, it is a landscape poem, recording a beautiful sunset; but at a deeper level, it is a metaphor for **life, an era, and fate itself**.

- **Time's relentlessness:** The sunset is beautiful, yet it cannot stop the night. This symbolises the inescapable decay of all beautiful things – youth, glory, ideals.
- **Personal lament:** The opening "unease" echoes the closing "dusk", hinting at the poet's sorrow over his own advancing age and unfulfilled aspirations. Li Shangyin, a man of immense talent who never found his political place, projects his own twilight years onto the scene.
- **A microcosm of an era:** In the late Tang, the once-glorious empire was in decline. The "infinitely beautiful" sunset mirrors the fading grandeur of the Tang dynasty – radiant, yet nearing its end. The poet's regret for the sunset is, in a sense, his farewell to a passing age.

**(5) Artistic Features**

- **Stark contrast:** The juxtaposition of "infinitely beautiful" with "nearing dusk" creates a powerful tension between splendor and transience, praise and regret.
- **Finding the universal in the particular:** Twenty short words move from a personal, momentary mood to a universal meditation on life, history, and the fate of civilizations.
- **Extreme concision:** Every character is essential, the emotional arc flows naturally, and the poem stands as a classic example of "conveying sentiment through scenery" in Tang poetry.

This poem endures because it captures, in the simplest language, the universal and profound human sadness we all feel when faced with the inevitable passing of what we cherish most.
