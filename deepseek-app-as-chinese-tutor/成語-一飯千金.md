### 一飯千金（一饭千金）—— 成語故事詳解

#### 一、成語基本信息 | Basic Information

| 項目              | 內容                                                    |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| 成語              | 一飯千金（一饭千金）                                            |
| 拼音              | yī fàn qiān jīn                                       |
| 注音              | ㄧ ㄈㄢˋ ㄑㄧㄢ ㄐㄧㄣ                                         |
| 出處              | 西漢·司馬遷《史記·淮陰侯列傳》                                      |
| 解釋              | 比喻厚厚地報答對自己有恩的人                                        |
| English Meaning | To repay a minor kindness with extravagant generosity |

---

#### 二、成語出處（原文）| Original Source

《史記·淮陰侯列傳》：

「信釣於城下，諸漂母漂，有一母見信飢，飯信，竟漂數十日。」

又：「信至國，如所從食漂母，賜千金。」

##### English Translation:

"Han Xin was fishing beneath the city wall, where a group of washerwomen were washing silk floss. One of the women saw that Han Xin was hungry, so she gave him food. She continued to feed him for over ten days."

And also: "When Han Xin returned to his feudal state, he summoned the washerwoman who had once fed him and bestowed upon her a thousand taels of gold."

#### 三、成語故事（完整版）| The Complete Story

##### 中文版

韓信年少貧困

漢朝開國名將韓信，年輕時家境非常貧寒，生活十分困苦。他父母早逝，沒有謀生的能力，經常吃了上頓沒下頓。為了填飽肚子，他時常到淮陰城下的護城河邊釣魚，希望運氣好時能釣上幾條魚來解決生活問題。然而，釣魚畢竟不是可靠的謀生方式，因此他經常餓著肚子。

漂母贈飯

在韓信經常釣魚的地方，有許多漂母（在河邊清洗絲棉絮或舊衣布的老婆婆）在那裡做工。其中有一位老婆婆，看到韓信饑餓不堪、非常可憐，便將自己的飯菜分給他吃。不僅如此，她還連續數十日不斷地接濟他，每天給他飯吃。

韓信立誓報恩

韓信在艱難困苦中得到這位漂母的恩惠，內心萬分感激，便對她說：「將來我若成功發達，必定要重重地報答您！」

不料，漂母聽了之後不但不高興，反而生氣地說：「你一個堂堂大丈夫，連自己都養不活，多麼丟臉！我是可憐你才給你飯吃，難道是為了貪圖你的回報嗎？」

韓信聽了這番話，感到非常慚愧，從此不再像過去那樣渾渾噩噩度日。

功成名就，千金報恩

後來，韓信加入了劉邦的起義軍隊，憑藉卓越的軍事才能屢建戰功，最終擊敗了西楚霸王項羽，協助劉邦建立了漢朝。他與張良、蕭何並稱為「漢初三傑」。因戰功赫赫，韓信被封為楚王（一說齊王）。

功成名就之後，韓信始終沒有忘記年少貧困時漂母的恩惠。他派人尋找到當年那位給他飯吃的老婆婆，命人送上豐盛的酒菜，更饋贈黃金一千兩來答謝她當年的那一飯之恩。

漂母本想推辭，但最終還是接受了這份厚禮。

##### English Version

Han Xin's Poverty-Stricken Youth

Han Xin, a famous general who helped found the Han Dynasty, lived in extreme poverty in his youth. Having lost both parents as a child, he had no means of support and often went hungry. To fill his stomach, he would often go fishing by the moat outside the city of Huaiyin, hoping to catch a few fish to sustain himself. However, fishing was not a reliable way to make a living, so he frequently went without food.

The Washerwoman Shares Her Meal

By the river where Han Xin often fished, there were many elderly washerwomen (old women who washed silk floss or old clothes by the river) working. One of these old women saw that Han Xin was desperately hungry and took pity on him. She shared her own meal with him. Not only that, but she continued to help him day after day, giving him food for over ten days in a row.

Han Xin Vows to Repay Her

Deeply grateful for the washerwoman's kindness during his time of hardship, Han Xin said to her, "One day, when I achieve success and wealth, I will surely repay you handsomely!"

To his surprise, the old woman became upset and replied angrily, "You are a grown man who cannot even feed himself—how disgraceful! I gave you food out of pity, not because I expected any repayment from you!"

Ashamed by her words, Han Xin resolved to stop living aimlessly and to make something of himself.

Great Success and Repayment with a Thousand Gold

Later, Han Xin joined Liu Bang's rebel army. With his extraordinary military talent, he achieved numerous victories and ultimately defeated Xiang Yu, the Hegemon-King of Western Chu, helping Liu Bang establish the Han Dynasty. Along with Zhang Liang and Xiao He, he was known as one of the "Three Heroes of the Han Founding". In recognition of his outstanding contributions, Han Xin was enfeoffed as the King of Chu (or King of Qi, according to some accounts).

After achieving great fame and success, Han Xin never forgot the kindness the washerwoman had shown him during his impoverished youth. He sent men to find the old woman who had once fed him, presented her with fine food and wine, and bestowed upon her one thousand taels of gold in gratitude for that single meal she had given him so long ago.

The old woman tried to decline but ultimately accepted the generous gift.

#### 四、成語寓意 | Moral of the Story

##### 中文版

「一飯千金」這則成語告訴我們：受人恩惠，切莫忘記。即使在困難時受到的是微小幫助，那份恩情也是極其可貴的。等到自己有能力時，應當重重地報答施恩於自己的人。

值得深思的是，漂母當初贈飯時從未想過要回報，她的善舉出自純粹的同情與憐憫。這更凸顯了韓信知恩圖報的可貴品質——感恩之心遠比物質回報更為重要。

正如古語所云：「受人滴水之恩，當以湧泉相報。」

##### English Version

The idiom "A Meal for a Thousand Gold" teaches us: Never forget the kindness you have received from others. Even if the help given to you during difficult times was small, that kindness is invaluable. When you are able, you should generously repay those who have helped you.

What is particularly noteworthy is that the washerwoman never expected any reward when she shared her meal—her act of kindness came purely from compassion and pity. This makes Han Xin's gratitude all the more admirable—a grateful heart is far more important than material repayment.

As the ancient saying goes: "Repay a drop of water with a gushing spring."

#### 六、現代應用 | Modern Application

##### 中文版

在現代漢語中，「一飯千金」的使用依然活躍。它的意義已超越了物質回報，更強調銘記恩情、回饋社會的精神。這個成語常出現在表彰慈善、感恩教育的語境中。例如，企業家成功後大力資助教育事業，便可形容為懷有「一飯千金」之心。

##### English Version

The idiom "A Meal for a Thousand Gold" remains widely used in modern Chinese. Its meaning has transcended material repayment and now emphasizes remembering kindness and giving back to society. It is often used in contexts such as charitable recognition and gratitude education. For example, when an entrepreneur, after achieving success, generously supports educational causes, they can be described as having the spirit of "a meal for a thousand gold".

#### 七、總結 | Summary

**中文**： 「一飯千金」出自《史記·淮陰侯列傳》，講述韓信年少貧困時受漂母一飯之恩，功成名就後以千金相報的故事。這個成語歌頌了知恩圖報的傳統美德，提醒我們永遠不要忘記別人的幫助，並在有能力時慷慨回報。

**English**: "A Meal for a Thousand Gold" originates from Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian (Shiji), telling the story of how Han Xin, after receiving a meal from a washerwoman during his impoverished youth, repaid her with a thousand taels of gold after achieving great success. This idiom celebrates the traditional virtue of gratitude and repayment, reminding us never to forget the kindness of others and to repay generously when we are able.
