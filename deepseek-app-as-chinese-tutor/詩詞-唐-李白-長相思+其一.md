**三、詳細釋義（字面 + 深層）**

**1. 字面意思（逐句白話翻譯）**  
- **長相思，在長安。** —— 長久的思念啊，牽掛著遠方的長安城。  
- **絡緯秋啼金井闌，微霜淒淒簟色寒。** —— 秋天裡，紡織娘（絡緯）在金井欄邊鳴叫；薄薄的秋霜帶來寒意，竹蓆（簟）的顏色看起來也冰冷刺骨。  
- **孤燈不明思欲絕，卷帷望月空長嘆。** —— 一盞孤燈昏暗不明，思念之情幾乎令人肝腸寸斷；我捲起窗簾望見明月，只能徒然長嘆。  
- **美人如花隔雲端。** —— 那位美人像花一樣美好，卻遠隔在雲端之上，可望而不可及。  
- **上有青冥之長天，下有淥水之波瀾。** —— 抬頭有青蒼遼闊的長天，低頭有清澈碧綠的流水，波濤起伏。  
- **天長路遠魂飛苦，夢魂不到關山難。** —— 天那麼高、路那麼遠，連魂魄想要飛去都備受苦楚；夢中的靈魂也越不過重重關山險阻。  
- **長相思，摧心肝。** —— 這永無盡頭的相思之苦，簡直要把我的心肝都摧碎了。

**2. 深層含義（政治寄託與象徵）**  
這首詩不能只當普通情詩來讀，它有濃厚的「**香草美人**」傳統（繼承自屈原《離騷》）。在唐代，士人常以男女之情比喻君臣關係：

- **「長安」**：不僅是地理上的都城，更是**政治權力中心**的象徵，代表皇帝所在之處，也就是仕途理想的歸宿。  
- **「美人」**：表面指容貌姣好的女子，實則**暗喻君王**（唐玄宗）或詩人渴望追隨的明主。美人在「雲端」，暗示君臣阻隔、恩寵難及。  
- **「關山難」「魂飛苦」**：並非真的跋山涉水之苦，而是**隱喻仕途上的障礙**——奸臣當道、進諫無門，詩人雖然心向朝廷，卻屢遭排擠，無法實現政治抱負。  
- **寫作背景**：李白供奉翰林期間，雖曾受玄宗賞識，但終因權貴讒言而被「賜金放還」。這首詩極可能作於離開長安之後，回憶當初在朝中的渴望與失落。  
- **情感昇華**：表面寫「相思」，實寫「**思君**」與「**思治世**」。詩中的淒涼秋景、孤燈長嘆，襯托的不僅是兒女情長，更是一位懷才不遇的知識分子對明君的渴望、對理想的執著，以及理想幻滅後的心碎。

**III. Detailed Explanation (Literal + Deeper Meaning)**

**1. Literal Meaning (Line‑by‑line vernacular translation)**  
- **"Everlasting longing, in Chang'an."** – My endless yearning is fixed on distant Chang'an.  
- **"Crickets cry by the golden well‑rail in autumn; light frost chills the mat's cold color."** – In autumn, katydids chirp by the golden well‑railing; the faint frost brings a biting chill, making the bamboo mat feel icy to the touch.  
- **"A lone lamp flickers, my longing almost breaks me; I roll up the curtain, gaze at the moon, and sigh in vain."** – Beneath a dim solitary lamp, lovesickness nearly tears me apart; I raise the blind to look at the moon, but can only heave empty sighs.  
- **"The beautiful lady is like a flower, but separated by the clouds."** – That fair lady, as lovely as a blossom, is far beyond the clouds – visible but unreachable.  
- **"Above is the vast blue sky; below are the rippling green waters."** – Looking up, I see the boundless azure heavens; looking down, I see the emerald waters surging with waves.  
- **"The sky is far, the road long – my soul's flight is bitter; even my dreaming soul cannot cross the mountain passes."** – The heavens are so high and the road so distant that even my spirit suffers in trying to fly there; my dream‑self cannot break through the rugged mountain barriers.  
- **"Everlasting longing crushes my heart and liver."** – This ceaseless, endless longing is grinding my very heart and liver to pieces.

**2. Deeper Political Allegory**  
This poem must not be read merely as a love lyric – it inherits the **"fragrant plants and beautiful women"** tradition (from Qu Yuan's *Li Sao*). In Tang‑dynasty literary culture, scholars often used romantic imagery to encode the ruler‑subject relationship:

- **"Chang'an"** is not just a geographic capital; it is a **symbol of political power** – the seat of the emperor and the ultimate destination of a scholar‑official's ambition.  
- **"The beautiful lady"** ostensibly refers to a ravishing woman, but in fact **alludes to the emperor** (Emperor Xuanzong) or the enlightened ruler the poet longs to serve. Her being "beyond the clouds" implies the ruler is inaccessible, hinting at the poet's failure to gain lasting imperial favor.  
- **"Mountain passes"** and **"bitter soul‑flight"** do not describe actual travel hardships; they are **metaphors for political obstacles** – treacherous ministers blocking the way, remonstrance ignored. Though Li Bai's heart faced the court, he was repeatedly sidelined and could not realize his political dreams.  
- **Historical context**: Li Bai once served as a Hanlin academician and enjoyed the emperor's favor briefly, but was eventually dismissed with a monetary gift due to slander from powerful officials. This poem was likely written after he left Chang'an, recalling his dashed hopes.  
- **Emotional culmination**: The surface "yearning" is actually **"yearning for the sovereign"** and **"yearning for good governance."** The desolate autumn scenery, the solitary lamp, and the vain sighs do not merely convey romantic loss – they speak of a frustrated intellectual's persistent longing for an enlightened ruler, and the heartbreak that follows when that ideal shatters.
