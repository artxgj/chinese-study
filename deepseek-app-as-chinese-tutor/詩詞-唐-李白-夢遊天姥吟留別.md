**三、詩意詳細解析**

**（一）寫作背景**

〈夢遊天姥吟留別〉是李白於唐玄宗天寶四載（745年）離開長安後，在東魯（今山東）準備南遊越中（今浙江）時所作，是李白詩作中極具代表性的浪漫主義長詩 [1]。詩題中的「吟」為古詩體裁之一（歌行體），「留別」指留贈給即將分別的友人。此時李白因受權貴排擠，被「賜金放還」，離開長安已一年餘，政治理想受挫，心緒複雜，此詩即借夢遊天姥的奇幻經歷，抒發其對自由與仙境的嚮往，以及對功名利祿的鄙棄 [1][2]。

**（二）字詞釋義**

- **天姥**：山名，在今浙江新昌東，傳說登山者能聞仙人天姥之歌。
- **瀛洲**：傳說中東海的三座仙山之一（另兩座為蓬萊、方丈）。
- **煙濤**：煙霧籠罩的波濤。
- **微茫**：隱約模糊。
- **越人**：今浙江一帶的人。
- **雲霞明滅**：雲霞忽明忽暗。
- **五嶽**：中國五大名山（泰山、華山、衡山、恆山、嵩山）。
- **赤城**：山名，在今浙江天台縣北，土石皆赤。
- **天台**：山名，在今浙江天台縣北。
- **因之**：依據越人的話。
- **鏡湖**：在今浙江紹興。
- **剡溪**：在今浙江嵊州。
- **謝公**：指南朝詩人謝靈運，曾在剡溪一帶遊歷。
- **淥水**：清澈的水。
- **謝公屐**：謝靈運特製的登山木屐，上山時去掉前齒，下山時去掉後齒。
- **青雲梯**：比喻高峻入雲的山路。
- **天雞**：傳說中天上的雞，日出時長鳴。
- **列缺**：閃電。
- **霹靂**：雷聲。
- **洞天**：道家所說的仙洞。
- **石扉**：石門。
- **訇然**：巨大聲響。
- **青冥**：青天。
- **金銀臺**：神仙居住的華麗樓臺。
- **雲之君**：雲中的神仙。
- **鸞**：傳說中鳳凰一類的神鳥。
- **魂悸**：心跳加速。
- **魄動**：魂魄震動。
- **恍**：恍惚。
- **長嗟**：長嘆。
- **向來**：先前。
- **白鹿**：傳說中仙人的坐騎。
- **青崖**：青翠的山崖。
- **摧眉折腰**：低頭彎腰，比喻趨炎附勢、卑躬屈膝。
- **權貴**：有權勢的貴族。

**（三）逐句解析**

- **「海客談瀛洲，煙濤微茫信難求」**  
  詩人起筆以傳說中的仙山瀛洲入手，海上客人談起瀛洲，但煙波浩渺，實在難以尋覓。以虛寫實，為下文「夢遊」做鋪墊。

- **「越人語天姥，雲霞明滅或可睹」**  
  越人談起天姥山，雲霞明滅之間或可窺見。由虛入實，點出詩人真正要夢遊的對象——天姥山。

- **「天姥連天向天橫，勢拔五嶽掩赤城」**  
  描寫天姥山的高峻雄偉：山勢連天，超越五嶽，遮蔽赤城。以誇張手法極言其壯麗。

- **「天台四萬八千丈，對此欲倒東南傾」**  
  天台山雖高，但面對天姥山卻顯得低矮，彷彿要向東南傾倒。進一步襯托天姥山之高。

- **「我欲因之夢吳越，一夜飛度鏡湖月」**  
  詩人決定依越人之言夢遊吳越，一夜之間飛越鏡湖之上的明月。進入夢境。

- **「湖月照我影，送我至剡溪」**  
  湖上的明月映照著詩人的身影，一路護送他到剡溪。夢境中的浪漫旅程開始。

- **「謝公宿處今尚在，淥水蕩漾清猿啼」**  
  謝靈運當年的住宿之地至今尚在，清澈的溪水蕩漾，猿猴清亮的啼聲迴盪。穿越時空，與前代詩人共遊。

- **「腳著謝公屐，身登青雲梯」**  
  穿上謝公特製的登山屐，登上直入青雲的石階。開始登山。

- **「半壁見海日，空中聞天雞」**  
  在山腰看見海上日出，聽見天雞在空中長鳴。從視覺與聽覺寫山之高、之奇。

- **「千岩萬轉路不定，迷花倚石忽已暝」**  
  千岩萬轉，山路曲折，迷戀於山花與怪石之間，不知不覺天色已暗。寫山路的複雜與詩人的忘我。

- **「熊咆龍吟殷巖泉，慄深林兮驚層巔」**  
  熊在咆哮，龍在吟嘯，聲音震動山岩與泉水，使深林戰慄，使層巔震驚。以恐怖的聲景渲染山林的神秘。

- **「雲青青兮欲雨，水澹澹兮生煙」**  
  雲層青黑，彷彿將要下雨；水面盪漾，升起煙霧。描寫山中天氣變幻，營造迷離氛圍。

- **「列缺霹靂，丘巒崩摧」**  
  閃電劃破天際，雷聲轟鳴，山丘崩裂。仙境出現前的劇變。

- **「洞天石扉，訇然中開」**  
  仙境的石門轟然打開，進入神仙洞府。神聖時刻的降臨。

- **「青冥浩蕩不見底，日月照耀金銀臺」**  
  青天浩蕩無邊，日月照耀著金銀裝飾的仙臺。描寫仙境的壯麗輝煌。

- **「霓為衣兮風為馬，雲之君兮紛紛而來下」**  
  彩虹為衣，風為馬，雲中的神仙紛紛降臨。仙人的華麗登場。

- **「虎鼓瑟兮鸞回車，仙之人兮列如麻」**  
  老虎彈奏瑟琴，鸞鳥駕車迴旋，仙人群集，如麻般眾多。極寫仙境之熱鬧與神奇。

- **「忽魂悸以魄動，恍驚起而長嗟」**  
  突然魂魄震動，恍惚驚醒，長嘆一聲。夢境驟然結束，回到現實。

- **「惟覺時之枕席，失向來之煙霞」**  
  醒來時只有枕席，先前的煙霞仙境已然消失。夢醒後的失落感。

- **「世間行樂亦如此，古來萬事東流水」**  
  世間的歡樂也如夢境一般，古往今來萬事如東流水一去不返。由夢悟理，感嘆人生如夢。

- **「別君去兮何時還？且放白鹿青崖間」**  
  離別友人之時，何日能再回來？暫且將白鹿放養在青崖之間。表現詩人的灑脫與不羈。

- **「須行即騎訪名山」**  
  需要行走時就騎上白鹿去探訪名山大川。顯示詩人對自由遊歷的嚮往。

- **「安能摧眉折腰事權貴，使我不得開心顏」**  
  詩人發出千古名句：怎能低眉順眼、卑躬屈膝地去侍奉權貴，讓自己得不到快樂呢？這是李白對權貴的蔑視與對自由的宣言，是全詩的靈魂與高潮 [3][4]。

**（四）整體意境與主題**

這首詩以一場奇幻的夢遊為線索，將神話傳說、自然景物與個人情感熔於一爐。全詩結構可分為三個層次 [2]：

- **入夢前**：從「海客談瀛洲」到「對此欲倒東南傾」，寫天姥山的高峻與神秘，為夢遊做鋪墊。
- **夢中遊**：從「我欲因之夢吳越」到「仙之人兮列如麻」，以瑰麗的筆觸描寫夢遊天姥的奇幻經歷，包括登山、見奇景、遇仙人，極盡浪漫之能事。
- **夢醒後**：從「忽魂悸以魄動」到「使我不得開心顏」，由夢醒的失落轉為人生哲理的領悟，最後以對權貴的蔑視收束全詩，展現李白豪放不羈的人格。

主題上，這首詩既是對仙境的禮讚，也是對現實的批判。李白借夢遊的奇幻經歷，表達了「人生如夢」的哲思，更以「安能摧眉折腰事權貴」的宣言，展現了中國文學中罕見的個人尊嚴與反抗精神 [1][4]。

**（五）藝術特色**

- **想像瑰麗**：詩中以天姥山、仙人、虎瑟鸞車等意象，構築出一個光怪陸離的仙境世界 [1]。
- **語言奔放**：全詩語言自由奔放，不受格律束縛，句式長短錯落，節奏變化多端，與「夢」的飄忽特質相呼應。
- **結構謹嚴**：看似雜亂無章，實則層次分明，從入夢、遊夢到夢醒，一氣呵成。
- **情感濃烈**：從對仙境的嚮往到夢醒的失落，再到對權貴的蔑視，情感起伏跌宕，真摯動人。

〈夢遊天姥吟留別〉是李白浪漫主義詩風的巔峰之作，也是中國文學史上最傑出的遊仙詩之一。它不僅展現了李白超凡的想像力，更通過「夢」的形式，表達了詩人對現實束縛的掙脫和對自由精神的追求 [1][2][4]。

〈梦游天姥吟留别〉  
唐・李白  

海客谈瀛洲，  
hǎi kè tán yíng zhōu，  

烟涛微茫信难求。  
yān tāo wēi máng xìn nán qiú。  

越人语天姥，  
yuè rén yǔ tiān mǔ，  

云霞明灭或可睹。  
yún xiá míng miè huò kě dǔ。  

天姥连天向天横，  
tiān mǔ lián tiān xiàng tiān héng，  

势拔五岳掩赤城。  
shì bá wǔ yuè yǎn chì chéng。  

天台四万八千丈，  
tiān tāi sì wàn bā qiān zhàng，  

对此欲倒东南倾。  
duì cǐ yù dǎo dōng nán qīng。  

我欲因之梦吴越，  
wǒ yù yīn zhī mèng wú yuè，  

一夜飞度镜湖月。  
yī yè fēi dù jìng hú yuè。  

湖月照我影，  
hú yuè zhào wǒ yǐng，  

送我至剡溪。  
sòng wǒ zhì shàn xī。  

谢公宿处今尚在，  
xiè gōng sù chù jīn shàng zài，  

渌水荡漾清猿啼。  
lù shuǐ dàng yàng qīng yuán tí。  

脚著谢公屐，  
jiǎo zhuó xiè gōng jī，  

身登青云梯。  
shēn dēng qīng yún tī。  

半壁见海日，  
bàn bì jiàn hǎi rì，  

空中闻天鸡。  
kōng zhōng wén tiān jī。  

千岩万转路不定，  
qiān yán wàn zhuǎn lù bù dìng，  

迷花倚石忽已暝。  
mí huā yǐ shí hū yǐ míng。  

熊咆龙吟殷岩泉，  
xióng páo lóng yín yǐn yán quán，  

栗深林兮惊层巅。  
lì shēn lín xī jīng céng diān。  

云青青兮欲雨，  
yún qīng qīng xī yù yǔ，  

水澹澹兮生烟。  
shuǐ dàn dàn xī shēng yān。  

列缺霹雳，  
liè quē pī lì，  

丘峦崩摧。  
qiū luán bēng cuī。  

洞天石扉，  
dòng tiān shí fēi，  

訇然中开。  
hōng rán zhōng kāi。  

青冥浩荡不见底，  
qīng míng hào dàng bù jiàn dǐ，  

日月照耀金银台。  
rì yuè zhào yào jīn yín tái。  

霓为衣兮风为马，  
ní wéi yī xī fēng wéi mǎ，  

云之君兮纷纷而来下。  
yún zhī jūn xī fēn fēn ér lái xià。  

虎鼓瑟兮鸾回车，  
hǔ gǔ sè xī luán huí chē，  

仙之人兮列如麻。  
xiān zhī rén xī liè rú má。  

忽魂悸以魄动，  
hū hún jì yǐ pò dòng，  

恍惊起而长嗟。  
huǎng jīng qǐ ér cháng jiē。  

惟觉时之枕席，  
wéi jué shí zhī zhěn xí，  

失向来之烟霞。  
shī xiàng lái zhī yān xiá。  

世间行乐亦如此，  
shì jiān xíng lè yì rú cǐ，  

古来万事东流水。  
gǔ lái wàn shì dōng liú shuǐ。  

别君去兮何时还？  
bié jūn qù xī hé shí huán？  

且放白鹿青崖间，  
qiě fàng bái lù qīng yá jiān，  

须行即骑访名山。  
xū xíng jí qí fǎng míng shān。  

安能摧眉折腰事权贵，  
ān néng cuī méi zhé yāo shì quán guì，  

使我不得开心颜？  
shǐ wǒ bù dé kāi xīn yán？


**III. Detailed Analysis**

**(1) Historical Background**

"Meng You Tian Mu Yin Liu Bie" is a highly representative romantic long poem written by Li Bai in 745 AD (the 4th year of the Tianbao era), after he left Chang'an and was in Donglu (present-day Shandong), preparing to travel south to Yuezhong (present-day Zhejiang) [1]. The character "吟" in the title refers to a genre of ancient poetry (a type of song-form), and "留别" means "a farewell gift" to friends he was about to leave. By this time, Li Bai had been "honorably dismissed" from the court due to political intrigue and had been away from Chang'an for over a year. His political aspirations thwarted, he expressed his longing for freedom and the immortal realm, as well as his disdain for fame and wealth, through this dream-journey to Tianmu Mountain [1][2].

**(2) Vocabulary Explanations**

- **天姥 (Tiān Mǔ)**: A mountain in present-day Xinchang, Zhejiang, legend has it that those who climb it can hear the song of the celestial goddess Tianmu.
- **瀛洲 (Yíng Zhōu)**: One of the three legendary fairy islands in the Eastern Sea (the other two are Penglai and Fangzhang).
- **煙濤 (Yān Tāo)**: Mist-covered waves.
- **微茫 (Wēi Máng)**: Vague and indistinct.
- **越人 (Yuè Rén)**: People from the Yue region (present-day Zhejiang).
- **雲霞明滅 (Yún Xiá Míng Miè)**: Clouds and mists brightening and fading.
- **五嶽 (Wǔ Yuè)**: China's five sacred mountains (Mount Tai, Hua, Heng, Heng, Song).
- **赤城 (Chì Chéng)**: A mountain in present-day Tiantai, Zhejiang, with reddish soil and rocks.
- **天台 (Tiān Tāi)**: A mountain in present-day Tiantai, Zhejiang.
- **因之 (Yīn Zhī)**: Based on the Yue people's words.
- **鏡湖 (Jìng Hú)**: A lake in present-day Shaoxing, Zhejiang.
- **剡溪 (Shàn Xī)**: A river in present-day Shengzhou, Zhejiang.
- **謝公 (Xiè Gōng)**: The Southern Dynasties poet Xie Lingyun, who travelled in the Shansi region.
- **淥水 (Lù Shuǐ)**: Clear water.
- **謝公屐 (Xiè Gōng Jī)**: A special wooden mountain-climbing shoe made by Xie Lingyun; the front teeth were removed for going uphill and the back teeth for going downhill.
- **青雲梯 (Qīng Yún Tī)**: A metaphor for a high, steep mountain path.
- **天雞 (Tiān Jī)**: A legendary celestial rooster that crows at sunrise.
- **列缺 (Liè Quē)**: Lightning.
- **霹靂 (Pī Lì)**: Thunderclap.
- **洞天 (Dòng Tiān)**: A Taoist celestial cave.
- **石扉 (Shí Fēi)**: A stone door.
- **訇然 (Hōng Rán)**: With a loud crash.
- **青冥 (Qīng Míng)**: The blue sky.
- **金銀臺 (Jīn Yín Tái)**: Magnificent terraces where immortals dwell.
- **雲之君 (Yún Zhī Jūn)**: Immortals in the clouds.
- **鸞 (Luán)**: A legendary bird similar to the phoenix.
- **魂悸 (Hún Jì)**: Palpitations of the heart.
- **魄動 (Pò Dòng)**: Trembling of the soul.
- **恍 (Huǎng)**: In a daze.
- **長嗟 (Cháng Jiē)**: A long sigh.
- **向來 (Xiàng Lái)**: Previously, before.
- **白鹿 (Bái Lù)**: A legendary mount of immortals.
- **青崖 (Qīng Yá)**: Green cliffs.
- **摧眉折腰 (Cuī Méi Zhé Yāo)**: To bow and scrape, metaphor for fawning on the powerful.
- **權貴 (Quán Guì)**: The powerful and wealthy.

**(3) Line-by-Line Interpretation**

- **"The sea-traveler speaks of Yingzhou, / In misty waves, hard to find."**  
  The poet begins with the legendary fairy island Yingzhou, which, according to sea-travelers, is hidden in misty waves and difficult to find. This introduces a theme of the elusive and the mysterious, setting the stage for the dream journey.

- **"The Yue people speak of Tianmu, / Its clouds and mists may be glimpsed."**  
  The Yue people speak of Tianmu Mountain, whose clouds and mists can sometimes be seen. This moves from the completely fictional Yingzhou to the more tangible but still mysterious Tianmu, grounding the poem in a real location.

- **"Tianmu spans the sky, crossing the heavens, / Its might surpasses the Five Sacred Mountains and overshadows Chicheng."**  
  This hyperbolic description emphasizes the immense height and power of Tianmu Mountain. It is not merely a mountain but a cosmic force.

- **"Mount Tiantai, forty-eight thousand zhang high, / Seems to lean southeast before it."**  
  Even the famously high Mount Tiantai appears small and leaning before Tianmu, further exaggerating its grandeur.

- **"I wish to dream of Wu and Yue, / And fly over Mirror Lake in a single night."**  
  The poet announces his intention to dream of the southern regions, and magically flies over Mirror Lake, entering the dream realm.

- **"The lake moon reflects my shadow, / Escorting me to the Shanxi River."**  
  The moonlit lake casts the poet's shadow, accompanying him to the Shanxi River, a place associated with the poet Xie Lingyun.

- **"Xie's lodging place still remains, / Clear waters ripple, while apes cry in the mountains."**  
  The poet imagines the scene of the ancient poet Xie Lingyun, creating a sense of timelessness and continuity with the past.

- **"I wear Xie's clogs, / And ascend the green cloud ladder."**  
  The poet puts on Xie's special mountain-climbing shoes, symbolizing a step into a spiritual and elevated state.

- **"Halfway up, I see the sun over the sea; / I hear the celestial rooster in the sky."**  
  The mountain's height is so great that halfway up, the poet witnesses the sunrise over the ocean and hears the legendary celestial rooster, a sign of entering a supernatural realm.

- **"A thousand crags, a myriad turns, the path is uncertain; / Enchanted by flowers, I lean on rocks, suddenly it is dusk."**  
  The mountain journey becomes increasingly complex and disorienting, and the poet, lost in wonder, loses track of time.

- **"Bears roar, dragons chant, shaking the mountain springs; / The deep forest trembles, the layered peaks are shocked."**  
  The soundscape becomes terrifying, with the roars of mythical beasts echoing through the mountains.

- **"The clouds are dark, as if about to rain; / The waters are rippling, rising with mist."**  
  The atmosphere shifts to one of impending change, creating a sense of unease and anticipation.

- **"A flash of lightning, a thunderclap, / The hills and peaks collapse."**  
  The sudden, violent storm signals a dramatic transformation.

- **"The stone gate of the celestial cavern / Opens with a mighty crash."**  
  The climax of the dream: the entrance to the immortal realm is revealed.

- **"The blue void is vast and bottomless; / The sun and moon shine on the golden and silver terraces."**  
  The poet describes the breathtaking, boundless beauty of the celestial realm.

- **"Rainbows for clothes, wind for steeds, / The lords of the clouds descend in multitudes."**  
  The immortals make their grand, spectacular appearance, riding the elements.

- **"Tigers pluck their zithers, phoenixes draw their carriages; / The immortals line up like hemp."**  
  The celestial court is filled with magical creatures and a multitude of immortals, creating a scene of overwhelming wonder.

- **"Suddenly, my soul trembles, my spirit stirs; / In a daze, I awake and give a long sigh."**  
  The dream ends abruptly, and the poet is jolted back to reality, feeling a sense of loss.

- **"I only feel the pillow and mat, / And have lost the mist and clouds of just now."**  
  The contrast between the dream's splendor and waking reality is stark. The poet is left with only the mundane.

- **"Worldly joys are also like this; / Since ancient times, all things flow east like water."**  
  The poet reflects that all worldly pleasures are as transient as the dream, echoing the Buddhist and Daoist concept of impermanence.

- **"When I leave you, when will I return? / For now, I'll release the white deer among the green cliffs."**  
  The poet turns to his friends, expressing uncertainty about his return, and symbolically frees himself from worldly constraints by releasing the immortal white deer.

- **"When I need to go, I'll ride and visit famous mountains."**  
  He reaffirms his intention to live a free, wandering life, exploring the natural world.

- **"How can I bow and scrape to serve the powerful, / And never have a moment of joy?"**  
  The final, powerful lines are a defiant rejection of the corrupt, official world. The poet chooses his own happiness and integrity over submission to those in power. This is the poem's soul and climax [3][4].

**(4) Overall Mood and Themes**

The poem is structured as a fantastic dream journey, weaving together myth, nature, and personal emotion. It can be divided into three parts [2]:

- **Before the Dream**: From "The sea-traveler speaks..." to "...leans southeast before it," this section introduces the mountain's magnificence, setting the stage for the dream.
- **During the Dream**: From "I wish to dream..." to "...like hemp," this is the core of the poem, a breathtakingly imaginative account of the dream journey, from ascent to encountering the immortals.
- **After the Dream**: From "Suddenly, my soul..." to the end, this section moves from the disillusionment of awakening to philosophical reflection, and finally to a defiant declaration of personal freedom.

Thematically, the poem is a celebration of the supernatural and a critique of the mundane. Li Bai uses the dream journey to express both his longing for transcendence and his rejection of earthly power. The poem's final, famous lines, "How can I bow and scrape to serve the powerful, and never have a moment of joy?" are a powerful testament to personal dignity and rebellion, rare in Chinese literature [1][4].

**(5) Artistic Features**

- **Vivid Imagination**: The poem creates a dazzling world of celestial mountains, immortals, and magical creatures [1].
- **Bold Language**: The language is free and unrestrained, breaking conventional poetic forms to match the erratic nature of a dream.
- **Structured Chaos**: Despite its seeming disorder, the poem follows a clear, three-part structure—prelude, dream, and reflection.
- **Intense Emotion**: The poem moves from wonder to awe to disappointment to defiance, creating a powerful emotional arc.

"Meng You Tian Mu Yin Liu Bie" is a masterpiece of Li Bai's romantic style and one of the most outstanding "immortal-quest" poems in Chinese literature. It showcases his extraordinary imagination and, through the medium of a dream, expresses a profound yearning for freedom [1][2][4].
