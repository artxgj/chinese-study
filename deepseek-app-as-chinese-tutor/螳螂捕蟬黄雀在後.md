#### 二、成語出處 / Origins

本成語最早見於兩部古代文獻：

This idiom first appears in two ancient Chinese texts:

1.《莊子·山木》（戰國） / Zhuangzi·Mountains and Trees (Warring States Period)

「睹一蟬，方得美蔭而忘其身；螳螂執翳而搏之，見得而忘其形；異鵲從而利之，見利而忘其真。」

"He saw a cicada that had found a nice shady spot but had forgotten its own safety; a mantis, hidden among the leaves, lunged at it, focused on its gain but forgetting its own form; an exotic magpie then took advantage, seeing the gain but forgetting its true self."

2. 漢·劉向《說苑·正諫》 / Liu Xiang's Garden of Stories·Straight Remonstrance (Han Dynasty)

「園中有樹，其上有蟬，蟬高居悲鳴飲露，不知螳螂在其後也；螳螂委身曲附，欲取蟬，而不知黃雀在其傍也。」

"In the garden there was a tree, and on the tree there was a cicada. The cicada sat high above, chirping mournfully and drinking dew, unaware that a mantis was behind it. The mantis crouched low and bent its forelegs, about to seize the cicada, unaware that an oriole was beside it."

#### 三、經典故事（吳王諫戰） / The Classic Story (The King of Wu and the Young Adviser)

##### 中文版 / Chinese Version

背景： 春秋戰國時期，吳國國君準備出兵攻打楚國。大臣們紛紛勸阻，但吳王不但聽不進去，反而憤怒地宣布：「誰敢再來勸阻，我就處死他！」

故事發展： 吳王身邊有一個年輕的門客（一說為吳王的兒子），他想勸諫卻不敢直言。於是，他每天清晨懷裡揣著彈弓，來到王宮的後花園，站在樹下仰頭張望，一連三個早晨，露水沾濕了他的衣服也不在意。

吳王見了覺得很奇怪，便問他：「你為什麼要這樣自討苦吃呢？」

年輕人回答說：

「這個園子裡有一棵樹，樹上有一隻蟬。蟬高高在上，悠閒地叫著，自由自在地吸著露水，卻不知道有一隻螳螂在牠身後呢！螳螂把身子緊緊地貼在隱蔽的地方，只想去捕蟬，卻不知道有一隻黃雀早已躲在牠的身後呢！黃雀正伸長了脖子想去啄螳螂，卻不知道在牠下面正有人拿著彈弓呢！

這三隻小生物，都只力求得到眼前的利益，卻完全不管身後隱伏著的禍患啊！」

結局： 吳王聽了恍然大悟，稱讚道：「你說得好啊！」於是取消了攻打楚國的計劃。

---

##### English Version

Background: During the Spring and Autumn Period (770–476 BCE), the King of the State of Wu decided to invade the State of Chu. His ministers all advised against it, but the king would not listen. Instead, he angrily declared: "If anyone dares to dissuade me from invading Chu again, I will put him to death!"

The Story Unfolds: A young retainer of the king (in some versions, the king's son) wanted to advise the king but did not dare to speak directly. So, carrying a slingshot, he went to the royal back garden every morning for three consecutive days. He stood under a tree, gazing upward, his clothes soaked through with morning dew.

The king noticed this and asked: "Why are you putting yourself through such hardship?"

The young man replied:

"There is a cicada on a tree in this garden. The cicada is high above, chirping leisurely and drinking dew freely, unaware that a mantis is right behind it. The mantis, pressing its body close in a concealed place, is intent on catching the cicada, unaware that an oriole is hiding right behind it. The oriole is stretching out its neck to peck at the mantis, unaware that a man with a slingshot is standing right beneath it.

These three small creatures are each focused solely on the benefit before them, while completely ignoring the disaster lurking behind them!"

The Outcome: Upon hearing these words, the King of Wu suddenly saw the light and said: "What you said is right!" Consequently, he abandoned his plan to invade the State of Chu.

#### 四、成語寓意 / Moral of the Idiom

##### 中文 / Chinese

「螳螂捕蟬，黃雀在後」原義是螳螂捕捉蟬，卻不知道黃雀在牠後面正想吃牠。這個成語比喻目光短淺，只想到算計別人、貪圖眼前利益，卻沒有想到背後正有人算計自己。

更深層的哲理在於警示人們：凡事要思慮周全、瞻前顧後。在競爭或對抗中，不可盲目冒進，因為當你專注於前方的目標時，危險可能正從背後悄悄逼近。

這個成語也諷刺了那些 「只顧眼前利益，不顧身後禍患」 的人，對鼠目寸光、利令智昏之人的提出警告。

##### English

The original meaning of "The mantis stalks the cicada, unaware of the oriole behind" is that the mantis is catching a cicada without realizing that an oriole is right behind it, ready to eat it. The idiom is a metaphor for shortsightedness — one thinks only of scheming against others and pursuing immediate gain, without realizing that others are scheming against them in return.

On a deeper level, it warns that one must think comprehensively and consider both what lies ahead and what lies behind. In competition or conflict, one should not blindly rush forward, because while you are focused on your target ahead, danger may be quietly approaching from behind.

The idiom also satirizes those who are "only concerned with immediate benefits while ignoring the calamities behind them" — a warning to the short-sighted and the profit-blinded.

##### 用法說明 / Usage:

這個成語在中文中一般充當分句，常用於警告或評論他人只顧眼前利益而忽略背後風險。

In Chinese, this idiom is generally used as a clause, often to warn others or comment on someone who focuses only on immediate gains while ignoring hidden risks.

##### 例句 / Example Sentences:

**中文**： 他處心積慮想併購對手公司，卻不知螳螂捕蟬，黃雀在後，另一家更大的企業早已盯上了他。

**English**: He schemed meticulously to acquire his rival company, unaware that the mantis stalks the cicada, unaware of the oriole behind — an even larger corporation had long had its eyes on him.

#### 七、總結 / Summary

「螳螂捕蟬，黃雀在後」是一則源自先秦寓言的中華經典成語。它通過生動的自然食物鏈場景——蟬→螳螂→黃雀→持彈弓者——深刻揭示了 「只顧眼前利益必將忽視背後危險」 的道理。這個成語不僅是語言的智慧結晶，更反映了中國古代重視謀略、強調深謀遠慮的文化傳統。

---

The idiom "The mantis stalks the cicada, unaware of the oriole behind" is a classic Chinese proverb originating from pre-Qin fables. Through a vivid natural food chain scene — cicada → mantis → oriole → slingshot bearer — it profoundly reveals the truth that "focusing only on immediate gain will inevitably lead one to ignore the dangers lurking behind". This idiom is not only a gem of linguistic wisdom but also reflects the ancient Chinese cultural tradition that values strategy and emphasizes far-sightedness and careful consideration.
