#### 二、成語釋義 | Meaning

##### 中文釋義

「圖窮匕見」指戰國時荊軻欲刺秦始皇，藏匕首於地圖中，地圖打開至盡頭時，露出匕首。後比喻事情發展到最後，形跡敗露，現出真相。

##### English Meaning

This idiom refers to the story of Jing Ke, who attempted to assassinate the First Emperor of Qin during the Warring States period by hiding a dagger inside a rolled map. When the map was completely unrolled, the dagger was revealed. Metaphorically, it means that as a situation develops to its final stage, the truth or hidden intention is exposed.

English translation: the real intention is revealed in the end

#### 三、典故出處 | Historical Origin

##### 中文出處

本成語最早出自西漢劉向《戰國策·燕策三》，原文記載：

「秦王謂軻曰：『起，取武陽所持圖。』軻既取圖奉之。發圖，圖窮而匕首見。」

《史記·刺客列傳》 亦有相同記載：

「秦王謂軻曰：『取舞陽所持地圖。』軻既取圖奏之，秦王發圖，圖窮而匕首見。因左手把秦王之袖，而右手持匕首揕之。」

##### English Origin

This idiom first appeared in Liu Xiang's Strategies of the Warring States (《戰國策》), chapter "Yan Ce III" , during the Western Han Dynasty:

"The King of Qin said to Ke: 'Rise and bring the map that Wuyang carries.' Ke took the map and presented it. As the map was unrolled, when it reached the end, the dagger appeared."

The same story is also recorded in Sima Qian's Records of the Grand Historian (《史記》), "Biography of Assassins":

"The King of Qin said to Ke: 'Bring the map that Wuyang carries.' Ke took the map and presented it. The King of Qin unrolled the map, and when it reached the end, the dagger appeared. Then Jing Ke grabbed the king's sleeve with his left hand and stabbed at him with the dagger in his right hand."

#### 四、完整成語故事 | Complete Idiom Story

##### 中文故事

背景： 戰國末期，秦國勢力強盛，不斷出兵侵佔其他國家的領土。秦王嬴政（後來的秦始皇）進行併吞六國的統一戰爭，先滅韓國和趙國，接著直搗燕國。燕國是一個地處北方的弱小國家，燕王曾將太子丹送到秦國做人質，但太子丹未受善待，後來逃回燕國。

策劃： 為挽救國家危亡，也為報仇，燕太子丹決定派刺客刺殺秦王。他物色了勇士荊軻擔任刺客，並指派秦舞陽為助手。

準備： 荊軻出發前，太子丹做了周密準備：

1. 帶上秦王重金懸賞的秦國叛將樊於期的首級作為禮物
2. 帶上燕國督亢地區（今河北涿縣東南）的地圖，假意割地求和
3. 將一把鋒利的匕首捲藏在地圖卷軸之中

出發： 荊軻告別太子丹時，慷慨高歌：「風蕭蕭兮易水寒，壯士一去兮不復還。」

行刺： 荊軻到秦國後，先以重金收買秦王寵臣蒙嘉，請他在秦王面前美言。秦王聽說燕國獻上督亢地圖和樊於期首級，非常高興，在咸陽宮接見。

荊軻捧著裝有樊於期頭顱的盒子，秦舞陽捧著裝地圖的匣子，走到殿前臺階時，秦舞陽臉色驚恐，荊軻趕緊解釋說：「北方偏遠地區的鄉下人，不曾見過天子，所以害怕，請大王寬容。」

接著荊軻獻上地圖，在秦王面前慢慢展開。當地圖完全展開到盡頭時，預先藏在裡面的匕首露了出來！

荊軻立刻左手抓住秦王的衣袖，右手抓起匕首刺向秦王。秦王大吃一驚，猛地站起來，掙斷了袖子。秦王想拔劍，但劍太長，一時拔不出來。荊軻追趕秦王，秦王繞著柱子逃跑。

結局： 最終荊軻行刺失敗，被秦王的侍衛們殺死。「圖窮匕見」這句成語便從這個驚心動魄的故事中演變而來。

##### English Story

Background: In the late Warring States period, the State of Qin grew increasingly powerful and continuously invaded other states. King Ying Zheng of Qin (later known as Qin Shi Huang, the First Emperor) embarked on a campaign to unify the six states. After conquering Han and Zhao, he advanced toward the State of Yan. Yan was a small, weak state in the north. The King of Yan had once sent Crown Prince Dan to Qin as a hostage, but Dan was mistreated and eventually escaped back to Yan.

The Plan: To save his state from annihilation and to seek revenge, Crown Prince Dan decided to send an assassin to kill the King of Qin. He selected the brave warrior Jing Ke as the assassin and assigned Qin Wuyang as his assistant.

Preparation: Before Jing Ke departed, Crown Prince Dan made thorough preparations:

1. Jing Ke would carry the head of Fan Yuqi — a general who had defected from Qin — as a gift, since the King of Qin had placed a heavy bounty on his head
2. He would carry a map of the Dukang region (in present-day Zhuozhou, Hebei) in Yan, pretending to offer the land in surrender
3. A sharp dagger was secretly rolled up inside the map scroll

Departure: As Jing Ke bid farewell to Crown Prince Dan, he sang a famous verse: "The wind sighs and the Yi River runs cold; once the brave man departs, he shall never return."

The Assassination Attempt: Upon arriving in Qin, Jing Ke bribed Meng Jia, a favorite minister of the King of Qin, to speak favorably of him. When the King heard that Yan was offering the Dukang map and Fan Yuqi's head, he was delighted and received them at Xianyang Palace.

Jing Ke carried the box containing Fan Yuqi's head, while Qin Wuyang followed with the map case. As they approached the palace steps, Qin Wuyang's face turned pale with fear. Jing Ke quickly explained: "He is a rustic from the remote north who has never seen the Son of Heaven. Please forgive him, Your Majesty."

Then Jing Ke presented the map and slowly unrolled it before the King. When the map was completely unrolled to its very end, the dagger hidden inside was revealed!

Jing Ke immediately grabbed the King's sleeve with his left hand and seized the dagger with his right hand, stabbing toward the King. The King was terrified, sprang up, and tore his sleeve free. He tried to draw his sword, but it was too long to pull out quickly. Jing Ke chased the King, who ran around a pillar to escape.

The End: In the end, Jing Ke's assassination attempt failed, and he was killed by the King's guards. The idiom "图穷匕见" (When the map is unrolled to the end, the dagger appears) was born from this dramatic and fateful story.

#### 七、現代應用 | Modern Usage

##### 中文

「圖窮匕見」在現代廣泛應用於政治評論、商業分析、社會事件報導及人際關係描述中。例如：商業競爭中對手的終極手段終於暴露、談判中一方的最終意圖被看穿、或日常生活中偽善者的真實面目被揭穿。

##### English

This idiom is widely used in modern contexts including political commentary, business analysis, social event reporting, and interpersonal relationships. For example: when a competitor's ultimate strategy in business is finally exposed, when one party's true intentions in a negotiation are seen through, or when a hypocrite's real face in daily life is uncovered.

---

**總結 Summary**： 「圖窮匕見」源自荊軻刺秦王的歷史悲劇，生動地描繪了「隱藏→鋪墊→最終暴露」的完整過程。這個成語提醒我們：任何精心掩飾的意圖，終有真相大白的一天。

*This idiom originates from the historical tragedy of Jing Ke's assassination attempt on the King of Qin. It vividly depicts the complete process of "concealment →铺垫 → final exposure". This idiom reminds us that no matter how carefully concealed one's intentions may be, the truth will eventually come to light. *
