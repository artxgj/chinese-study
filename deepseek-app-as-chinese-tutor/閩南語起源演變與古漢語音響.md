### 一、閩南語的起源與演變
### 1. Origin and Evolution of Hokkien (Southern Min)

閩南語屬漢語族閩語支，被學界視為**最保守的漢語方言之一**。其形成與歷史上幾次大規模的北方移民南遷直接相關。

Hokkien (Southern Min) belongs to the Min branch of the Sinitic language family. It is widely regarded as **one of the most conservative Chinese dialects**, and its formation is directly tied to several large‑scale southward migrations from northern China.

---

#### 主要形成階段 | Key Formation Stages

##### (1) 上古時期   (先秦至漢代）

**·** 秦漢時期中原政權開始進入福建，帶來上古漢語底層。語言學家推測，閩語祖先約在西元初年   (漢朝） 即已從上古漢語分化。

**·** During the Qin and Han dynasties, Central Plains authorities entered Fujian, introducing an Old Chinese substratum. Linguists suggest that the ancestor of Min had already diverged from Old Chinese around the early 1st millennium CE (Han dynasty).

##### (2）第一次大移民   (西晉永嘉之亂，4世紀）

**·** 西元311年「永嘉之亂」迫使中原士族   (「衣冠南渡」）大舉南遷福建。他們所操的「河洛語」   (上古漢語）與當地語言融合，在晉江流域形成泉州話雛形。

**·** In 311 CE, the “Disaster of Yongjia” forced Central Plains aristocrats to flee south to Fujian. Their “Heluo speech” (Old Chinese) merged with local languages, giving rise to the early Quanzhou dialect in the Jinjiang River basin.

##### (3）第二次大移民   (唐初與唐末，7–9世紀）

**·** 唐高宗時期   (7世紀）陳政、陳元光父子入閩平亂並設漳州，帶來中古漢語，奠定漳州話基礎；唐末   (9世紀）王潮、王審知兄弟再入閩，進一步強化中古漢語影響。

**·** In the 7th century (Tang Gaozong period), General Chen Zheng and his son Chen Yuanguang led troops into Fujian, established Zhangzhou, and introduced Middle Chinese – forming the basis of Zhangzhou dialect. In the 9th century, the Wang brothers (Wang Chao and Wang Shenzhi) entered Fujian again, reinforcing Middle Chinese influence.

##### (4）後續傳播   (明清時期）

**·** 明、清時期，漳州人與泉州人遷移臺灣及東南亞   (新加坡、馬來西亞等）。在臺灣，漳泉腔調融合，形成臺灣話   (臺語）。

**·** During the Ming and Qing dynasties, Zhangzhou and Quanzhou speakers migrated to Taiwan and Southeast Asia (e.g., Singapore, Malaysia). In Taiwan, the two accents blended to form Taiwanese Hokkien (Tâi‑gí).

### 二、古漢語、文言文與中古漢語的影響
### 2. Influences of Archaic Chinese, Classical Chinese, and Middle Chinese

閩南語因地處偏遠、交通不便，較少受到北方政權更迭與語言演變的衝擊，因而保留了大量的古漢語層次。

Because Fujian is mountainous and geographically isolated, Hokkien was less affected by political upheavals and sound changes in the north, thus preserving many archaic layers of Chinese.

---

#### (1）上古漢語   (魏晉之前）的影響 | Influence of Archaic Chinese (Old Chinese, pre‑Jin)

閩南語的白讀音   (口語音） 主要源於上古漢語。許多上古詞彙在現代華語中已消失，卻仍活躍於閩南語口語。

Hokkien’s colloquial readings (spoken pronunciations) derive mainly from Old Chinese. Many Old Chinese words that have disappeared or shifted in Mandarin remain current in Hokkien everyday speech.

| 閩南語白讀          | 中文意思 | 華語對應 | English meaning |
| -------------- | ---- | ---- | --------------- |
| tiáⁿ (鼎)       | 鍋子   | 鍋    | pot / wok       |
| tī (箸)         | 筷子   | 筷子   | chopsticks      |
| bīn (面)        | 臉    | 臉    | face            |
| ba̍k-chiu (目珠) | 眼睛   | 眼睛   | eye             |

---

#### (2）中古漢語   (隋唐時期）的影響 | Influence of Middle Chinese (Sui‑Tang period)

閩南語的文讀音   (讀書音） 主要源自隋唐時期的中古漢語，用於朗讀典籍、正式場合。

Hokkien’s literary readings (formal pronunciations) come primarily from Middle Chinese of the Sui‑Tang period, used in reading classical texts and formal settings.

· 聲調系統：閩南語完整保留中古漢語的八個聲調   (平上去入各分陰陽），華語僅剩四聲。

  Tonal system: Hokkien fully preserves the eight tones of Middle Chinese (four tones each split into yin/yang), whereas Mandarin has only four.

· 文白異讀：同一個漢字常有文、白兩種讀音。例如「共」：

  Literary‑colloquial dichotomy: The same character often has two readings. Example: 共

  · 文讀 / Literary: kiōng

  · 白讀 / Colloquial: kā

---

#### (3）文言文的影響 | Influence of Classical Chinese

文言文作為數千年中華書面語，對閩南語的正式書寫與語法有深遠影響：

· 語法與詞彙：正式表達中保留許多文言句式與古典詞彙。

· 韻律：閩南語豐富的聲調使其朗誦古詩文時，比華語更貼近原韻。

Classical Chinese, as the written standard for over two millennia, has profoundly shaped Hokkien’s formal and written registers:

· Grammar & vocabulary: Formal Hokkien retains many classical syntactic structures and lexicon.

· Prosody: Its rich tone system allows recitation of classical poetry with a rhythm much closer to the original than Mandarin can achieve.

### 四、最終結論 | Final Takeaway

閩南語絕非單純的「方言」，而是一座承載從上古到中古漢語演變軌跡的「活化石」。其文白異讀系統生動展現了漢語數千年的歷史層次。

Hokkien is far more than a mere “dialect” – it is a living fossil that preserves the phonological and lexical strata of Chinese from Old to Middle times. Its literary‑colloquial split offers a vivid window into the historical layering of the Chinese language over nearly two millennia.
