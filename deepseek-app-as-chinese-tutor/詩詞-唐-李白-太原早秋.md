**三、詩意詳細解析**

**（一）寫作背景**

〈太原早秋〉作於唐玄宗開元二十三年（735年）秋天。這年夏季，李白應友人元演之邀，同來太原（即並州，今山西太原），意欲「攀桂以求聞達」——即希望通過結交權貴或展示才學來謀求仕途發展。然而，他在三晉地區輾轉半年，始終未能得到實現抱負的機會。懷才不遇的失落與羈旅他鄉的孤獨交織，促使他在這年秋天寫下了這首懷鄉之作 [1]。

**（二）字詞釋義**

- **太原**：即並州，唐河東道太原府，今山西太原 [1]。
- **歲落**：光陰逝去，一年已過半。
- **眾芳歇**：群花凋謝。
- **大火**：星名，即二十八宿之一的心宿。每年夏曆五月黃昏出現於正南方，位置最高；六七月開始向西下行，故稱「流火」。《詩經·豳風·七月》：「七月流火」即指此星。
- **塞**：關塞，指長城一帶的邊塞。
- **雲色渡河秋**：雲彩飄過黃河，也呈現出秋色。
- **故國**：家鄉。當時李白家在湖北安陸。
- **汾水**：汾河，黃河第二大支流，流經山西中部、南部 [2]。
- **悠悠**：綿延不絕的樣子。

**（三）逐句解析**

- **首聯「歲落眾芳歇，時當大火流」**  
  詩人從時間入手，點出早秋的節令。「歲落」言光陰流逝，一年已過半；「眾芳歇」寫群花凋零，暗示秋季的到來。「大火流」化用《詩經》「七月流火」之典，表明時令已至夏曆七月之後，暑氣消退，秋意漸濃。首聯以自然景物含蓄點明季節，為全詩奠定基調。

- **頷聯「霜威出塞早，雲色渡河秋」**  
  此聯直接描寫太原早秋的氣候特徵。「霜威出塞早」寫塞外早降秋霜，突出北方邊地秋來之早；「雲色渡河秋」寫雲彩飄過黃河亦帶秋色。詩人巧妙地在兩句末尾鑲嵌了「早」、「秋」二字，既點題又形成對仗，是典型的鑲嵌修辭手法 [1]。

- **頸聯「夢繞邊城月，心飛故國樓」**  
  由寫景轉入抒情。詩人夜夜夢繞太原（邊城）的月亮，心卻早已飛回故鄉的樓閣。邊城月象徵羈旅之地，故國樓象徵家鄉與親人，兩者形成空間上的強烈對照，將思鄉之情具象化。

- **尾聯「思歸若汾水，無日不悠悠」**  
  詩人將思鄉之情比作汾水，綿延不絕，無日無之。汾水是流經太原的河流，以眼前之景喻心中之情，設喻新巧，真切自然 [2]。這份鄉愁沒有一天停止，正如汾水日夜流淌，永無止息。

**（四）整體意境與主題**

這首詩以早秋的自然景象為引，層層深入地抒發了詩人的羈旅之愁與懷鄉之情。全詩可分三個層次：

- **寫景（首聯、頷聯）**：通過「眾芳歇」、「大火流」、「霜威」、「雲色」等意象，勾勒出北方早秋的蕭瑟氣象，既寫實又點題。
- **抒情（頸聯）**：由景及情，「夢繞」與「心飛」形成虛實對照，表達身滯邊城、心繫故園的深切思念。
- **深化（尾聯）**：以汾水之悠悠比喻思歸之綿長，將抽象的鄉愁化為具體的意象，餘韻悠長。

主題上，這首詩既是對太原早秋景色的真實描寫，更是李白懷才不遇、壯志難酬時內心孤寂與鄉愁的深刻抒發。值得注意的是，詩中雖有濃厚的懷歸之意，但格調並不低沉，《唐宋詩醇》評其「健舉之至，行氣如虹」[1]，在羈旅愁思中仍保持著李白特有的雄健氣勢。

**（五）藝術特色**

- **鑲嵌點題**：頷聯句尾巧妙嵌入「早」、「秋」二字，與標題呼應，既突出主題又形成對仗 [1]。
- **設喻新巧**：以流經太原的汾水比喻思鄉之情，化無形為有形，真切動人 [2]。
- **結構嚴整**：從寫景到抒情，從外在節候到內在心緒，層層遞進，脈絡分明。
- **情景交融**：早秋的蕭瑟與詩人的孤寂、汾水的悠悠與鄉愁的綿長，自然景物與主觀情感融為一體。

**III. Detailed Analysis**

**(1) Historical Background**

"Taiyuan Early Autumn" was written in the autumn of 735 AD (the 23rd year of the Kaiyuan era of Emperor Xuanzong of Tang). That summer, Li Bai had come to Taiyuan (then known as Bingzhou, in present-day Shanxi Province) at the invitation of his friend Yuan Yan, hoping to advance his official career through connections and the display of his talents. However, after six months of wandering in the Sanjin region, he was unable to fulfill his ambitions. The disappointment of unfulfilled aspirations and the loneliness of being far from home intertwined, prompting him to write this homesick poem in the autumn [1].

**(2) Vocabulary Explanations**

- **太原 (Taiyuan)**: Then known as Bingzhou, the capital of Hedong Circuit in the Tang Dynasty; present-day Taiyuan, Shanxi [1].
- **歲落 (Suì Luò)**: The passing of time; the year is more than half over.
- **眾芳歇 (Zhòng Fāng Xiē)**: The many flowers have withered.
- **大火 (Dà Huǒ)**: The name of a star, one of the Twenty-Eight Mansions (the Heart Mansion). It appears in the southern sky at dusk in the fifth lunar month and begins to move westward in the sixth and seventh months, hence the term "flowing fire." The phrase comes from the "Book of Songs" (Shijing): "In the seventh month, the Fire Star flows".
- **塞 (Sài)**: The frontier passes, referring to the Great Wall region.
- **雲色渡河秋 (Yún Sè Dù Hé Qiū)**: The clouds drifting across the Yellow River also take on an autumnal hue.
- **故國 (Gù Guó)**: Hometown. At this time, Li Bai's family was in Anlu, Hubei.
- **汾水 (Fén Shuǐ)**: The Fen River, the second largest tributary of the Yellow River, flowing through central and southern Shanxi [2].
- **悠悠 (Yōu Yōu)**: Endless, continuous.

**(3) Line-by-Line Interpretation**

- **First Couplet: "As the year wanes, all fragrant flowers fade; / The season comes when the Fire Star flows."**  
  The poet begins by establishing the time and season. "The year wanes" speaks of time passing; "all fragrant flowers fade" suggests the arrival of autumn. The allusion to the "Fire Star flowing" from the "Book of Songs" indicates that the season has moved past the seventh lunar month, when the heat recedes and autumn begins to emerge. The opening couplet subtly establishes the season through natural imagery.

- **Second Couplet: "The frost's might comes early beyond the frontier; / The clouds' hue carries autumn across the river."**  
  This couplet directly describes the early autumn climate of Taiyuan. "The frost's might comes early beyond the frontier" highlights the early arrival of frost in the northern borderlands; "the clouds' hue carries autumn across the river" describes how even the clouds drifting across the Yellow River bear the colors of autumn. The poet cleverly embeds the characters for "early" (早) and "autumn" (秋) at the end of the two lines, both highlighting the theme and creating a parallel structure—a classic example of the rhetorical device of embedding [1].

- **Third Couplet: "In dreams I circle the frontier city's moon; / My heart flies to my homeland's tower."**  
  The poem shifts from scene to emotion. The poet dreams of the moon over the frontier city (Taiyuan), but his heart has already flown back to the towers of his hometown. The "frontier city's moon" symbolizes his place of sojourn, while the "homeland's tower" symbolizes home and family. The spatial contrast between the two powerfully expresses his longing for home.

- **Fourth Couplet: "My longing for home is like the Fen River: / Not a single day passes without its endless flow."**  
  The poet compares his homesickness to the Fen River, which flows endlessly, day after day. The Fen River, which flows through Taiyuan, serves as a vivid, local metaphor for his unceasing longing [2]. His homesickness never ceases, just as the Fen River flows without end.

**(4) Overall Mood and Themes**

The poem uses the scenery of early autumn as a starting point to progressively reveal the poet's feelings of homesickness and the melancholy of being far from home. It can be divided into three layers:

- **Scene-setting (first and second couplets)**: Through images like "flowers fade," "the Fire Star flows," "frost's might," and "clouds' hue," the poem depicts the bleak early autumn of the north, capturing both the scene and the theme.
- **Emotion (third couplet)**: Moving from scene to feeling, "dreams" and "heart" create a contrast between reality and longing, expressing the deep ache of being trapped in a frontier city while yearning for home.
- **Deepening (fourth couplet)**: The comparison of homesickness to the endless Fen River transforms an abstract emotion into a concrete image, leaving a lasting resonance.

Thematically, the poem is not only a realistic depiction of early autumn scenery in Taiyuan but also a profound expression of Li Bai's loneliness and homesickness in the face of unfulfilled ambitions. Notably, despite the strong sentiment of longing, the tone is not low or depressed. As the "Tang-Song Poetry Commentary" remarks, the poem is "vigorous to the extreme, its momentum like a rainbow" [1], maintaining Li Bai's characteristic heroic spirit even amid homesickness and disappointment.

**(5) Artistic Features**

- **Embedding to Highlight the Theme**: The poet cleverly embeds the characters "early" (早) and "autumn" (秋) at the end of the second couplet, echoing the title, emphasizing the theme, and creating a parallel structure [1].
- **Novel and Apt Metaphor**: The comparison of homesickness to the Fen River, which flows through Taiyuan, makes an abstract feeling concrete and vivid [2].
- **Strict Structure**: The poem moves from scene to emotion, from external season to internal feeling, in a clear and orderly progression.
- **Fusion of Scene and Emotion**: The bleakness of early autumn merges with the poet's loneliness; the endless flow of the Fen River merges with his unceasing homesickness—scenery and emotion become one.
