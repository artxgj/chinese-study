**三、詩意詳細解析**

**（一）寫作背景**

〈之廣陵宿常二南郭幽居〉是李白於唐玄宗天寶十三載（754年）創作的五言古詩 [1]。廣陵即今江蘇揚州，唐代隸屬淮南道 [2]。此時李白已年過半百，離開長安後漫遊四方，此詩記敘他遊歷廣陵期間投宿友人常二（排行第二）的城南居所的經歷。詩題中的「之」為「往、到」之意，「南郭」指城南郊外 [3]。

**（二）字詞釋義**

- **之**：往、到，前往之意。
- **廣陵**：郡名，即今江蘇揚州，唐代隸淮南道 [2]。
- **宿**：投宿、住宿。
- **常二**：友人，排行第二，名不詳。
- **南郭**：城南郊外。郭，外城。
- **幽居**：幽靜的居所。
- **柴門**：用樹枝編成的簡陋門，指隱居之處。
- **桃花源**：東晉陶淵明《桃花源記》中構想的理想世界，此處喻指友人居所之幽美。
- **忘憂**：忘卻憂愁。
- **假草**：借助花草。一說「假」為「借」之意，指借種植花草以忘憂。
- **羅**：羅列、分佈。
- **叢萱**：一叢叢的萱草。萱草又名「忘憂草」，古人認為食之可忘憂。
- **暝色**：暮色、天色昏暗。
- **軒**：窗戶或帶窗的長廊。
- **故人**：老朋友，指常二。
- **茅宇**：茅草屋。
- **楊園**：楊樹林中的園子，語出《詩經·小雅》。
- **還惜**：猶言「更珍惜」。
- **詩酒別**：以詩酒相送的道別。
- **江海言**：情深如江海的叮囑話語。
- **明朝**：明天早晨。
- **廣陵道**：通往廣陵的道路。
- **傾樽**：倒空酒杯，指飲盡杯中酒。

**（三）逐句解析**

- **「綠水接柴門，有如桃花源」**  
  詩人開篇描寫友人居所環境：清澈的綠水環繞著簡樸的柴門，景色之美如同陶淵明筆下的桃花源 [5]。以桃花源喻友人居所，既寫環境之幽美，更暗喻主人超然物外的高潔品格。

- **「忘憂或假草，滿院羅叢萱」**  
  滿院子羅列著一叢叢的萱草（忘憂草），主人或許是藉種植花草來忘卻世間的煩憂 [1]。此句既寫實景，又暗示主人淡泊名利、隱逸自適的人生態度。

- **「暝色湖上來，微雨飛南軒」**  
  暮色從湖面上瀰漫開來，細細的雨絲飄進南窗。此聯以細膩的筆觸描寫傍晚時分的景致變化，由遠而近，由室外而室內，營造出恬淡靜謐的氛圍。

- **「故人宿茅宇，夕鳥棲楊園」**  
  老朋友居住在茅草屋中，夕陽西下時鳥兒歸棲於楊樹林中。此聯以「故人」與「夕鳥」對舉，人與鳥各得其居，一片安詳和諧，也流露出詩人對友人隱居生活的欣賞。

- **「還惜詩酒別，深為江海言」**  
  更珍惜這以詩酒相伴的道別時刻，友人情深如江海般的叮囑話語，令人難忘 [3]。此句直接抒發詩人與友人離別時的深情厚誼。

- **「明朝廣陵道，獨憶此傾樽」**  
  明天一早就要踏上通往廣陵的道路了，到那時只能獨自回憶今夜把酒傾談的場景。末句以「明朝」與「此夜」對照，將離別的惆悵與對友情的珍重推向高潮。

**（四）整體意境與主題**

這首詩以友人幽居環境為線索，層層展開，情景交融 [1]。全詩可分三個層次 [4]：

- **寫景（前四句）**：從柴門外的綠水、滿院的萱草，到暮色湖光、微雨南窗，勾勒出一幅恬淡隱逸的田園畫卷。
- **敘事（中二句）**：由景及人，寫故人居於茅宇、夕鳥歸棲楊園，人與自然和諧共處。
- **抒情（末四句）**：轉入詩酒話別，以江海喻深情，以「獨憶」收束全詩，餘韻悠長。

詩中雖未直接言及世事，但以桃花源、忘憂草等意象暗喻友人超然物外的品格，同時也流露出李白對這種隱逸生活的嚮往與讚賞 [5]。末四句的離別之情真摯深沉，展現了李白「詩酒人生」的豪情與對友情的珍重 [6]。

**（五）藝術特色**

- **用典貼切**：以「桃花源」喻居所之幽，以「萱草忘憂」喻主人品格，用典自然而不露痕跡 [1]。
- **情景交融**：暮色、微雨、夕鳥等景物與離別之情融為一體，景語即情語 [3]。
- **結構嚴謹**：由遠及近、由景及人、由敘事到抒情，層層遞進，脈絡分明 [4]。
- **語言凝練**：全詩僅六十字，卻涵蓋環境描寫、人物刻畫與情感抒發，言簡意深。

**III. Detailed Analysis**

**(1) Historical Background**

"Zhi Guangling Su Chang Er Nanguo You Ju" is a five-character ancient-style poem written by Li Bai in 754 AD (the 13th year of the Tianbao era) [1]. Guangling is present-day Yangzhou in Jiangsu Province, which belonged to Huainan Circuit in the Tang Dynasty [2]. By this time, Li Bai was over fifty and had been wandering after leaving Chang'an. The poem records his experience of staying at the south-city dwelling of his friend Chang Er (the second son of the Chang family) during his travels in Guangling. The character "之" in the title means "to go to" or "arrive at," and "南郭" refers to the southern outskirts of the city [3].

**(2) Vocabulary Explanations**

- **之 (Zhī)**: To go to, to arrive at.
- **廣陵 (Guǎng Líng)**: The commandery name, present-day Yangzhou, Jiangsu, under Huainan Circuit in the Tang Dynasty [2].
- **宿 (Sù)**: To lodge, to stay overnight.
- **常二 (Cháng Èr)**: A friend, the second son of the Chang family; his full name is unknown.
- **南郭 (Nán Guō)**: The southern outskirts of the city. "Guo" refers to the outer city wall.
- **幽居 (Yōu Jū)**: A secluded, tranquil dwelling.
- **柴門 (Chái Mén)**: A simple gate made of brushwood, symbolizing a hermit's dwelling.
- **桃花源 (Táo Huā Yuán)**: The ideal world envisioned in Tao Yuanming's "Peach Blossom Spring," here used as a metaphor for the friend's beautiful and secluded residence [5].
- **忘憂 (Wàng Yōu)**: To forget one's worries.
- **假草 (Jiǎ Cǎo)**: To rely on plants/herbs. One interpretation is that "假" means "to borrow," implying the friend uses gardening to forget his cares.
- **羅 (Luó)**: To spread out, to arrange in rows.
- **叢萱 (Cóng Xuān)**: Clusters of daylilies (hemerocallis), also known as the "forget-worry plant," believed in ancient times to alleviate sorrow [1].
- **暝色 (Míng Sè)**: Dusk, the dimming light of evening.
- **軒 (Xuān)**: A window or a covered corridor with windows.
- **故人 (Gù Rén)**: An old friend, referring to Chang Er.
- **茅宇 (Máo Yǔ)**: A thatched hut.
- **楊園 (Yáng Yuán)**: A garden of poplar trees, alluding to a phrase from the "Book of Songs" (Shijing).
- **還惜 (Hái Xī)**: To cherish even more.
- **詩酒別 (Shī Jiǔ Bié)**: A farewell accompanied by poetry and wine.
- **江海言 (Jiāng Hǎi Yán)**: Words of parting as deep and vast as rivers and seas [3].
- **明朝 (Míng Zhāo)**: Tomorrow morning.
- **廣陵道 (Guǎng Líng Dào)**: The road leading to Guangling.
- **傾樽 (Qīng Zūn)**: To empty one's wine cup, meaning to drink all the wine.

**(3) Line-by-Line Interpretation**

- **"Green waters meet the brushwood gate, / Like the Peach Blossom Spring."**  
  The poem opens with a description of the friend's dwelling: clear green waters surround the simple brushwood gate, evoking the idyllic beauty of Tao Yuanming's Peach Blossom Spring [5]. This simile not only praises the natural scenery but also implies the host's transcendent, unworldly character.

- **"To forget care, perhaps he cultivates plants; / The courtyard is filled with clusters of daylilies."**  
  The courtyard is filled with daylilies (the "forget-worry" plant), suggesting that the host may be using gardening as a way to forget the world's troubles [1]. This line describes the scene while hinting at the host's reclusive, detached attitude toward life.

- **"Dusk settles over the lake, / A light rain drifts in through the southern window."**  
  The evening light spreads from the lake, and fine raindrops float in through the southern window. This couplet captures the subtle changes of dusk with delicate observation, moving from the distant lake to the nearby window, creating a tranquil and serene atmosphere.

- **"My old friend lodges in a thatched hut, / Evening birds roost in the poplar grove."**  
  The old friend dwells in a simple thatched hut, while birds return to roost in the poplar trees at sunset. The parallel between the "old friend" and the "evening birds" suggests a harmonious existence between humans and nature, expressing the poet's admiration for his friend's reclusive life.

- **"I cherish even more this parting with poetry and wine, / Your words run deep as rivers and seas."**  
  The poet directly expresses his deep affection for his friend: the farewell accompanied by poetry and wine is especially precious, and the friend's heartfelt words are as profound as the seas [3]. This line reveals the depth of their bond.

- **"Tomorrow morning on the Guangling road, / I'll remember this cup, alone."**  
  Tomorrow he will set out on the road to Guangling, and then he will have only memories of this night of shared wine and conversation. The contrast between "tomorrow morning" and "this night" heightens the poignancy of parting and the value of friendship.

**(4) Overall Mood and Themes**

The poem unfolds in three layers, blending scene, narrative, and emotion [1]:

- **Scene (first four lines)**: From the green water at the gate and the clusters of daylilies to the dusk light and light rain, the poem paints a tranquil, reclusive pastoral picture.
- **Narrative (middle two lines)**: The focus shifts from scene to people—the old friend in his thatched hut, the birds returning to the poplars—human and nature in harmony.
- **Emotion (last four lines)**: The poem turns to the farewell, using rivers and seas as a metaphor for deep feeling and closing with the solitary memory of the shared cup [4].

Although the poem does not directly address worldly affairs, the imagery of the Peach Blossom Spring and the "forget-worry" plant subtly praises the friend's unworldly character, while also revealing Li Bai's own admiration for such a reclusive life [5]. The final four lines express a sincere and profound feeling of parting, showcasing Li Bai's characteristic blend of wine, poetry, and deep friendship [6].

**(5) Artistic Features**

- **Appropriate Allusions**: The use of "Peach Blossom Spring" to evoke the secluded dwelling and "daylilies" to suggest the host's character is natural and unobtrusive [1].
- **Fusion of Scene and Emotion**: The dusk, light rain, and evening birds are seamlessly blended with the feelings of parting—scenery becomes emotion [3].
- **Structured Progression**: From distance to near, from scene to people, from narrative to lyricism, the poem proceeds in clear, orderly layers [4].
- **Concise Language**: In just sixty characters, the poem covers environmental description, character portrayal, and emotional expression, achieving depth with brevity.
