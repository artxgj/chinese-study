# 三星堆完整詳解 | Sanxingdui – Complete Detailed Guide

---

## 一、遺址概況 | I. Site Overview

#### 中文
三星堆遺址（Sanxingdui Site），原名興古遺址，位於中國四川省廣漢市三星堆鎮鴨子河南岸。遺址總面積約12平方公里，核心區域古蜀國都城面積約3.6平方公里，是中國西南地區迄今發現的分布範圍最廣、延續時間最長、文化內涵最豐富的古文化遺址。

三星堆遺址以夏商時期三星堆古城為主體，依托鴨子河，橫跨馬牧河，形成經東、西、南三面城牆及北側鴨子河的防禦體系。古城面積約370公頃，可分作祭祀區、居住區、作坊區、墓葬區，並有三星堆、月亮灣等重要夯土建築遺跡，體現出高度繁榮、布局嚴整的古代都邑氣象。

遺址年代範圍約為公元前2700年至公元前1000年，相當於新石器時代晚期至商周時期。三星堆文化是分布於中國西南四川盆地一帶、中心在成都平原、年代範圍大致在公元前1600至公元前1000年間的青銅文化，年代跨度大致相當於中原地區的商代。

#### English
The Sanxingdui Site, originally known as Xinggu Site, is located on the south bank of the Yazi River in Sanxingdui Town, Guanghan City, Sichuan Province, China. The site covers a total area of approximately 12 square kilometers, with the core area of the ancient Shu kingdom's capital spanning about 3.6 square kilometers. It is the most extensive, longest-lasting, and culturally richest ancient cultural site discovered to date in Southwest China.

Centered around the Sanxingdui ancient city from the Xia and Shang dynasties, the site relies on the Yazi River and spans the Muma River, forming a defense system with eastern, western, and southern city walls, with the Yazi River serving as the northern boundary. The ancient city covers about 370 hectares and can be divided into sacrificial, residential, workshop, and burial areas, featuring important rammed-earth architectural remains such as Sanxingdui and Yueliangwan, reflecting a highly prosperous and well-organized ancient capital.

The site dates from approximately 2700 BC to 1000 BC, spanning the late Neolithic period to the Shang and Zhou dynasties. Sanxingdui Culture is a Bronze Age culture distributed in the Sichuan Basin of Southwest China, centered on the Chengdu Plain, dating roughly from 1600 BC to 1000 BC, contemporaneous with the Shang dynasty in the Central Plains.

## 二、發現與發掘歷程 | II. Discovery and Excavation History

### (一) 1927年：首次發現 | 1927: First Discovery

#### 中文
三星堆遺址首次發現古代文化遺存的時間是1927年春天。廣漢當地農民燕道誠（一說燕青保）在月亮灣疏浚自家水渠時，掘出一坑玉石器，包括玉璧、玉環、玉刀以及石鑿、石斧、石珠等，出自一個長方形的坑洞中。這些遺物最初被當作無用之物送人，後經廣漢縣長陶宗伯、牧師董宜笃（V. H. Donnithorne）等人之手，最終轉交給華西協和大學博物館（今四川大學博物館前身）館長戴謙和（D. S. Dye）。今天看來，燕家在月亮灣所掘的，可能是三星堆的早期祭祀坑。

#### English
The first discovery of ancient cultural remains at the Sanxingdui Site occurred in the spring of 1927. A local farmer named Yan Daocheng (or Yan Qingbao) in Guanghan accidentally unearthed a pit of jade artifacts while dredging an irrigation ditch at Yueliangwan, including jade bi discs, jade rings, jade knives, stone chisels, stone axes, and stone beads, all from a rectangular pit. Initially regarded as worthless and given away, these artifacts eventually came into the hands of Guanghan County Magistrate Tao Zongbo, Pastor V. H. Donnithorne, and finally D. S. Dye, curator of the West China Union College Museum (predecessor of today's Sichuan University Museum). In retrospect, what the Yan family excavated at Yueliangwan was likely an early sacrificial pit of Sanxingdui.

### (二) 1934年：首次科學發掘 | 1934: First Scientific Excavation

#### 中文
1934年春，三星堆遺址啟動了歷史上的第一次正式發掘工作。由於當時法律規定外國人不得在中國境內採掘古物，發掘以廣漢縣政府和華西協和大學博物館合作的方式開展，由時任縣長羅雨蒼主辦，美國學者、華大博物館館長葛維漢（David C. Graham）指導。

發掘前後歷時約10天（1934年3月6日至14日），葛維漢詳細記錄了每日發掘進展，繪製了平面圖、地層剖面圖及器物線圖，並將出土器物按順序編號記錄位置。發掘結束後，出土遺物全部捐贈給華西協合大學博物館永久保存。這次發掘引起了遠在日本的郭沫若的關注，他在1934年7月9日致信林名均，對這一發現給予高度評價。

#### English
In the spring of 1934, the first formal excavation of the Sanxingdui Site commenced. Due to laws forbidding foreigners from excavating antiquities in China, the excavation was conducted as a collaboration between the Guanghan County Government and the West China Union College Museum, hosted by County Magistrate Luo Yucang and directed by American scholar David C. Graham, curator of the museum.

The excavation lasted about ten days (March 6–14, 1934). Graham meticulously recorded daily progress, created plan views, stratigraphic profiles, and artifact drawings, and numbered and documented the position of each artifact. All excavated artifacts were donated to the West China Union College Museum for permanent preservation. The excavation attracted the attention of Guo Moruo, then in Japan, who wrote to Lin Mingjun on July 9, 1934, highly praising the discovery.

### (三) 1980年代：大規模發掘與一、二號祭祀坑 | 1980s: Large-Scale Excavation and Pits No. 1 & 2

#### 中文
1980年至1981年，考古隊清理出新石器時代房屋遺址，出土數萬件標本，「三星堆文化」因此得名。1986年，三星堆遺址迎來了歷史上最大規模的一次發掘，發掘面積達1325平方米，共發現房子9座、灰坑101個，出土各種陶器（片）、玉石器十多萬件。

最重要的是，1986年夏天相繼發現了一號和二號兩個大型祭祀坑，出土了超過900件青銅、金、玉、象牙等器物。一號祭祀坑出土器物420件，包括玉器129件、石器70件、陶器39件、青銅器178件、金器4件，另有象牙門齒13枚、海貝62枚。這次發現震驚了世界，被譽為「20世紀最偉大的考古發現」和「世界第九大奇蹟」。

1988年，三星堆遺址被國務院列為全國重點文物保護單位。

#### English
From 1980 to 1981, archaeologists uncovered Neolithic house sites and excavated tens of thousands of specimens, giving rise to the name "Sanxingdui Culture". In 1986, Sanxingdui experienced its largest-scale excavation to date, covering 1,325 square meters, discovering 9 houses, 101 ash pits, and over 100,000 pottery and jade artifacts.

Most importantly, in the summer of 1986, two large-scale sacrificial pits—Pit No. 1 and Pit No. 2—were discovered successively, containing over 900 bronze, gold, jade, and ivory artifacts. Pit No. 1 yielded 420 artifacts, including 129 jade items, 70 stone items, 39 pottery items, 178 bronze items, and 4 gold items, along with 13 elephant tusks and 62 cowrie shells. This discovery stunned the world and was hailed as "the greatest archaeological discovery of the 20th century" and "the ninth wonder of the world".

In 1988, the Sanxingdui Site was designated a National Key Cultural Relics Protection Unit by the State Council.

### (四）2019年至今：新一輪發掘（三至八號坑)  | 2019–Present: New Round of Excavation (Pits No. 3–8)

#### 中文
2019年10月至2020年8月，四川省文物考古研究院在三星堆遺址區域發現了六個新的祭祀坑（三號至八號坑）。2020年3月，在國家文物局的支持下，新一輪發掘正式啟動，採用多學科交叉的研究方法和先進的科技分析技術。截至2026年，三星堆遺址已出土一萬多件精美文物。

#### English
From October 2019 to August 2020, the Sichuan Provincial Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology discovered six new sacrificial pits (Pits No. 3 to 8) in the Sanxingdui site area. In March 2020, with the support of the National Administration of Cultural Heritage, a new round of excavation was launched, employing multidisciplinary research methods and advanced analytical techniques. As of 2026, over 10,000 exquisite artifacts have been unearthed from the Sanxingdui Site.

## 三、代表性文物 | III. Representative Artifacts

### (一) 青銅大立人像 | Bronze Standing Figure

#### 中文
青銅大立人像是三星堆最具代表性的文物之一，出土於二號祭祀坑。人像通高約260.8厘米（近2.61米），重180公斤，是世界上現存最高、最完整的青銅站立人像。人像身穿華麗長袍，赤足站立於大象頭形狀的底座之上。頭部後方有兩個插槽，表明曾佩戴髮髻，暗示其為統治階層成員。

#### English
The Bronze Standing Figure is one of the most iconic artifacts of Sanxingdui, unearthed from Pit No. 2. The figure stands approximately 260.8 cm (nearly 2.61 meters) tall and weighs 180 kilograms, making it the tallest and most complete standing bronze figure in existence. Gorgeously dressed, the elongated figure stands barefoot on a pedestal in the shape of an elephant head. Two slots at the back of the head indicate it once wore a topknot, suggesting the figure was part of the elite class.

### (二) 青銅神樹 | Bronze Sacred Tree

#### 中文
一號大型銅神樹出土於二號祭祀坑，修復後通高396厘米（約3.96米），由底座、樹和龍三部分組成，採用分段鑄造法製造，使用了套鑄、鉚鑄、嵌鑄等工藝。這棵青銅神樹被認為是連接天地的媒介，是古人與神靈溝通的橋樑。它是全世界迄今發現的最大的單件青銅文物。三星堆共出土八棵青銅神樹。

#### English
The No. 1 Large Bronze Sacred Tree was unearthed from Pit No. 2. After restoration, it stands 396 cm (approximately 3.96 meters) tall, composed of three parts: base, tree, and dragon. It was manufactured using the piece-mold casting method, employing techniques such as sleeve casting, rivet casting, and inlay casting. This bronze sacred tree is believed to have served as a medium connecting heaven and earth, a bridge between humans and deities. It is the largest single-piece bronze artifact ever discovered in the world. A total of eight bronze sacred trees have been unearthed at Sanxingdui.

### (三) 黃金權杖 | Golden Rod / Gold Scepter

#### 中文
黃金權杖出土於一號祭祀坑，是三星堆金器中最具代表性的文物之一。金杖的發現打破了中國傳統「九鼎至尊」的獨家格局，證明古蜀文明擁有獨特的權力象徵體系。

#### English
The Golden Rod (Gold Scepter) was unearthed from Pit No. 1 and is one of the most representative gold artifacts of Sanxingdui. The discovery of the gold scepter broke the traditional Chinese pattern of "supremacy of the Nine Tripods," proving that the ancient Shu civilization possessed its own unique system of power symbols.

### (四) 青銅面具 | Bronze Masks

#### 中文
三星堆出土了大量青銅面具和青銅人頭像，造型奇特、風格獨特。二號祭祀坑出土的青銅器中包含多件青銅人頭像和人面具。這些面具通常具有誇張的眼部造型——突出的柱狀眼睛，是三星堆藝術最鮮明的特徵之一。

#### English
Sanxingdui has yielded a large number of bronze masks and bronze heads with bizarre shapes and unique styles. The bronze artifacts from Pit No. 2 include multiple bronze heads and human masks. These masks typically feature exaggerated eye shapes—protruding columnar eyes—which are among the most distinctive characteristics of Sanxingdui art.

### (五) 彩繪青銅器 | Painted Bronze Artifacts

#### 中文
最新的考古研究發現，三星堆青銅器原本並非今日所見的銅綠色，而是表面裝飾有絢麗的色彩。研究發現的顏色包括黑色和紅色：黑色常見於青銅人像和面具的眉毛、眼睛和頭髮；紅色則常見於青銅神樹的花瓣和青銅器的紋飾中。這一發現將中國彩繪青銅器的出現時間向前推進了近一千年。

#### English
Recent archaeological research has revealed that Sanxingdui bronze artifacts were not originally the greenish hue seen today, but were instead adorned with brilliant colors on their surfaces. The colors discovered so far include black and red: black is commonly found in the eyebrows, eyes, and hair of bronze figures and masks, while red often appears on the petals of bronze sacred trees and in the decorative patterns of bronze vessels. This discovery has pushed back the timeline for the emergence of painted bronze artifacts in China by nearly a thousand years.

四、文化意義與價值 | IV. Cultural Significance and Value

### (一) 證實古蜀國的存在 | Proving the Existence of the Ancient Shu Kingdom

#### 中文
三星堆出土的大量實物證明了古蜀國的存在。通過發掘找到了從新石器時代到春秋時代的連續地層關係，可證明在中國的西南確有一個強大的「蜀王國」與中原的「夏、商、周」同時並存，而不是虛擬的神話傳說。初步證實三星堆遺址可能就是古蜀王國的「都邑」所在地。

#### English
The large number of artifacts unearthed at Sanxingdui proves the existence of the ancient Shu Kingdom. Through excavation, continuous stratigraphic relationships from the Neolithic period to the Spring and Autumn period have been found, demonstrating that a powerful "Shu Kingdom" indeed coexisted with the Central Plains' Xia, Shang, and Zhou dynasties in Southwest China, rather than being merely mythical legend. It has been preliminarily confirmed that the Sanxingdui Site was likely the capital of the ancient Shu Kingdom.

### (二) 中華文明「多元一體」的見證 | Testament to the "Pluralistic and Integrated" Chinese Civilization

#### 中文
三星堆文化是中華文明的重要組成部分。考古研究表明，三星堆文化獨具特色、自成體系，是中華文明多元一體格局中的重要組成部分。三星堆文化具有強烈的開放性和包容性。以三星堆為代表的古蜀文明不斷與外界碰撞、交流、融合與學習，成為多元、包容且綿延不絕的中華文明的重要歷史見證。

正如中國社會科學院學部委員王巍所說：「即使在中原王朝興盛的時期，各區域都有自己非常有特色的區域文明，既接受中原的影響，同時又有很多自身的發展和創造，這樣才構成了我們豐富多彩的中華文明。」

#### English
Sanxingdui Culture is an important component of Chinese civilization. Archaeological research shows that Sanxingdui Culture is unique and自成體系 (self-contained), constituting a significant part of the pluralistic and integrated pattern of Chinese civilization. Sanxingdui Culture exhibits strong openness and inclusiveness. The ancient Shu civilization, represented by Sanxingdui, continuously collided, exchanged, integrated, and learned from the outside world, becoming an important historical witness to the pluralistic, inclusive, and continuous Chinese civilization.

As Wang Wei, a member of the Academic Division of the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, stated: "Even during the flourishing period of the Central Plains dynasties, each region had its own distinctive regional civilization, both accepting influences from the Central Plains and possessing its own developments and creations. This is precisely what constitutes our rich and colorful Chinese civilization."

### (三) 學術研究價值 | Academic Research Value

#### 中文
三星堆遺址的存在，對探索人類早期的政治組織及社會形態的演化，對研究早期國家進程、宗教意識的發展均具有重要的價值，在人類文明發展史上占有重要地位。三星堆遺址出土青銅器產地研究、文化性質研究等持續推動著學術界對古蜀文明和中國早期文明的認識。

#### English
The Sanxingdui Site holds significant value for exploring early human political organization and social evolution, studying early state formation processes, and understanding the development of religious consciousness, occupying an important position in the history of human civilization. Research on the provenance of Sanxingdui bronzes and the cultural nature of the site continues to advance academic understanding of the ancient Shu civilization and early Chinese civilization.

## 五、最新研究成果 | V. Latest Research Findings

#### 中文
2026年7月10日，四川省文物考古研究院公布三星堆青銅器最新研究成果。專家通過對青銅頂尊跪坐人像的深入研究發現，這件器物並非抽象的神話符號，而是真實祭祀儀典的生動再現，反映出數千年前古蜀先民對神靈的崇拜。

研究團隊通過金屬成分分析及文化源流考證，發現青銅人像和其頂著的青銅尊是由不同的工匠、在不同時間、不同地點分別製作好後，再在三星堆本地拼接組合而成。這也印證了三星堆青銅器有外來鑄造的可能性。

2026年5月10日，四川大學考古文博學院與四川省文物考古研究院在國際學術期刊《亞洲考古研究》上發表研究成果，確認三星堆遺址7號祭祀坑出土的鐵質殘片為純隕鐵製品。

#### English
On July 10, 2026, the Sichuan Provincial Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology announced the latest research findings on Sanxingdui bronzes. Through in-depth research on the bronze kneeling figure with a zun vessel on its head, experts discovered that this artifact is not an abstract mythological symbol but a vivid re-creation of an actual sacrificial ceremony, reflecting the ancient Shu people's reverence for deities thousands of years ago.

Through metal composition analysis and cultural provenance research, the team found that the bronze figure and the bronze zun it bears were separately manufactured by different craftsmen at different times and places, and then assembled together at Sanxingdui. This also confirms the possibility that some Sanxingdui bronzes were cast elsewhere.

On May 10, 2026, the School of Archaeology and Museology at Sichuan University and the Sichuan Provincial Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology published research findings in the international academic journal Asian Archaeology, confirming that the iron fragment unearthed from Pit No. 7 at Sanxingdui is a pure meteoritic iron artifact.

## 六、三星堆博物館 | VI. Sanxingdui Museum

#### 中文
三星堆博物館位於四川省廣漢市三星堆遺址東北角，靠近鴨子河。博物館於1992年8月奠基，1997年10月正式對外開放。展覽面積約4000平方米。博物館收藏並展示了在三星堆遺址出土的上千件珍貴文物，包括青銅器、玉器、金器和陶器等。2020年，三星堆博物館完成了最大規模的升級改造。

#### English
The Sanxingdui Museum is located at the northeast corner of the Sanxingdui Site in Guanghan, Sichuan Province, near the Yazi River. The museum's foundation was laid in August 1992, and it was formally opened to the public in October 1997. The exhibition area covers approximately 4,000 square meters. The museum holds and displays over a thousand precious cultural relics unearthed at the Sanxingdui Site, including bronze, jade, gold, and ceramic artifacts. In 2020, the Sanxingdui Museum completed its largest-scale upgrade.

## 總結 | Summary

#### 中文
三星堆是20世紀最偉大的考古發現之一，距今約3000至5000年。它位於四川廣漢，是一個新石器時代至商周時期的古蜀文化遺址。三星堆出土的青銅大立人、青銅神樹、黃金權杖、青銅面具等文物，造型奇特、工藝精湛，揭示了古蜀文明獨特的宗教信仰、社會結構和藝術成就。三星堆不僅證實了古蜀國的存在，更以其鮮明的地域特色和開放包容的文化特質，成為中華文明「多元一體」格局的典型見證。

#### English
Sanxingdui is one of the greatest archaeological discoveries of the 20th century, dating back approximately 3,000 to 5,000 years. Located in Guanghan, Sichuan, it is a Neolithic to Shang-Zhou period ancient Shu cultural site. The bronze standing figure, bronze sacred tree, golden scepter, bronze masks, and other artifacts unearthed at Sanxingdui feature unique shapes and exquisite craftsmanship, revealing the ancient Shu civilization's distinctive religious beliefs, social structure, and artistic achievements. Sanxingdui not only proves the existence of the ancient Shu Kingdom but also, with its distinctive regional characteristics and open, inclusive cultural qualities, stands as a typical testament to the "pluralistic and integrated" pattern of Chinese civilization.
