#### 中文解釋

「五代十國」是對中國歷史上**907年唐朝滅亡至960年北宋建立**之間動盪時期的統稱。這個名稱源於當時中原地區與南方割據政權的政治格局：

1. **五代（Five Dynasties）**：指先後控制中原地區（黃河流域）的五個短命王朝，依序為**後梁、後唐、後晉、後漢、後周**。史家以這五個政權為「正統」主線，因它們地處核心、自認接續唐朝國祚，且更迭頻繁（「代」有更替之意）。
2. **十國（Ten Kingdoms）**：指同期並存或先後存在於南方（及山西）的十個主要割據政權，分別為**前蜀、後蜀、南吳、南唐、吳越、閩、楚、南漢、荊南（南平）、北漢**。它們多為唐朝節度使割據而成，地處偏遠，稱「國」以示地方政權。

此稱謂首見於宋代歐陽修編撰的《新五代史》，史家為區分「中央正朔」與「地方藩鎮」，將中原五朝統稱「五代」，其餘較大的割據勢力合稱「十國」，合併後便成為這個時代的標準名稱。需注意「十國」是約數，實際割據政權不止十個，但史書慣以此概括。

#### English Explanation

The term **"Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms"** refers to the tumultuous period in Chinese history from the fall of the Tang Dynasty in **907 AD** to the establishment of the Northern Song Dynasty in **960 AD**. The name derives from the political landscape of that era:

1. **The Five Dynasties** (五代) : These were five short-lived regimes that successively controlled the Central Plains (Yellow River valley) in northern China. In chronological order, they are the Later Liang, Later Tang, Later Jin, Later Han, and Later Zhou. Historians considered these five the "orthodox" line of succession, as they occupied the political core, claimed to inherit the Tang mandate, and frequently replaced one another (the character dài implies succession).
2. **The Ten Kingdoms** (十國) : These were ten major regional regimes that existed contemporaneously or successively, mostly in southern China (plus Northern Han in Shanxi). They include the Former Shu, Later Shu, Southern Wu, Southern Tang, Wuyue, Min, Chu, Southern Han, Jingnan (Nanping), and Northern Han. Most were founded by former Tang military governors (jiedushi) and were referred to as "kingdoms" to denote their regional autonomy.

This nomenclature was formalized by the Song Dynasty historian Ouyang Xiu in his New History of the Five Dynasties. To distinguish the "central legitimate rule" from "local vassal states," historians grouped the northern Central Plains dynasties as the "Five Dynasties" and the other major separatist regimes as the "Ten Kingdoms." The number "ten" is a conventional round figure—there were actually more than ten autonomous states, but historiography traditionally uses this number as a convenient summary.
