## 2. Bilingual Response

### 中文部分 (Chinese Section)

**摘要**  
不，“詞際”不适合装这两个语法格式，因为它专指“同一个词跨学科的语义对比”。而“在…上/中”是**句法结构/固定搭配**问题，属于语法和语用层面。针对它，我推荐“位式”等两字名称；针对更广的中文学习分类（装词汇、句型等），我推荐“語構”等名称。下面分两层排序推荐。

**详细说明**

**第一层：专门针对「在…上」和「在…中」的两字分类名（按贴合度从高到低排序）**

- **第1名：位式 (wèi shì)** —— “位置 + 格式”。精准点出这两个结构都用于标记“空间或抽象方位”，且强调它们是固定“格式”。最直观、最贴切。  
- **第2名：方置 (fāng zhì)** —— “方位 + 置放”。强调“把某事物放置在某个维度上/里”，也暗含了“上/中”的空间隐喻。略微比“位式”抽象一点，但仍非常准确。  
- **第3名：介位 (jiè wèi)** —— “介词 + 位置”。语言学上更专业，因为“在”是介词，后面接位置词。好处是术语感强，但缺点是对普通学习者稍显晦涩。  
- **第4名：处格 (chù gé)** —— “处所 + 格”。借用了语法学“格”的概念（方位格）。比较学术，但不如前几个直观。  

**补充说明**：“詞際”为什么不行？因为“際”强调“不同领域之间的穿梭对比”。而“在…上/中”并不涉及“同一个词在不同学科中的含义变化”，它讲的是**同一结构在不同语境下的方位投射**，本质上是语法问题，不是词汇的跨域语义问题。

---

**第二层：更宽泛的中文学习分类名（可同时收纳词汇、句型、固定格式等）**

如果你的目标是建立一个总文件夹或总标签，把“词汇跨域对比”、“介词方位结构”、“其他句型”、“日常表达疑问”都放进去，我推荐以下两字名称（按通用性和扩展性从高到低排序）：

- **第1名：語構 (yǔ gòu)** —— “语言结构”。这是最稳、最全的选项。它天然覆盖了词汇搭配（词汇也是一种构式）和句法格式（固定句型）。学术上接近“构式语法”的“构”，但日常用也毫无违和感。  
- **第2名：格式 (gé shì)** —— “格式/范式”。强调“固定的表达框架”。所有“在…上/中”、“把字句”、“被字句”都是“格式”，词汇辨析也可以视为“词汇格式”的对比。偏重“模板”感。  
- **第3名：文用 (wén yòng)** —— “语言运用/用法”。非常接地气，直白说明这是“中文怎么用”的合集。但略泛，缺乏结构感。  
- **第4名：詞句 (cí jù)** —— “词汇与句子”。简单拆分两大块，但把“语法规则”单独挤掉了，容易让人忽略“结构规律”。  

**我的最终建议**：  
- 如果你只开一个总文件夹 → 用 **語構**，包容性最强。  
- 如果想突出“固定套路” → 用 **格式**。  
- 至于“詞際”，建议保留为 **語構 下的一个子标签**，专门放“跨学科同词对比”类内容（如 modality、loss function 那类）。

### English Section

**Abstract**  
No, “詞際” does not fit these two grammar patterns, because it specifically means “cross‑disciplinary semantic contrasts of the same word.” The patterns「在…上/中」are **syntactic structures / fixed collocations** – a grammar and usage issue. For them, I recommend “位式” (positional pattern) and others; for broader categories (covering vocabulary, patterns, etc.), I recommend “語構” (linguistic structure) and others. Rankings are provided below in two tiers.

**Detailed Explanation**

**Tier 1 – Two‑character names specifically for「在…上」and「在…中」(ranked best to good)**

- **#1: 位式 (wèi shì)** – “Position + pattern.” Precisely captures that both structures mark spatial or abstract “locations,” and that they are fixed “formats.” Most intuitive and accurate.  
- **#2: 方置 (fāng zhì)** – “Orientation + placement.” Emphasizes “placing something onto/into a certain dimension,” capturing the spatial metaphor of 上/中. Slightly more abstract than #1, but still very clear.  
- **#3: 介位 (jiè wèi)** – “Preposition + position.” More linguistically technical – since 在 is a preposition followed by a location word. It sounds academic, but may feel obscure to casual learners.  
- **#4: 处格 (chù gé)** – “Location + case.” Borrows the grammatical concept of “case” (locative case). Quite scholarly, but less intuitive than the top choices.  

**Why 詞際 doesn't work**: “際” emphasizes “traversing and contrasting across different fields/domains.” The patterns「在…上/中」do not involve “the same word changing meaning across disciplines” – they are about **spatial/metaphorical projection of a single syntactic structure** across different contexts. That is a grammar problem, not a cross‑disciplinary lexical problem.

---

**Tier 2 – Broader category names for overall Chinese study (covering vocabulary, sentence patterns, fixed structures, etc.)**

If you want one master folder or tag that holds “cross‑field vocabulary,” “preposition‑location structures,” “other patterns,” and “daily usage questions,” here are my two‑character recommendations (ranked by versatility and extensibility):

- **#1: 語構 (yǔ gòu)** – “Linguistic structure.” The safest, most comprehensive option. Naturally covers lexical collocations (words are also constructions) and syntactic formats (fixed sentence frames). It aligns with “construction grammar” but works perfectly for everyday study.  
- **#2: 格式 (gé shì)** – “Format / paradigm.” Emphasizes “fixed expressive templates.” All patterns like「在…上/中」, the 把‑construction, and the 被‑construction fit under “格式” – even vocabulary comparisons can be seen as “lexical format” contrasts. Leans toward a “template” feel.  
- **#3: 文用 (wén yòng)** – “Language usage / application.” Very down‑to‑earth – it plainly says “this is a collection of how Chinese is used.” However, it is a bit vague and lacks a structural flavor.  
- **#4: 詞句 (cí jù)** – “Words and sentences.” Simply splits things into two big boxes, but it tends to squeeze out “grammatical rules,” making it easy to overlook structural regularities.

**My final practical advice**:  
- If you are opening one master folder → choose **語構** – it is the most inclusive.  
- If you want to highlight “fixed formulas” → choose **格式**.  
- As for **詞際**, keep it as a **sub‑tag under 語構**, dedicated exclusively to “cross‑disciplinary same‑word comparisons” (like modality, loss function, etc.).
