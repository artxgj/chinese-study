### 屈原与粽子的故事 — The Story of Qu Yuan and Zongzi

#### 一、屈原其人 — Who Was Qu Yuan

屈原（约公元前340年—公元前278年），名平，字原，是中国战国时期楚国的贵族、政治家，也是中国最伟大的浪漫主义诗人之一。他出身楚国贵族，曾担任左徒、三闾大夫等要职，主张对内举贤授能、修明法度，对外联齐抗秦。

Qu Yuan (c. 340–278 BCE), named Ping with the courtesy name Yuan, was an aristocrat, statesman of the State of Chu during China's Warring States period, and one of the greatest romantic poets in Chinese history. Born into the Chu nobility, he served as a high-ranking minister, advocating for the promotion of capable officials, legal reform, and an alliance with the State of Qi to resist the powerful State of Qin.

然而，屈原的政治主张遭到以靳尚、子兰等贵族为首的强烈反对。他在谗言中逐渐被楚怀王疏远，先后被流放到汉北和江南地区。在流放期间，他依然心系祖国和人民，写下了《离骚》《天问》《九歌》等不朽诗篇，开创了“楚辞”这一诗歌体裁。

However, Qu Yuan's political ideals faced fierce opposition from aristocrats led by Jin Shang and Zi Lan. He was gradually alienated by King Huai of Chu due to slander and was exiled to the Hanbei and Jiangnan regions. During his exile, he remained deeply concerned about his country and people, composing immortal masterpieces such as Li Sao (The Lament), Tian Wen (Heavenly Questions), and Jiu Ge (Nine Songs), establishing the "Chu Ci" (Songs of Chu) poetic tradition.

公元前278年，秦国大将白起攻破楚国都城郢都。屈原得知国都沦陷后，悲愤绝望，在农历五月初五这一天，怀抱大石投汨罗江自尽，以身殉国。

In 278 BCE, the Qin general Bai Qi captured the Chu capital, Yingdu. Upon hearing the news of his homeland's fall, Qu Yuan, overcome with grief and despair, clasped a large stone to his chest and plunged into the Miluo River on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month, giving his life for his country.

#### 二、百姓的哀悼 — The People’s Mourning

屈原投江的消息传开后，楚国百姓悲痛万分。渔民们纷纷划船沿江寻找他的尸体，从汨罗江一直寻到洞庭湖，却始终未能找到。

When news of Qu Yuan's death spread, the people of Chu were overwhelmed with grief. Fishermen rushed out in their boats, searching up and down the river for his body—from the Miluo River all the way to Dongting Lake—but to no avail.

百姓们担心江中的鱼虾会啃食屈原的身体，便纷纷将米饭、鸡蛋等食物投入江中，希望以此喂饱鱼虾，保护屈原的遗体不受侵害。一位老郎中还倒入一壶雄黄酒，希望能将水中的蛟龙水兽药昏。

The local people, fearing that fish and shrimp would devour Qu Yuan's body, threw rice, eggs, and other food into the river, hoping to feed the fish and protect his remains. An old doctor even poured a jug of realgar wine into the water, hoping to intoxicate the river dragons and beasts.

这些最初出于保护屈原遗体的举动，逐渐演变成了延续至今的传统习俗——赛龙舟源于人们划船搜寻屈原的活动，而吃粽子则源于投入江中的米团。

These acts of protection gradually evolved into traditions that continue to this day—dragon boat racing originated from the people's search for Qu Yuan, while eating zongzi came from the rice dumplings thrown into the river.

#### 三、粽子的由来 — The Origin of Zongzi

关于粽子与屈原的联系，最早的文字记载见于南朝梁代吴均所著的志怪小说集《续齐谐记》。书中记载：

The earliest written record linking zongzi to Qu Yuan appears in Xu Qixie Ji (Continuation of the Records of the Extraordinary), a collection of supernatural tales by Wu Jun of the Southern Liang dynasty. It records:

“屈原五月五日投汨罗水，楚人哀之，至此日，以竹筒子贮米，投水以祭之。”

"Qu Yuan threw himself into the Miluo River on the fifth day of the fifth month. The people of Chu mourned for him, and from then on, on this day, they stored rice in bamboo tubes and threw them into the water as an offering to him."

这就是中国最早的粽子形式——“筒粽” （用竹筒装米的粽子）。

This was the earliest form of zongzi in China—"tong zong" (rice stuffed in bamboo tubes).

《续齐谐记》中还记载了另一个有趣的传说：

Xu Qixie Ji also contains another interesting legend:

东汉建武年间（公元22-55年），长沙人区曲（一说欧回）在江边遇到一位自称是三闾大夫（屈原官名）的人。此人告诉区曲：“你每年祭奠我，我很感激。但投放的祭品常被蛟龙偷吃。今后若再祭奠，请用楝叶包裹糯米，再用五彩丝线缠绕——这两样东西是蛟龙所惧怕的。”

During the Jianwu period of the Eastern Han dynasty (22–55 CE), a man named Ou Hui (or Qu Qu) from Changsha encountered a figure by the river who claimed to be the Three-Venerable Grand Master—Qu Yuan's official title. The figure told him: "I appreciate your offerings, but they are often stolen by dragons. In the future, wrap the rice in chinaberry leaves and tie it with five-colored silk threads—these two things are what dragons fear."

区曲按照指示照办，从此人们便用植物叶子包裹糯米并以彩线捆扎，这就是今天我们所熟悉的粽子的雏形。后来，楝叶逐渐被各地替换为艾叶、芦叶、箬叶等，彩线也被棕线、马莲草等替代。

Ou Hui followed these instructions, and from then on, people wrapped glutinous rice in plant leaves and tied them with colored threads—this became the prototype of the zongzi we know today. Over time, chinaberry leaves were replaced locally with mugwort leaves, reed leaves, bamboo leaves, and others, while colored threads were substituted with palm fiber, marsh grass, and the like.

#### 四、历史的另一面 — The Historical Perspective

值得注意的是，现代学者考证后发现，端午节的起源其实远比纪念屈原更加古老。

It is worth noting that modern scholars have discovered that the origins of the Dragon Boat Festival are actually much more ancient than the commemoration of Qu Yuan.

著名学者闻一多在《端午考》等文章中论证，端午节吃粽子、赛龙舟等习俗，很可能源自古代长江中下游百越民族的龙图腾崇拜。百越民族以龙为图腾，每年五月五日举行龙舟竞渡，将粽子投入江河祭祀龙神。粽子作为一种食品，早在春秋时期就已存在，当时北方称“角黍”（用菰叶包黍米成牛角状），南方称“筒糉”（用竹筒装米）。

The renowned scholar Wen Yiduo argued in works such as A Study of the Dragon Boat Festival that customs like eating zongzi and dragon boat racing likely originated from the dragon totem worship of the Baiyue peoples in the ancient Yangtze River basin. The Baiyue peoples revered the dragon as their totem, held dragon boat races on the fifth day of the fifth month, and threw zongzi into rivers as offerings to the dragon god. As a food, zongzi existed as early as the Spring and Autumn period—called "jiao shu" in the north (millet wrapped in wild rice leaves in the shape of an ox horn) and "tong zong" in the south (rice in bamboo tubes).

也就是说，是先有端午节的习俗和粽子这种食品，后人在漫长的历史中才将屈原的故事“附会”到这个节日之上。然而，正因为屈原的忠贞爱国与悲剧命运深深打动了中国人的心，这位伟大诗人的故事最终成为端午节最核心的文化内涵。

In other words, the customs of the Dragon Boat Festival and the food zongzi existed first; only later, over the long course of history, did people associate Qu Yuan's story with this festival. However, precisely because Qu Yuan's loyalty, patriotism, and tragic fate deeply moved the Chinese heart, the story of this great poet ultimately became the festival's most cherished cultural core.

#### 五、粽子的演变 — The Evolution of Zongzi

粽子历经两千多年的发展，品种日益丰富：

Over more than two thousand years of development, zongzi have become increasingly diverse:

- 晋代：粽子被正式定为端午节食品，开始添加中药“益智仁”，称为“益智粽”
- 南北朝：出现“杂粽”，米中掺杂禽兽肉、板栗、红枣、赤豆等
- 唐代：粽米“白莹如玉”，出现锥形、菱形等形状
- 宋代：出现“蜜饯粽”，果品入粽
- 元明时期：包裹料从菰叶改为箬叶、芦苇叶，馅料更加丰富
- Jin Dynasty: Zongzi were officially designated as the Dragon Boat Festival food, with the addition of the medicinal herb "yizhiren"
- Northern and Southern Dynasties: "Mixed zongzi" appeared, with meat, chestnuts, red dates, and red beans added to the rice
- Tang Dynasty: The rice became "white as jade," with cone and diamond shapes emerging
- Song Dynasty: "Candied zongzi" appeared, with fruits added
- Yuan and Ming Dynasties: Wrapping materials shifted from wild rice leaves to bamboo and reed leaves, with even more varied fillings

今天，粽子已发展出甜、咸两大流派，北方以甜粽为主（豆沙、红枣等），南方以咸粽为主（猪肉、咸蛋黄、香菇等）。粽子的形状也五花八门，有三角形、四角形、锥形、长条形等。

Today, zongzi have developed into two major schools—sweet and savory. The north favors sweet zongzi (with red bean paste, dates, etc.), while the south prefers savory ones (with pork, salted duck egg yolks, mushrooms, etc.). The shapes are also diverse—triangular, four-cornered, conical, cylindrical, and more.

每到农历五月初五端午节，家家户户包粽子、吃粽子，赛龙舟，挂艾草，饮雄黄酒——这些延续了两千多年的习俗，既是对伟大诗人屈原的纪念，也是中华民族文化传承的生动体现。

Every year on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month—the Dragon Boat Festival—families make and eat zongzi, race dragon boats, hang mugwort, and drink realgar wine. These customs,延续 for over two thousand years, are both a commemoration of the great poet Qu Yuan and a vivid embodiment of the living heritage of Chinese culture.
