# 屈原与粽子：端午节起源详解

# Qu Yuan and Zongzi: A Detailed Explanation of the Dragon Boat Festival Origins

## 一、屈原其人 | Qu Yuan: The Man Behind the Legend

### 中文

屈原（约公元前340年—约公元前278年），名平，字原，战国时期楚国丹阳（今湖北秭归）人。他是中国战国时期杰出的诗人、政治家，也是“楚辞”的创立者和代表人物。屈原出身于楚国贵族，与楚王同族，官拜左徒，主张联齐抗秦、富国强兵。

然而，由于遭贵族谗言陷害，屈原逐渐被楚王疏远、冷落，最终被流放。在流放期间，他写下了《离骚》《天问》《九章》等不朽诗篇，表达了对祖国深沉的热爱和对黑暗政治的悲愤。

公元前278年，秦国大将白起攻破楚国都城郢（今湖北江陵）。屈原目睹国破家亡，悲愤交加，于农历五月初五怀抱大石投汨罗江自尽，以身殉国。

### English

Qu Yuan (c. 340 BC – c. 278 BC), born Qu Ping and styled Yuan, was a native of Danyang in the State of Chu (present-day Zigui, Hubei Province) during the Warring States period. He was an outstanding poet, statesman, and the founder of the “Chu Ci” (Songs of Chu) literary tradition. Born into the Chu royal clan, Qu Yuan held the office of Left Minister and advocated for alliance with the State of Qi against the powerful State of Qin, as well as reforms to strengthen Chu.

However, slandered by jealous rivals at court, Qu Yuan gradually fell out of favor with the king and was eventually exiled. During his exile, he composed immortal poems such as Li Sao (Encountering Sorrow), Tianwen (Heavenly Questions), and Jiuzhang (Nine Pieces), expressing his profound love for his homeland and his grief over political corruption.

In 278 BC, General Bai Qi of Qin captured the Chu capital Ying (present-day Jiangling, Hubei). Unable to bear the fall of his beloved country, Qu Yuan, in deep despair, clasped a large stone and threw himself into the Miluo River on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month, ending his life for his country.

## 二、屈原投江与百姓的反应 | Qu Yuan’s Suicide and the People’s Response

### 中文

屈原投江的消息传开后，楚国百姓无不感到悲痛和惋惜。据南朝梁代吴均《续齐谐记》记载：“屈原五月五日自投汨罗而死，楚人哀之，每至此日，以竹筒贮米，投水祭之。”

百姓们因敬仰屈原的忠诚与正直，纷纷划船赶往汨罗江试图打捞他的遗体。他们争先恐后，一路追至洞庭湖，却始终未能找到屈原的踪迹。

与此同时，人们担心江中的鱼虾会啃食屈原的身体，便纷纷将米粮投入江中，希望以此引开鱼虾，保护这位爱国诗人的遗体。

### English

When news of Qu Yuan’s suicide spread, the people of Chu were overcome with grief and sorrow. According to Xu Qixie Ji (Continuation of Records of the Strange), written by Wu Jun of the Liang Dynasty: “Qu Yuan threw himself into the Miluo River and died on the fifth day of the fifth month. The people of Chu mourned him. Every year on this day, they would fill bamboo tubes with rice and throw them into the river as offerings.”

Admiring Qu Yuan for his loyalty and integrity, the local people rushed to the Miluo River in their boats, trying to retrieve his body. They raced one another all the way to Dongting Lake, but his body was never found.

At the same time, fearing that fish and other river creatures would devour Qu Yuan’s body, the people scattered rice into the river to distract the fish and protect the patriot’s remains.

## 三、粽子的起源 | The Origin of Zongzi

### 3\.1 最早的粽子——“筒粽” | The Earliest Zongzi — “Tongzong” (Bamboo Tube Rice)

### 中文

最早的粽子被称为“筒粽”。据传，屈原投江后，人们为了不让鱼虾损伤他的躯体，纷纷用竹筒装米投入江中。这就是中国最早的粽子——“筒粽”的由来。

此后，为了表示对屈原的崇敬和怀念，每到五月初五这一天，人们便用竹筒装米，投江祭奠。

### English

The earliest form of zongzi was called “tongzong” (bamboo tube rice). Legend has it that after Qu Yuan threw himself into the river, people filled bamboo tubes with rice and threw them into the water to prevent fish from damaging his body. This is the origin of China’s earliest zongzi — the “tongzong”.

From then on, to express their reverence and remembrance of Qu Yuan, people would fill bamboo tubes with rice and throw them into the river as offerings on the fifth day of the fifth month every year.

### 3\.2 粽子的演变 | The Evolution of Zongzi

### 中文

到了汉代，粽子的形态发生了变化。《续齐谐记》记载了一个有趣的故事：东汉建武年间，长沙人欧回在江边遇到一个自称是三闾大夫屈原的人。屈原（的鬼魂）对他说：“你们祭祀的米粮很好，但常常被江中的蛟龙偷吃。如果用楝叶把竹筒堵上，再用五彩丝线捆扎，蛟龙就会害怕而不敢靠近。”

从此，人们便开始用楝叶包裹糯米，用彩丝绑扎，投入江中祭祀屈原。这便是后来粽子的雏形。

到了晋代（265—420年），粽子开始成为端午节的特制食品。随着时间的推移，粽子的种类越来越丰富，从最初的竹筒装米，发展到用芦苇叶、箬叶等包裹，馅料也从单一的糯米扩展到红枣、豆沙、鲜肉、火腿、蛋黄等多种花样。

### English

During the Han Dynasty, the form of zongzi underwent changes. Xu Qixie Ji records an interesting story: During the Jianwu reign of the Eastern Han Dynasty, a man named Ouhui from Changsha met a man by the river who claimed to be Qu Yuan, the Minister of the Three Clans. Qu Yuan (or rather, his spirit) told him: “The rice offerings you make are excellent, but they are often stolen by the river dragons. If you wrap the rice in chinaberry leaves and tie them with five-colored threads, the dragons will fear them and stay away.”

From then on, people began wrapping glutinous rice in chinaberry leaves tied with colorful threads and throwing them into the river as offerings to Qu Yuan. This became the prototype of the zongzi we know today.

By the Jin Dynasty (265–420 AD), zongzi had become a specialty food for the Dragon Boat Festival. Over time, zongzi diversified greatly — from the original bamboo tube rice to reed leaves and bamboo leaves, and from plain glutinous rice to a wide variety of fillings including red dates, bean paste, fresh meat, ham, and egg yolk.

## 四、赛龙舟的起源 | The Origin of Dragon Boat Racing

### 4\.1 拯救屈原的传说 | The Legend of Rescuing Qu Yuan

### 中文

赛龙舟的起源，最广为流传的说法是为了纪念和拯救屈原。据南朝梁代宗懔《荆楚岁时记》记载：“五月五日竞渡，俗为屈原投汨罗日，人伤其死，故命舟楫以拯之。”

相传屈原投江后，楚国百姓因舍不得这位贤臣，许多人划船追赶拯救。他们争先恐后，一路追至洞庭湖，却始终不见屈原的踪迹。此后，为了纪念屈原，每年五月五日，人们都会划龙舟以表达哀思。

划龙舟还有另一个目的：人们借龙舟驱散江中之鱼，防止鱼吃掉屈原的身体。鼓声隆隆、船桨击水，都是为了吓跑鱼群。

### English

The most widely told origin of dragon boat racing is to commemorate and rescue Qu Yuan. According to Jingchu Suishiji (Records of the Seasons of Jingchu) by Zong Lin of the Liang Dynasty: “Boat races are held on the fifth day of the fifth month. It is customary because it is the day Qu Yuan threw himself into the Miluo River. People grieved his death, so they commanded boats to rescue him.”

Legend has it that after Qu Yuan drowned, the people of Chu, unwilling to lose their virtuous minister, raced out in boats to save him. They rowed frantically all the way to Dongting Lake, but Qu Yuan was nowhere to be found. Thereafter, in memory of Qu Yuan, people would hold dragon boat races on the fifth day of the fifth month every year.

Dragon boat racing also served another purpose: people used the boats to drive away the fish in the river, preventing them from eating Qu Yuan’s body. The beating of drums and the splashing of paddles were all meant to scare the fish away.

### 4\.2 从拯救到竞渡 | From Rescue to Competition

### 中文

最初，人们划船是为了打捞和拯救屈原，带有强烈的悲悼色彩。但随着时间的推移，这一行为逐渐演变为龙舟竞渡——一种带有竞技性质的民俗活动。

到了魏晋南北朝时期，纪念屈原的故事广泛流传，人们把赛龙舟和吃粽子都说成是为了纪念屈原。到南朝梁代《荆楚岁时记》成书时，五月五日竞渡源于屈原投汨罗已经成为普遍的说法。

赛龙舟在中国已有两千多年的历史。如今，龙舟竞赛已发展成一项国际性的水上体育运动，在世界各地的许多城市每年春天都会举办龙舟赛。

### English

Initially, people took to their boats to search for and rescue Qu Yuan — an act filled with grief and mourning. Over time, however, this practice gradually evolved into dragon boat racing — a competitive folk activity.

By the Wei, Jin, and Northern and Southern Dynasties period, the story of commemorating Qu Yuan had become widely known, and both dragon boat racing and eating zongzi were attributed to honoring him. By the time Jingchu Suishiji was compiled in the Liang Dynasty, it had become common belief that the boat races on the fifth day of the fifth month originated from Qu Yuan’s drowning in the Miluo River.

Dragon boat racing has a history of over 2,000 years in China. Today, dragon boat racing has developed into an international water sport, with nearly 60 races held outside China every spring in cities from Vancouver to Sydney, from Gdańsk to Cape Town.

## 五、其他说法与综合考证 | Other Theories and Scholarly Research

### 中文

需要指出的是，关于端午节、粽子和赛龙舟的起源，学术界还有不同的说法。

**赛龙舟的多种起源说**：除了纪念屈原，赛龙舟的起源还有纪念伍子胥、纪念越王勾践等说法。也有学者认为，龙舟竞渡最早是古代百越民族的龙图腾崇拜活动——人们将舟船刻画成龙形在水面竞渡，是对龙的祭祀。

**端午节的更古老起源**：据现代学者考证，端午节的起源更为古老，本与纪念屈原等人无关。闻一多先生在《端午考》等文章中指出，端午节吃粽子、赛龙舟等习俗都和古代百越民族的龙图腾崇拜有关。

尽管如此，屈原的故事因其所承载的爱国情怀和忠贞精神，逐渐成为端午节最深入人心的文化符号。自魏晋南北朝以来，纪念屈原便成为端午节最广为流传的主题。

### English

It is worth noting that scholars hold different views on the origins of the Dragon Boat Festival, zongzi, and dragon boat racing.

**Multiple Origin Theories for Dragon Boat Racing**: Besides commemorating Qu Yuan, there are theories linking dragon boat racing to Wu Zixu and King Goujian of Yue. Some scholars also believe that dragon boat racing originally stemmed from the dragon totem worship of the ancient Baiyue peoples — decorating boats as dragons and racing them on water was a form of dragon worship.

**More Ancient Origins of the Dragon Boat Festival**: Modern scholarly research suggests that the Dragon Boat Festival has even more ancient origins, not originally connected to figures like Qu Yuan. In his essay “A Study of the Dragon Boat Festival,” scholar Wen Yiduo pointed out that customs such as eating zongzi and dragon boat racing are related to the dragon totem worship of the ancient Baiyue peoples.

Nevertheless, because of the patriotic sentiment and unwavering loyalty embodied in Qu Yuan’s story, he gradually became the most enduring cultural symbol of the Dragon Boat Festival. Since the Wei, Jin, and Northern and Southern Dynasties, commemorating Qu Yuan has become the most widely known theme of the festival.

## 六、文化传承与现代意义 | Cultural Heritage and Modern Significance

### 中文

两千多年来，吃粽子和赛龙舟的习俗代代相传，成为中华民族最重要的传统节日之一。端午节与春节、中秋节并列为中国三大传统节日。

吃粽子的习俗不仅在中国盛行不衰，还流传到朝鲜、日本及东南亚各国。赛龙舟则已走向世界，成为一项受到全球人民喜爱和参与的体育活动。

2009年，端午节被联合国教科文组织列入人类非物质文化遗产代表作名录。这不仅是对端午节文化价值的国际认可，也是对屈原精神——爱国、忠诚、正直——的永恒铭记。

### English

For over 2,000 years, the customs of eating zongzi and racing dragon boats have been passed down through generations, becoming one of the most important traditional festivals of the Chinese nation. The Dragon Boat Festival, together with the Spring Festival and the Mid-Autumn Festival, is one of China’s three major traditional holidays.

The custom of eating zongzi has not only flourished in China but has also spread to Korea, Japan, and Southeast Asian nations. Dragon boat racing has gone global, becoming a sport loved and participated in by people around the world.

In 2009, the Dragon Boat Festival was inscribed on UNESCO’s Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. This is not only international recognition of the festival’s cultural value but also an enduring tribute to the spirit of Qu Yuan — patriotism, loyalty, and integrity.
