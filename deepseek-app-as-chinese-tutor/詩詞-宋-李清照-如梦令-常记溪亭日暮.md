**三、詳細釋義與深層內涵**

**1. 創作背景**  
這首詞是李清照早年（約十六七歲）在故鄉濟南所作，屬其「少女詞」的代表作。詞中以追憶筆法，記敘一次在溪亭遊玩飲酒、日暮歸舟的趣事。全詞不著一字議論，純以畫面串聯，卻成為千古傳誦的經典。

**2. 逐句字面含義**  
- **「常記溪亭日暮」**——時常記得那天在溪邊亭子遊玩，直到夕陽西下。點出時間（日暮）、地點（溪亭），且以「常記」開篇，表明此事令詞人印象深刻、久久難忘。  
- **「沉醉不知歸路」**——因為喝得盡興（或沉醉於美景），竟忘記了回去的路。「沉醉」一語雙關，既指酒醉，也指陶醉於山水之間。  
- **「興盡晚回舟」**——等到遊興滿足了，才在傍晚時分划船返程。一個「晚」字呼應前文的「日暮」，時序流轉自然。  
- **「誤入藕花深處」**——因天色漸暗且方向迷糊，小船竟划進了荷花叢的深處。此處「誤入」帶有意外之趣，為下文埋下伏筆。  
- **「爭渡，爭渡」**——急忙划槳、搶著找尋出路。兩個「爭渡」疊用，節奏急促，生動再現了詞人當下的慌張與急切。  
- **「驚起一灘鷗鷺」**——一陣急促的划水聲，驚起了棲息在灘上的水鳥（鷗鳥和鷺鷥），撲稜稜飛滿天。全詞在此戛然而止，留下一個充滿動感與聲響的畫面。

**3. 修辭手法與藝術特色**  
- **白描傳神**：全詞不用典故、不施濃彩，純以簡練筆墨勾勒場景，卻讓讀者如見其景、如聞其聲。  
- **動靜相生**：前五句偏靜（日暮、溪亭、藕花），末句陡然一動（驚起鷗鷺），以動破靜，餘韻無窮。  
- **疊句強化節奏**：「爭渡，爭渡」的重複，不僅是詞牌格律要求，更以語音上的急促感模擬划槳的動作與慌亂的心緒。

**4. 深層內涵與情感寄託**  
- **青春本真的再現**：這首詞是李清照對少女時代自由生活的「自畫像」。詞中沒有閨閣女性的矜持拘束，只有一個天真爛漫、率性而為的少女——飲酒、泛舟、迷路、驚鳥，處處洋溢著蓬勃的生命力。  
- **瞬間的永恆化**：詞人將一次偶然的「錯誤」（誤入藕花深處）轉化為一場美麗的奇遇。這種「無心之美」恰恰是青春最動人的特質。  
- **暮年回望的深層悵惘**：雖然全詞基調明快歡暢，但開頭的「常記」二字卻透露了寫作時與回憶時的「時間差」。當李清照在晚年歷經國破、家亡、夫逝之後，回看這一段無憂無慮的過往，那「驚起一灘鷗鷺」的喧鬧，反倒映襯出後半生的孤寂與漂泊。詞中未寫一字「愁」，但那股對逝去時光的眷戀與惘然，卻隱藏在歡快的筆觸之下，構成了中國古典詩詞中典型的「以樂景寫哀」的張力。

**III. Detailed Explanation and Deeper Meaning**

**1. Historical Context**  
This ci was composed in Li Qingzhao's early youth (around age 16–17) in her hometown of Jinan. It is a representative work of her "maiden lyrics." Written in retrospect, it recounts a delightful incident of drinking and boating at a creek-side pavilion at dusk. Without any direct commentary, the poem strings together vivid images, yet it has become a timeless classic.

**2. Literal Interpretation (Line by Line)**  
- **"Always remember the pavilion by the creek at sunset"** – She often recalls that day of playing by the creek pavilion until sunset. Starting with "always remember" highlights how deeply this memory is etched in her heart.  
- **"Drunk and lost, I knew not the way back"** – Having drunk freely (or being utterly intoxicated by the scenery), she forgot the return route. "Drunk" works as a pun—both wine and the enchantment of nature.  
- **"Having had my fill of pleasure, I turned the boat late"** – Only after her joy was fully satisfied did she row back in the evening. The word "late" echoes the "sunset" of the first line.  
- **"And strayed into the depths of the lotus flowers"** – As darkness fell and she lost her bearings, her boat drifted into the thick of the lotus blooms. This "straying" carries a delightful sense of accidental discovery, setting up the climax.  
- **"Rowing frantically, rowing frantically"** – Hastily paddling, desperately seeking a way out. The repetition quickens the rhythm, vividly conveying her fluster and urgency.  
- **"I startled a shoreful of gulls and egrets"** – The sudden splashing startled the waterbirds resting on the bank, sending them fluttering into the sky. The poem ends abruptly with this dynamic, sonorous image.

**3. Rhetorical Devices and Artistic Features**  
- **Vivid plain description (Baimiao)** : No allusions or ornate adornments—just stark, simple strokes that conjure an immediate scene with sound and motion.  
- **Interplay of stillness and motion** : The first five lines are relatively still (dusk, pavilion, lotus); the last line bursts into motion (startled birds). Motion breaks stillness, leaving endless resonance.  
- **Repetition for rhythmic urgency** : The duplicate "rowing frantically" not only fits the cipai's metrical pattern but also mimics—through verbal beat—the frantic paddling and flustered feelings.

**4. Deeper Emotional Core**  
- **Portrait of untainted youth** : This ci serves as a "self-portrait" of Li Qingzhao's maiden freedom. There is no restrained, cloistered lady here—only a spirited, spontaneous girl drinking, boating, losing her way, and startling birds. Every line pulses with raw vitality.  
- **Transforming chance into eternal beauty** : A simple "mistake" (straying into lotus depths) becomes a beautiful adventure. This "effortless beauty" is precisely what makes youth so captivating.  
- **Veiled sorrow of backward gazing** : Though the tone is cheerful and brisk, the opening "always remember" betrays the gap between the event and its recollection. When Li Qingzhao later endured national ruin, family loss, and her husband's death, looking back at this carefree episode, the noisy commotion of "startled gulls and egrets" only accentuates the loneliness of her later years. Though the word "sorrow" never appears, the nostalgia and faint melancholy hidden beneath the jovial surface create a classic tension in Chinese poetry—where joy is used to frame grief.
