#### 第一部分：歷史背景與故事詳情

#### Part 1: Historical Background and Detailed Story

**中文**

汪踦是春秋末期魯國的一位少年（約15歲）。公元前484年（魯哀公十一年），齊國派兵攻打魯國，雙方在魯國的郎地（今山東境內）展開激戰。當時魯國國力衰弱，面對強大的齊軍，形勢十分危急。

魯昭公的兒子公叔務人（亦稱公為）負責率軍抵禦。在巡視城防時，他看見汪踦，便感嘆國家的衰弱，認為若戰敗則對不起先君。汪踦雖是孩童，卻毫無畏懼，毅然拿起武器，跟隨公叔務人衝鋒陷陣。最終，兩人在這場保衛社稷的戰鬥中一同壯烈犧牲。

**English**

Wang Qi was a young teenager (around 15 years old) from the State of Lu during the late Spring and Autumn period. In 484 BCE (the 11th year of Duke Ai of Lu), the State of Qi invaded Lu. The two armies fiercely clashed at Lang (in present-day Shandong). At that time, Lu was militarily weak, and facing the formidable Qi forces, the situation was critical.

Gongshu Wuren (also known as Gong Wei), a son of Duke Zhao of Lu, led the defense. While inspecting the city walls, he noticed Wang Qi and lamented the state’s decline, saying they would fail their ancestors if defeated. Though merely a child, Wang Qi showed no fear. He resolutely took up his shield and spear, followed Gongshu Wuren into the thick of battle, and ultimately, both died heroically together in defense of their homeland.

#### 第二部分：經典出處與孔子評語（核心）

#### Part 2: Classical Source and Confucius’s Commentary (The Core)

**中文**

此事最權威的記載見於儒家經典《禮記·檀弓下》：

「魯人有童汪踦，死於郎之戰。或問曰：『可殤乎？』曰：『能執干戈以衛社稷，雖欲勿殤也，不亦可乎！』」

在當時的周禮中，未滿16歲（一說19歲）的未成年人死亡，稱為「殤」（夭折），喪葬禮儀必須降級，且不能以成年人規格下葬。然而，汪踦雖是「童子」（未成年人），卻立下赫赫戰功。當人們討論是否該以「殤禮」安葬他時，孔子果斷地給出千古名句：「他能拿起盾牌和長矛保衛國家，即使我們不想用成年人的喪禮對待他，難道不可以破例嗎？」（意即：為國捐軀者，不應以年齡論英雄。）

**English**

The most authoritative record of this event comes from the Confucian classic The Book of Rites (Liji - Tangong II):

"There was a youth of Lu named Wang Qi who died in the battle of Lang. Someone asked, 'Should he be buried with the rites due to a child who dies young (Shang)?' The reply was: 'He could wield a shield and spear to defend the state. Even if we wish not to treat him as an adult in death, is it not entirely appropriate to do so?'"

According to the Zhou ritual system, if a minor under 16 (or 19, per some interpretations) died, it was classified as "Shang" (premature death), requiring downgraded funeral rites that did not match adult standards. However, although Wang Qi was a "youth" (minor), he had rendered great military service. When people debated whether to apply the "child's funeral rites" to him, Confucius decisively made his timeless judgment: "He could hold shield and spear to safeguard the altars of the state. Even if we wished not to treat him as an adult, isn't it permissible to do so?" (Meaning: Those who die for their country should not be judged by age.)

#### 第三部分：歷史意義與文化遺產

#### Part 3: Historical Significance and Cultural Legacy

**中文**

1. 打破禮制束縛：孔子此舉並非廢棄禮儀，而是靈活運用「義」的精神。他肯定了汪踦的行為符合「大義」，因此可以「超越常禮」，這體現了儒家思想中「經」與「權」（原則與變通）的智慧。
2. 少年英雄的典範：汪踦成為中國歷史上第一位有明確記載的「童子軍」英雄。後世常以「汪踦」或「魯童」來稱頌英勇愛國的少年。
3. 華夏民族氣節：故事強調了「天下興亡，匹夫有責」的觀念。即使是最年輕的百姓，也有守護家園的義務，這種精神深深融入了中華民族的血脈。

**English**

1. Breaking Ritual Constraints: Confucius did not abandon the rites; rather, he flexibly applied the spirit of "Righteousness (Yi)." He affirmed that Wang Qi's actions aligned with "Supreme Justice," thus allowing an "exception to common rituals." This embodies the Confucian wisdom of "Jing and Quan" (Principles and Adaptability).
2. Model of a Teenage Hero: Wang Qi became the first clearly documented "underage soldier" hero in Chinese history. Later generations often used "Wang Qi" or "the Youth of Lu" to praise patriotic young people.
3. Ethos of the Chinese Nation: The story reinforces the idea that "every individual is responsible for the fate of their country." Even the youngest citizens have a duty to defend their homeland—a spirit deeply embedded in the Chinese cultural identity.

#### 第四部分：現代啟示

#### Part 4: Modern Reflection

**中文**

汪踦的故事至今仍有強烈的現實意義。它告訴我們：

- **英雄不問出處，更不問年齡**。真正的勇氣來自於對責任的擔當。
- **禮儀的本質在於內在的敬意**。孔子不在乎形式上的「殤」，而在乎汪踦對國家「社稷」的貢獻。這提醒我們，看待人事物應看其實質，而非表面的標籤。

**English**

The story of Wang Qi still holds profound contemporary relevance. It teaches us:

- **Heroes are defined not by their background or age, but by their courage**. True bravery stems from the willingness to shoulder responsibility.
- **The essence of etiquette lies in inner respect**. Confucius did not care about the formal label of "premature death"; he cared about Wang Qi's contribution to the "Altars of the State." This reminds us to judge people and actions by their substance, not by superficial labels.

---

**總結（Summary）**：汪踦衛國不僅是一則歷史事件，更是一場跨越千年的禮儀辯證。透過孔子的評語，一個拿起武器保家衛國的少年，永遠擺脫了「夭折」的陰影，成為了中國文化中「少年壯志」與「民族大義」的永恆象徵。

**Summary**: The story of Wang Qi defending his state is not just a historical event, but a millennium-spanning dialectic on rituals. Through Confucius's commentary, a boy who took up arms to protect his home was forever freed from the shadow of "dying young," becoming an enduring symbol of "youthful ambition" and "national righteousness" in Chinese culture.
