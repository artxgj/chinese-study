#### CHINESE SECTION

**摘要**

唐代詩歌「體大備矣」，既繼承了前代的豐富遺產，又發展出極盡精微的格律體系。唐人主要的師法對象包括《詩經》的四言詩、楚辭（騷體）、漢魏六朝的五言古詩、樂府民歌，以及齊梁時期萌芽的永明體（講究聲律的新體詩）[1]。在此基礎上，唐人將詩歌形式發展至完備，確立了**古體詩**（含五古、七古、樂府、歌行）與**近體詩**（含五絕、七絕、五律、七律、排律）兩大系統 [1]。以下將詳細說明各體裁的規則，並以李白及杜甫、王昌齡、王維等詩人的作品舉例 [2][3]。


**細節**

**一、唐代詩人繼承的前代詩體**

唐人作詩，博採眾長，主要師法以下前代詩體：

- **四言詩**：源於《詩經》，以四字一句為特色。唐作者不多，但李白偶有仿作 [1]。
- **楚辭／騷體**：戰國楚地詩歌，句式長短錯落，多用「兮」字。李白有《鳴皋歌》，杜甫有《桃竹杖引》等仿騷之作 [1]。
- **五言古詩**：始於西漢，盛於漢魏六朝。唐初沿襲六朝風格，至陳子昂始「復漢魏舊」。李白五古氣勢磅礴，有不可一世之概 [2]。
- **樂府詩**：始於漢武帝設立的樂府機構，後成為詩體名稱。唐人或沿用古題（古樂府），或自製新題（新樂府）。李白是古樂府創作的集大成者 [2]。
- **齊梁新體詩**：六朝時期講究聲律對偶的詩歌，是唐代近體詩的直接前身 [1]。唐人將梁陳時期的五言四韻、七言四韻之作加以改造，形成嚴格的律詩。

**二、唐代確立的詩體與辨識方法**

**（一）古體詩（Ancient Style Poetry）**

- **辨識特徵**：句數不定（可少可多）；字數以五言或七言為主，亦有雜言；平仄相對自由，不嚴格要求對仗；可押平聲韻或仄聲韻，允許換韻 [1]。
- **李白作品示例**：《月下獨酌》「花間一壺酒，獨酌無相親。舉杯邀明月，對影成三人。」句式自由，不講平仄對仗 [2]。
- **杜甫作品示例**：《石壕吏》「暮投石壕村，有吏夜捉人。老翁踰牆走，老婦出門看。」敘事性強，格律自由 [3]。
- **頂尖詩人**：李白（五古、七古皆冠絕一時）；杜甫（以古體敘事見長）[2][3]。

**（二）樂府詩（Yuefu Poetry）**

- **辨識特徵**：可分「古樂府」（沿用漢魏古題）與「新樂府」（唐人新製題目）；屬古體詩範疇，格律自由；題名常帶「歌」、「行」、「引」、「曲」、「謠」、「吟」等字；最初可入樂歌唱 [1]。
- **李白作品示例**：《將進酒》「君不見黃河之水天上來，奔流到海不復回。君不見高堂明鏡悲白髮，朝如青絲暮成雪。」雜言樂府，句式長短錯落 [2]。
- **杜甫作品示例**：《兵車行》「車轔轔，馬蕭蕭，行人弓箭各在腰。爺娘妻子走相送，塵埃不見咸陽橋。」新樂府典範，以時事入詩 [3]。
- **頂尖詩人**：李白（「樂府則太白擅奇古今」）；杜甫（新樂府的開創者）[2][3]。

**（三）歌行體（Gexing / Song Style）**

- **辨識特徵**：樂府詩的一種，題名多帶「歌」或「行」；句式長短錯落，以七言為主，間雜五言、三言等；格律自由，情感奔放 [1]。
- **李白作品示例**：《蜀道難》「噫吁嚱，危乎高哉！蜀道之難，難於上青天！」雜言歌行，氣勢磅礴 [2]。
- **杜甫作品示例**：《茅屋為秋風所破歌》「安得廣廈千萬間，大庇天下寒士俱歡顏，風雨不動安如山。」七言歌行，情感深沉 [3]。
- **頂尖詩人**：李白（歌行體冠絕古今）；杜甫（歌行體情感深厚）[2][3]。

**（四）五言絕句（Five-character Jueju）**

- **辨識特徵**：每首四句，每句五字，共二十字；屬近體詩，有嚴格的平仄格律；不要求對仗；通常押平聲韻，一韻到底 [1]。
- **李白作品示例**：《靜夜思》「床前明月光，疑是地上霜。舉頭望明月，低頭思故鄉。」（此詩為古體絕句，不講究格律，但唐代多數五絕為近體）[2]。
- **王維作品示例**：《鹿柴》「空山不見人，但聞人語響。返景入深林，復照青苔上。」近體五絕，平仄合律 [4]。
- **頂尖詩人**：王維（五絕意境深遠）；李白（五絕「冠古絕今」）[2][4]。

**（五）七言絕句（Seven-character Jueju）**

- **辨識特徵**：每首四句，每句七字，共二十八字；屬近體詩，格律嚴謹；不要求對仗；通常押平聲韻，一韻到底 [1]。
- **李白作品示例**：《早發白帝城》「朝辭白帝彩雲間，千里江陵一日還。兩岸猿聲啼不住，輕舟已過萬重山。」七絕典範，平仄工整 [2]。
- **王昌齡作品示例**：《出塞》「秦時明月漢時關，萬里長征人未還。但使龍城飛將在，不教胡馬度陰山。」七絕壓卷之作 [4]。
- **頂尖詩人**：王昌齡（「七絕聖手」）；李白（與王昌齡並稱唐代絕句最高峰）[2][4]。

**（六）五言律詩（Five-character Lüshi）**

- **辨識特徵**：每首八句，每句五字，共四十字；屬近體詩，平仄嚴謹；**頷聯（三、四句）與頸聯（五、六句）必須對仗**；通常押平聲韻，一韻到底 [1]。
- **李白作品示例**：《渡荊門送別》「渡遠荊門外，來從楚國遊。山隨平野盡，江入大荒流。月下飛天鏡，雲生結海樓。仍憐故鄉水，萬里送行舟。」頷聯頸聯對仗工整 [2]。
- **杜甫作品示例**：《春望》「國破山河在，城春草木深。感時花濺淚，恨別鳥驚心。烽火連三月，家書抵萬金。白頭搔更短，渾欲不勝簪。」頸聯「烽火連三月，家書抵萬金」對仗精工 [3]。
- **頂尖詩人**：杜甫（五律集大成者）；李白（五律現存約一百餘首，多用散句，風格自由）[2][3]。

**（七）七言律詩（Seven-character Lüshi）**

- **辨識特徵**：每首八句，每句七字，共五十六字；屬近體詩，平仄嚴謹；**頷聯與頸聯必須對仗**；通常押平聲韻，一韻到底 [1]。
- **李白作品示例**：《登金陵鳳凰台》「鳳凰台上鳳凰遊，鳳去台空江自流。吳宮花草埋幽徑，晉代衣冠成古丘。三山半落青天外，二水中分白鷺洲。總為浮雲能蔽日，長安不見使人愁。」中間兩聯對仗，是李白七律代表作（李白七律僅存八首）[2]。
- **杜甫作品示例**：《登高》「風急天高猿嘯哀，渚清沙白鳥飛回。無邊落木蕭蕭下，不盡長江滾滾來。萬里悲秋常作客，百年多病獨登台。艱難苦恨繁霜鬢，潦倒新停濁酒杯。」被譽為「古今七律第一」[3]。
- **頂尖詩人**：杜甫（七律登峰造極）；李白（七律數量極少，成就不算突出）[2][3]。

**（八）排律（Pailü / Extended Regulated Verse）**

- **辨識特徵**：超過八句的律詩，又稱「長律」；句數不限，可達數十句甚至上百句；通常為五言；其餘格律要求（平仄、對仗、押韻）與律詩相同 [1]。
- **李白作品示例**：現存五言排律數十首，但非其創作主流 [2]。
- **杜甫作品示例**：《奉贈韋左丞丈二十二韻》「紈袴不餓死，儒冠多誤身。丈人試靜聽，賤子請具陳……」二十二韻（四十四句）排律 [3]。
- **頂尖詩人**：杜甫（排律功力深厚）；李白（偶有佳作）[2][3]。

#### ENGLISH SECTION

**Abstract**

Tang poetry "achieved completeness in form," inheriting a rich legacy from previous dynasties while developing an extremely sophisticated prosodic system. The Tang poets' main models included the four-character poems of the *Book of Songs*, the *Chu Ci* (Sao style), the five-character ancient poems of the Han, Wei, and Six Dynasties, Yuefu folk songs, as well as the "Yongming Style" of the Qi and Liang periods that emphasized tonal prosody [1]. On this foundation, the Tang poets perfected poetic forms, establishing the two major systems of **Ancient Style Poetry** (including five-character ancient, seven-character ancient, Yuefu, and Gexing) and **Modern Style Poetry** (including five-character Jueju, seven-character Jueju, five-character Lüshi, seven-character Lüshi, and Pailü) [1]. Below is a detailed explanation of each form, illustrated with examples from Li Bai and other Tang poets such as Du Fu, Wang Changling, and Wang Wei [2][3].


**Details**

**I. Earlier Poetry Forms That Tang Poets Modeled Upon**

Tang poets drew extensively from previous traditions, primarily modeling their poetry on:

- **Four-character Poetry**: Originating from the *Book of Songs*, characterized by four-character lines. Few Tang poets wrote in this form, though Li Bai occasionally imitated it [1].
- **Chu Ci / Sao Style**: Poetry from the Warring States period in the Chu region, with lines of varying lengths and frequent use of the particle "兮." Li Bai's "Ming Gao Ge" and Du Fu's "Tao Zhu Zhang Yin" are examples of Sao-style imitations [1].
- **Five-character Ancient Poetry**: Originating in the Western Han and flourishing in the Han, Wei, and Six Dynasties. In the early Tang, it followed Six Dynasties styles until Chen Zi'ang revived the "old Han-Wei style". Li Bai's five-character ancient poems are majestic and powerful [2].
- **Yuefu Poetry**: Originating from the Music Bureau established by Emperor Wu of Han, later becoming a poetic genre. Tang poets either used old titles (Old Yuefu) or created new ones (New Yuefu). Li Bai is the great master of Old Yuefu [2].
- **Qi-Liang New Style Poetry**: Poetry from the Six Dynasties that emphasized tonal patterns and parallelism, serving as the direct predecessor of Tang regulated verse [1]. Tang poets transformed the five- and seven-character four-couplet poems of the Liang and Chen periods into strict Lüshi.

**II. Poetic Forms Established in the Tang Dynasty and How to Identify Them**

**(1) Ancient Style Poetry (Gushi)**

- **Identifying Features**: Variable number of lines; predominantly five- or seven-character lines, though mixed lengths also appear; relatively free tonal patterns; parallelism not strictly required; may use either level or oblique rhymes, with permitted rhyme changes [1].
- **Example from Li Bai**: *Drinking Alone under the Moon* – "Among the flowers, a jug of wine, / I drink alone, with no companion near. / I raise my cup to invite the bright moon, / And with my shadow, we are three." The form is free, without strict tonal parallelism [2].
- **Example from Du Fu**: *The Bailiff of Shihao* – "At dusk I put up at Shihao village, / A bailiff came to seize men in the night. / My old host climbed over the wall and fled, / His wife went out to answer the call." Narrative-driven, with free prosody [3].
- **Master Poet**: Li Bai (unmatched in both five- and seven-character ancient poetry); Du Fu (excellent in narrative ancient-style poems) [2][3].

**(2) Yuefu Poetry**

- **Identifying Features**: Divided into "Old Yuefu" (using Han-Wei old titles) and "New Yuefu" (newly created Tang titles); belongs to ancient style poetry with free prosody; titles often include characters like "歌" (song), "行" (ballad), "引" (introduction), "曲" (tune), "謠" (folk song), or "吟" (chant); originally intended to be set to music [1].
- **Example from Li Bai**: *Bring in the Wine* – "Do you not see the Yellow River's waters coming from heaven, / Rushing to the sea, never to return? / Do you not see the bright mirror in the high hall, / Grieving over white hair, / At dawn like black silk, at dusk turned to snow?" A mixed-length Yuefu with varying line lengths [2].
- **Example from Du Fu**: *Song of the War Chariots* – "Chariots rumble, horses neigh, / Soldiers carry bows and arrows at their waists. / Fathers, mothers, wives, and children run to see them off, / Dust rises, hiding the Xianyang Bridge." A classic New Yuefu, incorporating current events [3].
- **Master Poet**: Li Bai ("Li Bai excelled in Yuefu, unmatched across the ages"); Du Fu (pioneer of New Yuefu) [2][3].

**(3) Gexing / Song Style**

- **Identifying Features**: A subgenre of Yuefu; titles often include "歌" (song) or "行" (ballad); lines of varying lengths, predominantly seven-character but interspersed with five- and three-character lines; free prosody; passionate and unrestrained emotion [1].
- **Example from Li Bai**: *The Hard Road to Shu* – "Alas! How perilous and high! / The Shu Road is hard, / Harder than climbing to the blue sky!" A mixed-length Gexing with magnificent momentum [2].
- **Example from Du Fu**: *My Thatched Hut Unroofed by Autumn Gales* – "Could I get mansions covering ten thousand miles, / I'd house all poor scholars and make them smile, / In wind and rain, as secure as a mountain." A seven-character Gexing with profound emotion [3].
- **Master Poet**: Li Bai (Gexing style unequaled in the past and present); Du Fu (Gexing style with deep emotional resonance) [2][3].

**(4) Five-character Jueju (Quatrain)**

- **Identifying Features**: Four lines, five characters per line (20 characters total); belongs to Modern Style Poetry with strict tonal patterns; parallelism not required; usually uses level-tone rhyme throughout [1].
- **Example from Li Bai**: *Thoughts on a Quiet Night* – "Moonlight before my bed, / I take it for frost on the ground. / I raise my head to gaze at the moon, / Lower it, and think of my hometown." (This particular poem is actually an ancient-style Jueju, not strictly adhering to regulated tonal patterns, but most Tang five-character Jueju follow Modern Style rules.) [2]
- **Example from Wang Wei**: *The Deer Enclosure* – "Empty mountains, no one seen, / Only heard echoes of human speech. / Returning light enters the deep woods, / Again shines on the green moss." A Modern Style five-character Jueju with regular tonal patterns [4].
- **Master Poet**: Wang Wei (five-character Jueju with profound artistic conception); Li Bai (five-character Jueju "surpassing the ancient and the present") [2][4].

**(5) Seven-character Jueju (Quatrain)**

- **Identifying Features**: Four lines, seven characters per line (28 characters total); belongs to Modern Style Poetry with strict prosodic rules; parallelism not required; usually uses level-tone rhyme throughout [1].
- **Example from Li Bai**: *Leaving Baidi City at Dawn* – "At dawn I leave Baidi City amid the rosy clouds; / A thousand li to Jiangling, yet I return in a day. / The cries of apes on both banks never cease; / My light boat has already passed ten thousand mountains." A classic seven-character Jueju with precise tonal patterns [2].
- **Example from Wang Changling**: *On the Frontier* – "The moon of Qin, the passes of Han, / The long march of ten thousand li, no one has returned. / If the flying general of Longcheng were still here, / No Tartar horses would dare cross the Yin Mountains." A masterpiece of seven-character Jueju [4].
- **Master Poet**: Wang Changling ("Master of Seven-character Jueju"); Li Bai (on par with Wang Changling as the pinnacle of Tang quatrains) [2][4].

**(6) Five-character Lüshi (Regulated Verse)**

- **Identifying Features**: Eight lines, five characters per line (40 characters total); belongs to Modern Style Poetry with strict tonal patterns; **the second couplet (lines 3-4) and third couplet (lines 5-6) must have verbal and structural parallelism**; usually uses level-tone rhyme throughout [1].
- **Example from Li Bai**: *Farewell at the Jingmen Ferry* – "Sailing far from Jingmen Ferry, / I come to roam in Chu's domain. / Hills follow the plains' end, / The river flows into the vast wilderness. / The moon down from heaven mirrors the sky, / Clouds rise to form sea towers. / Still I cherish my homeland's water, / It escorts my boat for ten thousand li." The second and third couplets feature precise parallelism [2].
- **Example from Du Fu**: *Spring View* – "The state is broken, mountains and rivers remain; / In spring, the city is thick with grass and trees. / Grieving for the times, flowers shed tears; / Hatred of parting, birds startle the heart. / Beacon fires have burned for three months, / A letter from home is worth ten thousand gold. / I scratch my white hair even shorter, / It can barely hold a hairpin." The third couplet "Beacon fires... / A letter from home..." is a masterpiece of parallelism [3].
- **Master Poet**: Du Fu (consummate master of five-character Lüshi); Li Bai (about 100 extant five-character Lüshi, often using prose-like lines with a freer style) [2][3].

**(7) Seven-character Lüshi (Regulated Verse)**

- **Identifying Features**: Eight lines, seven characters per line (56 characters total); belongs to Modern Style Poetry with strict tonal patterns; **the second and third couplets must have parallelism**; usually uses level-tone rhyme throughout [1].
- **Example from Li Bai**: *On Climbing the Phoenix Terrace at Jinling* – "On Phoenix Terrace, phoenixes once came to play; / The birds have flown, the river flows on day by day. / Flowers and grass of Wu Palace bury the path; / Robes and caps of Jin Dynasty lie in ancient mounds. / Three peaks hang half outside the blue sky; / Two streams divide the Egret Isle. / Always clouds obscure the sun; / To see Chang'an not seen, I grieve." The middle couplets are parallel, a rare masterpiece of Li Bai's seven-character Lüshi (only eight survive) [2].
- **Example from Du Fu**: *On the Height* – "The wind so swift, the sky so wide, apes wail in grief; / The river清清, the sand so white, birds wheel in flight. / The boundless forest sheds its leaves shower by shower; / The endless river rolls its waves hour after hour. / A thousand miles from home, I grieve at autumn's plight; / Ill and old, I climb this height alone. / Hardships and regrets have frosted my temples; / Down and out, I've quit my cup of wine." Hailed as "the greatest seven-character Lüshi of all time" [3].
- **Master Poet**: Du Fu (seven-character Lüshi at its zenith); Li Bai (very few seven-character Lüshi, not a major part of his oeuvre) [2][3].

**(8) Pailü (Extended Regulated Verse)**

- **Identifying Features**: More than eight lines; also called "Long Lüshi"; number of lines unlimited, can reach dozens or even hundreds; usually five-character; all other prosodic rules (tonal patterns, parallelism, rhyme) are the same as Lüshi [1].
- **Example from Li Bai**: Dozens of five-character Pailü survive, but they are not his major contribution [2].
- **Example from Du Fu**: *Presented to Wei Zuo Cheng, Twenty-two Rhymes* – "The silk-clad don't starve to death, / But scholars often ruin their lives. / Sir, please listen quietly, / Let me speak in detail..." A twenty-two-rhyme (forty-four-line) Pailü [3].
- **Master Poet**: Du Fu (deep mastery of Pailü); Li Bai (occasional fine works) [2][3].


**III. Summary Assessment of Li Bai's Mastery by Form**

| Form                        | Li Bai's Mastery                | Remarks                                                      |
| :-------------------------- | :------------------------------ | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Five-character Ancient**  | Majestic                        | "An air of unsurpassed grandeur", represented by the 59 "Ancient Airs" [2] |
