**三、詩意詳細解析**

**（一）寫作背景**

〈望天門山〉是李白描繪長江山水景色的著名七言絕句，作於唐玄宗開元十三年（725年）李白初出巴蜀、乘舟東下時 [1]。天門山位於今安徽省蕪湖市東北的長江兩岸，東為東梁山（古代又稱博望山），西為西梁山（古代又稱梁山），兩山隔江對峙，形如門戶，故合稱天門山。李白此次出蜀，懷著「仗劍去國，辭親遠遊」的豪情壯志，面對壯闊的長江天險，寫下此詩，充分展現了盛唐時代的蓬勃氣象與詩人開闊的胸襟。

**（二）字詞釋義**

- **天門山**：位於今安徽省蕪湖市東北的長江兩岸，東梁山與西梁山隔江對峙，形如門戶。
- **中斷**：指天門山被長江從中間切斷，一分為二。
- **楚江**：長江流經古代楚地的一段，即今安徽境內的長江。
- **開**：衝開、劈開。
- **碧水**：清澈的江水。
- **至此回**：江水東流到天門山處迴旋激盪。
- **兩岸青山**：指天門山東西兩岸的東梁山和西梁山。
- **相對出**：兩山相對而出，互相對峙。
- **孤帆**：孤獨的一葉帆船。
- **日邊**：太陽旁邊，意指遠處天際。

**（三）逐句解析**

- **首句「天門中斷楚江開」**：詩人從遠處著筆，描寫長江以雷霆萬鈞之勢衝開了天門山，將原本連為一體的山脈一分為二。「中斷」與「開」兩字極富動感，化靜為動，表現出大自然的神奇力量與長江的雄渾氣勢 [1]。

- **次句「碧水東流至此回」**：江水東流至天門山狹窄的江口，受到兩岸山勢的阻遏，形成迴旋激盪的壯觀景象。「至此回」三字既寫出了水勢的變化，又暗含詩人乘舟行至此處的親身感受。

- **第三句「兩岸青山相對出」**：詩人視角由遠及近，描寫船行江中，兩岸的青山彷彿迎面而來，相對而出。「相對出」三字將靜止的山巒賦予動態，表現出詩人乘船行進中的視覺感受，極具現場感。

- **末句「孤帆一片日邊來」**：遠處一片孤帆從太陽旁邊駛來，點綴在遼闊的江面之上。「日邊」不僅指遠方天際，更給人以光明、壯麗的聯想，使整幅畫面充滿生機與希望。

**（四）整體意境與主題**

這首詩以雄奇的筆觸描繪了天門山的壯麗景色與長江的浩瀚氣勢，展現了李白對祖國山河的熱愛與讚美 [1]。全詩以動寫景，通過「開」、「回」、「出」、「來」四個動詞，將靜態的山水寫得生機勃勃，氣勢磅礡 [2]。

詩中的「孤帆一片日邊來」一筆，既是對眼前景物的真實描繪，也象徵著詩人初出茅廬、滿懷抱負的豪情壯志。整首詩境界開闊，氣勢雄渾，充滿了盛唐詩歌特有的樂觀昂揚精神，是李白山水詩的代表作之一。

**（五）藝術特色**

- **以動寫靜**：全詩通過「開」、「回」、「出」、「來」等動詞，將靜止的山水寫得充滿動感與生命力。
- **視角變換**：從遠望到近觀，從宏觀到微觀，層次分明，變換自然。
- **語言凝練**：短短四句二十八字，卻勾勒出一幅氣勢磅礡的長江山水畫卷。
- **情景交融**：壯麗的山水與詩人豪邁的情懷融為一體，達到情景交融的境界。

**III. Detailed Analysis**

**(1) Historical Background**

"Wang Tianmen Shan" is a famous seven-character quatrain written by Li Bai in 725 AD (the 13th year of the Kaiyuan era), when he first left Shu (Sichuan) and sailed eastward along the Yangtze River [1]. Mount Tianmen is located on both banks of the Yangtze River, northeast of present-day Wuhu City, Anhui Province. The eastern peak is called East Liang Mountain (formerly Bowang Mountain) and the western peak is called West Liang Mountain (formerly Liang Mountain). The two mountains face each other across the river, forming a shape like a gate, hence the name Tianmen (Heaven's Gate). This poem captures the young Li Bai's ambition and awe as he embarked on his journey into the wider world.

**(2) Vocabulary Explanations**

- **天門山 (Tiān Mén Shān)**: Mount Tianmen, located on both banks of the Yangtze River northeast of Wuhu, Anhui.
- **中斷 (Zhōng Duàn)**: Cut in two; the mountain is split by the river.
- **楚江 (Chǔ Jiāng)**: The Yangtze River flowing through the ancient Chu region (modern Anhui).
- **開 (Kāi)**: To split open, to cleave.
- **碧水 (Bì Shuǐ)**: Clear green water.
- **至此回 (Zhì Cǐ Huí)**: The river flows eastward but is forced to swirl and turn back here.
- **兩岸青山 (Liǎng Àn Qīng Shān)**: The green mountains on both banks (East and West Liang Mountains).
- **相對出 (Xiāng Duì Chū)**: The two mountains face each other, as if emerging to meet the viewer.
- **孤帆 (Gū Fān)**: A lone sailboat.
- **日邊 (Rì Biān)**: From beside the sun, meaning from the distant horizon.

**(3) Line-by-Line Interpretation**

- **First Line: "Tianmen is split in two by the opening of the Chu River."**  
  The poet begins with a grand view: the Yangtze River has split Mount Tianmen in two, forcing its way through the mountain gate. The verbs "split" (中斷) and "open" (開) are dynamic, turning a static scene into a powerful act of nature [1].

- **Second Line: "The green waters flow east but turn back here."**  
  The river, flowing eastward, is forced to swirl and eddy as it passes through the narrow strait between the two mountains. This line captures both the power of the water and the poet's personal experience of traveling through this dramatic landscape.

- **Third Line: "On both banks, green mountains emerge, facing each other."**  
  The perspective shifts from a distant view to the immediate surroundings. As the boat moves forward, the mountains on both sides seem to come forward, "emerging" to greet the traveler. This phrase animates the mountains, giving them a sense of presence and motion.

- **Fourth Line: "A single sail comes from the edge of the sun."**  
  In the distance, a lone sailboat appears, coming from the horizon, as if sailing out of the sun itself. This image conveys a sense of vastness and hope, symbolizing the poet's own journey into a bright and boundless future.

**(4) Overall Mood and Themes**

This poem uses bold imagery to depict the majestic scenery of Mount Tianmen and the mighty Yangtze River, expressing Li Bai's deep admiration for the natural world [1]. Through the dynamic verbs "opens" (開), "turns" (回), "emerge" (出), and "comes" (來), the poet transforms a static landscape into a living, energetic scene [2].

The image of the "single sail coming from the sun's edge" not only describes the scene but also embodies the poet's youthful ambition and optimism. The poem's vast perspective and vigorous spirit capture the essence of the High Tang era—confident, expansive, and full of promise.

**(5) Artistic Features**

- **Dynamic Description of Static Scenery**: Using powerful verbs ("split," "turn," "emerge," "come"), the poet brings the landscape to life.
- **Shifting Perspectives**: The poem moves from a distant, panoramic view to a close-up, immediate experience.
- **Concise Language**: In just twenty-eight characters, the poem creates a grand, sweeping landscape.
- **Fusion of Scene and Emotion**: The majestic scenery is seamlessly blended with the poet's bold, ambitious spirit.
