# Part 2: 中英雙語回答 (Bilingual Response)

## 中文部分

### 一、战国时期方言与文字的继续发展和分化

进入战国时期，随着周王室权威的彻底衰落，原先作为共同语的“雅言”失去了其标准音的地位[reference:118][reference:119]。长期的政治分裂使得 **“言语异声，文字异形”** （语出许慎《说文解字·叙》）的局面日益严重[reference:120][reference:121][reference:122][reference:123]。口語語言的不同，慢慢影響到文字上，也形成文字上較大的差異[reference:124][reference:125]。

**方言方面**：战国是语音最庞杂的时期。各国不再尊崇周室，纷纷以 **各自国家的方言为标准**[reference:126]。楚方言在这一时期形成并趋于成熟[reference:127][reference:128]。楚方言的形成是多民族语言融合的结果，华夏语在融合中起主导作用[reference:129][reference:130][reference:131]。楚方言中保留了清鼻流音和复辅音声母等西周春秋雅言的留存特征[reference:132][reference:133]。此外，像巴蜀地区还存在着与中原文字系统完全不同的 **“巴蜀图语”** [reference:134]。

**文字方面**：战国文字继承西周金文，但地域性差异急剧扩大。古文字学界一般将战国文字划分为 **“五系”** [reference:135][reference:136][reference:137]：

*   **秦系**（秦国）：相对保守，继承西周文字，字形规整，为后来小篆的基础[reference:138]。
*   **楚系**（楚及吴、越、曾、徐、蔡、宋等）：文字颀长飘逸，以**鸟虫书**最具特色[reference:139]。
*   **晋系**（韩、赵、魏及中山、郑、卫等）：结构修长[reference:140]。
*   **齐系**（齐、鲁、邾等）：增加装饰笔画[reference:141]。
*   **燕系**（燕国）：下笔稳重[reference:142]。

这种“异形”体现在**字形差异**（同一字不同写法）和**用字习惯差异**（同一词用不同字表示）两个层面[reference:143][reference:144]。例如“秋”字在各系中写法差异很大[reference:145]。

尽管如此，**“书同文”仍是战国文字的主流**[reference:146][reference:147]。各国文字同源商周金文，基本结构相通[reference:148]，加上各国政客商贾交往频繁[reference:149]，文字差异并未形成交流上的巨大障碍[reference:150]。

### 二、七雄并立对语言和文字的影响

**七雄争霸格局**从正反两方面深刻影响了语言和文字的发展：

**推动分化**：政治分裂使各国强化自身文化认同，纷纷以本国方言为“标准”，文字也向不同方向演变[reference:151]。楚王甚至在军事行动中有意使用楚语来保密军令[reference:152][reference:153]。

**促进交流与“雅言”存续**：频繁的外交、战争和商贸活动客观上需要沟通工具，**“雅言”作为共同语依然存在**[reference:154][reference:155]。苏秦、张仪等纵横家奔走各国游说，必须使用各方都能听懂的雅言[reference:156]。但雅言的普及有阶层局限，主要在上层贵族和士人中使用[reference:157]。

**催生沟通机制**：为应对语言障碍，各国发展出各种辅助沟通方式，如翻译[reference:158]、手势比划，以及专门教授多国语言的“译馆”[reference:159]。

### 三、语言误解是否导致了更多战争？

目前**没有直接证据**表明语言误解是导致战争增多的主要原因。战国战争的根源在于政治、经济和领土争夺。但语言差异在军事上确实是一个**影响因素**：

*   **利用方言保密**：前666年楚伐郑，楚将担心郑国玩“空城计”，**特意改用楚语下令全军撤退**，以防被郑军截获情报[reference:160][reference:161][reference:162]。这表明方言在军事上可被用作“加密”工具。

*   **外交沟通依赖雅言**：重要外交场合依赖雅言沟通，如果一方无法有效使用雅言，可能导致误解甚至冲突升级。能够熟练使用雅言的精英（如戎人代表驹支）[reference:163]可以更好地参与政治博弈。

### 四、战国文字文物的主要发现

战国时期留下了极为丰富的文字文物：

#### （一）简牍

*   **慈利楚简**：1987年湖南慈利出土，战国中期，1000余枚（残断成4000余片），21000余字，内容涉及《逸周书》、《国语》等，是楚系文字的重要代表[reference:164][reference:165][reference:166]。
*   **曾侯乙墓竹简**：湖北随县出土，战国早期[reference:167][reference:168]。
*   **郭店楚简**（荆门）、**望山楚简**、**信阳楚简**等。
*   **云梦睡虎地秦简**：秦统一前后的法律文书[reference:169]。

#### （二）盟书

*   **侯马盟书**：1965-1966年山西侯马出土，春秋晚期至战国早期，**5000余件**玉石片上用毛笔朱书书写，是晋系文字最直接的实物证据[reference:170][reference:171][reference:172][reference:173][reference:174]。

#### （三）帛书

*   **长沙子弹库楚墓帛书**：战国中晚期，是目前发现最早的帛书实物[reference:175]。

#### （四）青铜器铭文

*   各国青铜器上多有铭文，如**中山国石刻文字**[reference:176]、**鸟篆铭文戈**[reference:177]等。

#### （五）其他载体

*   **玺印、陶器、货币**等也都承载了大量战国文字[reference:178]。

这些文物表明，战国文字正处于 **“上承文字形体较统一的西周金文，下启文字严重异形的战国文字”** 的过渡阶段[reference:179]。各国文字在继承西周传统的基础上，逐步发展出各自的地域特色，为后来秦始皇 **“书同文字”** 政策提供了历史前提[reference:180][reference:181]。


## English Part

### I. The Continued Development and Divergence of Dialects and Scripts in the Warring States Period

As the Zhou royal authority collapsed, the standard language “Yayan” lost its status as the linguistic norm[reference:182][reference:183]. Prolonged political fragmentation led to a situation where **“speech differed in sound and writing differed in form”** (from Xu Shen’s *Shuowen Jiezi*)[reference:184][reference:185][reference:186][reference:187].

**Dialects**: The Warring States period was the most phonetically diverse era. States adopted **their own dialects as standards**[reference:188]. The Chu dialect formed and matured through multi-ethnic linguistic fusion[reference:189][reference:190][reference:191], preserving features from the Western Zhou Yayan[reference:192][reference:193]. In regions like Ba-Shu, there existed entirely different script systems like the **“Ba-Shu graphic script”** [reference:194].

**Scripts**: Warring States scripts inherited Western Zhou bronze inscriptions but developed significant regional variations. Scholars generally divide them into **five systems**[reference:195][reference:196][reference:197]:

*   **Qin system**: Conservative, regular forms, foundation for small seal script[reference:198].
*   **Chu system**: Elongated and elegant, famous for **bird-and-worm script**[reference:199].
*   **Jin system**: Slender structures[reference:200].
*   **Qi system**: Decorative strokes[reference:201].
*   **Yan system**: Steady strokes[reference:202].

This “variation” manifested in **shape differences** and **usage habit differences**[reference:203][reference:204].

However, **“script unification remained the mainstream”**[reference:205][reference:206]. All scripts shared a common origin, and frequent interactions meant differences did not pose insurmountable barriers[reference:207].

### II. How the Seven Hegemons Affected Dialects and Scripts

The seven-power configuration had a dual impact:

**Promoting divergence**: Political fragmentation led states to strengthen their cultural identities[reference:208].

**Promoting exchange and preserving Yayan**: Frequent diplomatic, military, and trade activities required a common language. **Yayan survived as a lingua franca**[reference:209][reference:210]. Strategists like Su Qin and Zhang Yi had to use Yayan to lobby across states[reference:211].

**Spawning communication mechanisms**: Translation[reference:212], gestures, and even “language schools” emerged[reference:213].

### III. Did Language Misunderstandings Lead to More Wars?

**No direct evidence** suggests language misunderstandings were a primary cause. However, linguistic differences were a **tactical factor**:

*   **Using dialects for secrecy**: In 666 BCE, a Chu general **deliberately gave orders in the Chu dialect** to prevent interception by Zheng forces[reference:214][reference:215][reference:216].

*   **Diplomacy relied on Yayan**: Effective use of Yayan was crucial. Those who mastered it could better participate in political maneuvering[reference:217].

### IV. Surviving Artifacts of Written Scripts from the Warring States Period

#### (A) Bamboo and Wooden Slips

*   **Cili Chu Slips**: Unearthed in Hunan in 1987, mid-Warring States period, over 1,000 slips with more than 21,000 characters[reference:218][reference:219][reference:220].
*   **Tomb of Marquis Yi of Zeng Slips**: Suixian, Hubei[reference:221][reference:222].
*   **Guodian Chu Slips**, **Wangshan Chu Slips**, **Xinyang Chu Slips**.
*   **Yunmeng Shuihudi Qin Slips**: Legal texts[reference:223].

#### (B) Covenant Texts

*   **Houma Covenant Texts**: Unearthed in Shanxi in 1965-1966, late Spring and Autumn to early Warring States, **over 5,000 pieces** of jade and stone with red ink brush writing[reference:224][reference:225][reference:226][reference:227][reference:228].

#### (C) Silk Manuscripts

*   **Changsha Zidanku Chu Silk Manuscript**: Mid-to-late Warring States period[reference:229].

#### (D) Bronze Inscriptions

*   Inscriptions on bronze vessels from various states[reference:230][reference:231].

#### (E) Other Carriers

*   **Seals, pottery, and currency** also bear Warring States script[reference:232].

These artifacts show that Warring States script was in a transitional phase[reference:233], eventually leading to Qin Shi Huang’s **“script unification”** policy[reference:234][reference:235].
