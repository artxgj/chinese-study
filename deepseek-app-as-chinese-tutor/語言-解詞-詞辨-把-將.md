## 1. Prompt Repetition & Chinese Essence

**Original Prompt (English):**  
> 「把」和「將」這兩個常用詞，在實際造句時怎麼用、聽起來什麼感覺、各自強調的重點有什麼不一樣。

**Chinese Essence (简明口语化):**  
> 你問的是「把」和「將」這對在中文裡用來「處置」或「對待」某個對象的介詞，實際造句時怎麼用、聽起來語感差在哪、各自強調的重點是什麼。

## 2. Bilingual Response

### 中文部分 (Chinese Section)

**摘要**  
「把」和「將」的核心區別在於**語體色彩**和**正式程度**，語法功能上高度重疊（都引導賓語提前）。**「把」是口語核心，日常必備，語氣自然、隨意、動作感強**；**「將」是書面核心，正式典雅，語氣莊重、客觀、帶有規劃性或文學性**。另外，「將」除了當介詞，還能當時間副詞（表「即將、快要」），而「把」沒有這個功能。掌握「日常動手」vs「書面處置」這把尺，就能用得恰如其分。

**詳細說明**

**1. 「把」——動作處置，口語核心**  
- **核心用法**：用於口語和日常書寫中，將動詞的賓語提前，強調「對這個東西做了什麼」。它是現代漢語口語中最核心的處置式標記。  
- **語感**：自然、隨性、貼近生活。帶有較強的「動作施加」感，常用於具體的、當下的、實際的操作。  
- **側重點**：**「動作的執行和對賓語的影響」**。  
- **例句**：  
  - 請**把**門關上。（日常口語指令，非常自然）  
  - 我已經**把**那本書看完了。（強調「書」被我「看完」這個結果）  
  - 他**把**房間打掃得乾乾淨淨。（強調「房間」受到的處理）

**2. 「將」——書面處置，正式典雅**  
- **核心用法**：語法上與「把」完全相同（將 + 賓語 + 動詞短語），但幾乎只用於**書面語、正式公文、新聞報導、演講或古典文學風格**的語境中。  
- **語感**：莊重、簡潔、冷靜。帶有「規劃、部署、客觀陳述」的意味，不像「把」那樣帶有個人當下的動作感。  
- **側重點**：**「正式地引出受事對象」**，凸顯動作的計畫性或結果的嚴肅性。  
- **例句**：  
  - 請**將**門窗關閉。（正式公告語體，對比「把門窗關上」更顯書面）  
  - 公司決定**將**該專案延期。（正式商務書面語）  
  - 我們**將**此方案提交董事會審議。（公文語氣，莊重客觀）

**3. 「將」特有的時間副詞用法（「把」沒有）**  
- 「將」除了當介詞，還可以當時間副詞，表示「即將、快要、未來會」。此時不能替換成「把」。  
- 例句：  
  - 天色**將**暗。（快黑了，書面語）  
  - 他**將**於明天抵達北京。（正式通知，表將來）

**4. 決定性選詞法則：看語境和動詞**  
- **試換法**：一條最實用的經驗法則——**在口語對話中，優先選「把」；在正式寫作（公文、論文、新聞）中，優先選「將」**。  
- **動詞音節搭配**：「把」可以輕鬆搭配單音節口語動詞（把書**放**下、把燈**關**了）；「將」搭配單音節詞雖然可行，但聽起來文縐縐（將書**置**於桌上），日常極少用，通常搭配雙音節書面動詞（將檔案**提交**、將問題**解決**）。

**5. 對比總表（一目了然）**

| 詞語   | 詞性       | 語體色彩           | 核心語感             | 典型例句場景             | 可否表「將來」   |
| :----- | :--------- | :----------------- | :------------------- | :----------------------- | :--------------- |
| **把** | 介詞       | 口語 / 日常        | 自然、動作感強、隨性 | 日常對話、家務操作、日記 | ❌ 不行           |
| **將** | 介詞／副詞 | 書面 / 正式 / 典雅 | 莊重、客觀、計畫性   | 公告、新聞、合約、演講   | ✅ 可以（副詞時） |

**6. 實戰選詞心法**  
- 跟家人同事說「幫我把那個拿過來」→ 用 **「把」**，不然太怪。  
- 寫會議紀錄或公文「請將本通知轉發各單位」→ 用 **「將」**，不然顯得不夠正式。  
- 寫作文或小說想提高文氣 → 適度用 **「將」**（「將一切煩惱拋諸腦後」），但一篇裡不要超過兩三次，否則矯揉造作。  
- 一句話檢查：如果後面接的是「關上、拿走、吃掉、擦擦」這種口語動詞 → 絕不選「將」；如果後面接的是「實施、執行、提交、審閱」這種書面雙音節動詞 → 可優先考慮「將」。

### English Section

**Abstract**  
The core difference between「把」(bǎ) and「將」(jiāng) lies in **register and formality**, though their grammatical functions largely overlap (both are prepositions that move the object before the verb). **「把」is the backbone of spoken Chinese – natural, casual, and action-oriented**; **「將」is the backbone of written Chinese – formal, elegant, and carrying a sense of planning or literariness**. Additionally,「將」can also function as a temporal adverb meaning "about to" or "will," a role that「把」cannot play. Grasp the contrast between "everyday handling" and "formal disposition," and you will use them appropriately.

**Detailed Explanation**

**1. 「把」(bǎ) – Action Disposal, Spoken Core**  
- **Core Usage**: Used in spoken and casual written Chinese to move the object before the verb, emphasizing "what was done to this thing." It is the core disposal marker in modern spoken Mandarin.  
- **Tone**: Natural, casual, grounded in daily life. Carries a strong sense of "applied action," often used for concrete, immediate, and practical operations.  
- **Emphasis**: **"The execution of the action and its effect on the object."**  
- **Examples**:  
  - 請**把**門關上。(Please **把** the door close – everyday spoken instruction, very natural.)  
  - 我已經**把**那本書看完了。(I have already **把** that book finished reading – emphasizes the result "finished" on the book.)  
  - 他**把**房間打掃得乾乾淨淨。(He **把** the room cleaned spotless – emphasizes the treatment of the room.)

**2. 「將」(jiāng) – Formal Disposal, Written Core**  
- **Core Usage**: Grammatically identical to「把」(將 + object + verb phrase), but almost exclusively used in **written language, official documents, news reports, speeches, or classical/literary styles**.  
- **Tone**: Solemn, concise, calm. Carries a sense of "planning, deployment, and objective statement," unlike「把」's immediate personal action feel.  
- **Emphasis**: **"Formally introducing the object of action,"** highlighting the planned nature of the action or the seriousness of the outcome.  
- **Examples**:  
  - 請**將**門窗關閉。(Please **將** the doors and windows close – formal announcement style, contrasting with the more spoken「把門窗關上」.)  
  - 公司決定**將**該專案延期。(The company decided **將** the project postpone – formal business writing.)  
  - 我們**將**此方案提交董事會審議。(We **將** this proposal submit to the board for review – official document tone, solemn and objective.)

**3. 「將」's Unique Temporal Adverb Usage (Not shared by「把」)**  
- Besides being a preposition,「將」can be a temporal adverb meaning "about to," "soon," or "will" (future). In this usage, it cannot be replaced by「把」.  
- Examples:  
  - 天色**將**暗。(The sky **will** soon grow dark – literary/formal.)  
  - 他**將**於明天抵達北京。(He **will** arrive in Beijing tomorrow – formal notice.)

**4. The Decisive Selection Rule: Look at Context and Verb**  
- **The Swap Test**: A very practical rule – **in spoken conversation, prioritize「把」; in formal writing (documents, papers, news), prioritize「將」**.  
- **Verb syllable collocation**:「把」works seamlessly with monosyllabic spoken verbs (把書**放**下 – put the book down, 把燈**關**了 – turn the light off). While「將」can technically pair with monosyllabic verbs, it sounds overly literary (將書**置**於桌上 – place the book on the table) and is rarely used in daily speech. It typically pairs with bisyllabic written verbs (將檔案**提交** – submit the file, 將問題**解決** – resolve the issue).

**5. Comparison Table (At a Glance)**

| Word   | Part of Speech       | Register                   | Core Tone                      | Typical Example Scenario                 | Can Mean "Will" (Future) |
| :----- | :------------------- | :------------------------- | :----------------------------- | :--------------------------------------- | :----------------------- |
| **把** | Preposition          | Spoken / Daily             | Natural, action-driven, casual | Daily chat, chores, diaries              | ❌ No                     |
| **將** | Preposition / Adverb | Written / Formal / Elegant | Solemn, objective, planned     | Announcements, news, contracts, speeches | ✅ Yes (as adverb)        |

**6. Practical Decision‑Making Guide**  
- Telling a family member or colleague "Help me **把** that bring over" → Use **「把」**, otherwise it sounds bizarre.  
- Writing meeting minutes or an official notice "Please **將** this notice forward to all units" → Use **「將」**, otherwise it doesn't sound formal enough.  
- Writing an essay or story to elevate the literary tone → Use **「將」** moderately (e.g.,「將一切煩惱拋諸腦後」– cast all worries aside), but don't overuse it – more than two or three times per piece and it starts to feel pretentious.  
- A quick litmus test: if the following verb is a casual spoken monosyllable like 關上, 拿走, 吃掉, 擦擦 → never choose「將」; if it is a formal bisyllabic verb like 實施, 執行, 提交, 審閱 → prioritize「將」.
