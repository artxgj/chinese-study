**三、詩意詳細解析**

**（一）寫作背景**

〈早發白帝城〉是李白最著名的七言絕句之一，作於唐肅宗乾元二年（759年）春天。當時李白因永王李璘事件被流放夜郎（今貴州），行至白帝城（今重慶奉節）時，突然接到朝廷赦免的消息，隨即乘舟東下江陵（今湖北荊州），途中所作 [1]。此詩記錄了詩人遇赦後的歡快心情與長江三峽的壯麗景色，是李白晚年詩作中難得的輕快之作 [2]。

**（二）字詞釋義**

- **早發**：早晨出發。
- **白帝城**：位於今重慶市奉節縣東，三峽西口，古代著名關隘。
- **彩雲間**：白帝城高聳入雲，故云。
- **千里**：白帝城至江陵的距離，約五百多公里，古代以「千里」極言其遠。
- **江陵**：今湖北荊州市，古稱江陵。
- **一日還**：一天就可到達，極言船行之快。
- **猿聲**：長江三峽兩岸猿猴的啼叫聲。
- **啼不住**：連綿不斷，啼聲不絕。
- **輕舟**：輕快的小船。
- **萬重山**：極言山之多、之高、之險。

**（三）逐句解析**

- **首句「朝辭白帝彩雲間」**：詩人清晨從高聳入雲的白帝城出發。彩雲間既寫白帝城地勢之高，又暗示詩人遇赦後的明朗心境，朝霞與彩雲相映，充滿了希望與喜悅 [1]。

- **次句「千里江陵一日還」**：千里之外的江陵，一日之間即可到達。「還」字不僅指到達，更有「歸來」之意，暗含詩人經歷劫難後重獲自由的歡欣。此句極言船行之快，體現了詩人歸心似箭的迫切心情。

- **第三句「兩岸猿聲啼不住」**：長江三峽兩岸的猿猴啼叫聲連綿不斷。猿聲本是淒涼之聲，但在此詩中，卻被詩人輕快的心情所「過濾」，反而增添了旅途的生趣與節奏感。

- **末句「輕舟已過萬重山」**：輕快的小舟已經越過了萬重山巒。此句以極度的誇張，寫出船行之快、三峽之險、詩人之喜。萬重山既是實景，也象徵詩人一生所經歷的艱難險阻，如今已被拋在身後。

**（四）整體意境與主題**

這首詩以極快的節奏和極度誇張的語言，描寫了詩人從白帝城到江陵的長江三峽之旅，表達了詩人遇赦後的狂喜與對自由生命的歌頌 [1]。全詩一氣呵成，節奏明快，氣勢奔騰，被譽為「古今七絕第一」 [2]。

詩中的「千里江陵一日還」與「輕舟已過萬重山」，既是寫實，又是詩人精神狀態的寫照。經歷了流放的磨難，詩人終於重獲自由，心中之喜躍然紙上。整首詩充滿了盛唐詩歌特有的豪放與樂觀，是李白豪放詩風的絕佳代表。

**（五）藝術特色**

- **誇張與寫實並存**：「千里」與「一日」對比強烈，既是誇張，又是詩人真實感受的體現。
- **節奏明快**：全詩語言流暢，節奏輕快，與船行的速度感相呼應。
- **情景交融**：猿聲、輕舟、萬重山等意象，既是客觀景物，又是詩人主觀情感的投射。
- **雙重象徵**：「萬重山」既是實寫三峽之險，也象徵人生的艱難困苦。

**III. Detailed Analysis**

**(1) Historical Background**

"Zao Fa Baidi Cheng" is one of Li Bai's most famous seven-character quatrains, written in the spring of 759 AD. Li Bai had been exiled to Yelang (modern Guizhou) due to his involvement with Prince Yong's rebellion. He had reached Baidi City (modern Fengjie, Chongqing) when he unexpectedly received a pardon from the emperor and immediately sailed eastward down the Yangtze toward Jiangling (modern Jingzhou, Hubei) [1]. This poem captures his immense joy upon receiving the pardon and the breathtaking scenery of the Yangtze Gorges. It is a rare, lighthearted work from Li Bai's later years [2].

**(2) Vocabulary Explanations**

- **早發 (Zǎo Fā)**: Departing at dawn.
- **白帝城 (Bái Dì Chéng)**: Baidi City, located at the western entrance to the Three Gorges, in present-day Fengjie, Chongqing.
- **彩雲間 (Cǎi Yún Jiān)**: Surrounded by colorful clouds, emphasizing the city's height.
- **千里 (Qiān Lǐ)**: A thousand *li* (about 500 km), exaggerating the great distance.
- **江陵 (Jiāng Líng)**: Jiangling, present-day Jingzhou, Hubei.
- **一日還 (Yī Rì Huán)**: Returning in a single day, emphasizing the boat's speed and the poet's eagerness.
- **猿聲 (Yuán Shēng)**: The cries of monkeys along the gorges, traditionally a mournful sound.
- **啼不住 (Tí Bù Zhù)**: Ceaseless crying.
- **輕舟 (Qīng Zhōu)**: A light, fast boat.
- **萬重山 (Wàn Chóng Shān)**: Myriad layers of mountains, symbolizing both the physical gorges and life's hardships.

**(3) Line-by-Line Interpretation**

- **First Line: "At dawn I leave Baidi City, amidst the colorful clouds."**  
  The poet departs from the towering Baidi City at dawn, surrounded by radiant clouds. "Colorful clouds" evokes both the height of the city and the poet's hopeful, joyful mood after receiving the pardon [1].

- **Second Line: "A thousand *li* to Jiangling, but I return in a single day."**  
  The great distance to Jiangling is covered in a single day, emphasizing incredible speed. The word "return" (還) is key—it implies not just arrival, but the joy of coming back after a period of exile and suffering.

- **Third Line: "On both banks, the monkeys' cries never cease."**  
  The constant cries of the monkeys along the riverbanks are normally a mournful sound, but filtered through the poet's joy, they become a lively part of the thrilling journey, adding rhythm and excitement.

- **Fourth Line: "My light boat has already passed the ten thousand mountains."**  
  The light boat has swiftly sailed past the countless mountains. This hyperbole captures both the speed of the boat and the poet's sense of overcoming immense obstacles. The "ten thousand mountains" symbolize not only the towering gorges but also the many hardships and dangers the poet has left behind.

**(4) Overall Mood and Themes**

This poem, with its rapid rhythm and bold exaggeration, depicts the poet's journey through the Three Gorges and expresses his overwhelming joy at being pardoned [1]. The poem flows with a sense of exhilarating speed and unrestrained spirit. It is often hailed as "the greatest seven-character quatrain ever written" [2].

The lines "A thousand *li* to Jiangling in a single day" and "my light boat has already passed the ten thousand mountains" are both realistic description and a portrait of the poet's liberated spirit. After the ordeal of exile, Li Bai had regained his freedom, and the poem is a pure expression of joy and relief, capturing the bold and optimistic essence of High Tang poetry.

**(5) Artistic Features**

- **Hyperbole Blended with Reality**: The contrast between "a thousand *li*" and "a single day" is both an exaggeration and a true reflection of the poet's exhilarating experience.
- **Fast Rhythm**: The poem's language is smooth and rapid, matching the speed of the journey.
- **Fusion of Scene and Emotion**: The monkeys' cries, the light boat, and the countless mountains are not just scenery; they are projections of the poet's emotions.
- **Double Symbolism**: "Ten thousand mountains" is both a literal description of the gorges and a metaphor for the poet's past trials and tribulations.
