## 古代泉州对外贸港口的由来和发展
## The Origin and Development of Ancient Quanzhou as a Foreign Trade Port

---

### 一、由来：刺桐古港的诞生
### I. Origin: The Birth of the Ancient Port of Zayton

泉州港古称 **“刺桐港”** (Zayton / Zaitun），因港口所在的城市遍栽刺桐树而得名。刺桐是一种热带植物，随着海外贸易的兴起由海外传入泉州。外国商人约定俗成地将泉州出产的丝绸、陶瓷分别冠以“刺桐”之名，使这一名称蜚声海外。

泉州港坐落于福建晋江下游出海口，海岸线曲折绵长，多天然良港，具备优越的船只停泊条件。历史上这片区域以 **“三湾十二港”** 闻名于世。

Quanzhou Port was historically known as **"Zayton"** (or Zaitun), named after the coral trees (Erythrina variegata) that were widely planted throughout the city. The coral tree, a tropical plant, was introduced to Quanzhou through overseas trade. Foreign merchants commonly referred to the silk and ceramics produced in Quanzhou as "Zayton" products, making the name famous worldwide.

Located at the estuary of the Jinjiang River in Fujian, Quanzhou Port boasts a long and winding coastline with numerous natural deep-water harbors, providing excellent conditions for ship anchorage. Historically, this area was renowned as the **"Three Bays and Twelve Harbors"**.

---

### 二、发展历程
### II. Development History

#### (一)南朝至唐代：初兴与崛起

#### (I) Southern Dynasties to Tang Dynasty: Early Rise

泉州与海外的交往最早可追溯到公元**6世纪的南朝**。唐代，泉州港开始崭露头角，成为**中国古代四大对外贸易港口之一**。唐朝在泉州设参军一职，负责管理海外交通贸易事宜。当时的泉州已出现**“市井十洲人”**的盛况，贸易对象辐射阿拉伯半岛、东南亚及东亚诸国。

公元**720年(唐开元八年）** ，晋江航海家林銮在石湖半岛建造码头，开通泉州至渤泥(今文莱）航线，以丝绸换取香料。石湖码头因此被称为 **“林銮渡”** 。

Quanzhou's overseas interactions can be traced back to the **Southern Dynasties in the 6th century AD**. During the Tang Dynasty, Quanzhou Port began to emerge as **one of China's four major foreign trade ports**. The Tang court established the position of Canjun (Military Advisor) to manage overseas transportation and trade affairs. The port witnessed the remarkable scene of **"traders from ten foreign nations"** gathering in the city, with trade networks reaching the Arabian Peninsula, Southeast Asia, and East Asian countries.

**In 720 AD (the 8th year of Kaiyuan, Tang Dynasty)** , the Jinjiang navigator Lin Luan constructed a dock at the Shihu Peninsula and opened a sea route from Quanzhou to Brunei (then known as Bonit) to trade silk for spices. This dock became known as **"Linluan Ferry"**.

#### (二）五代十国：奠基与拓展

#### (II) Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms: Foundation and Expansion

五代十国时期，闽王深知贸易的重要性，大力招徕外商、吸引外资，加大城市基础设施建设，新建道路、增开货栈。泉州港依托得天独厚的自然条件，跻身**中国南方四大商港之列**。

During the Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms period, the King of Min fully recognized the importance of trade, actively attracted foreign merchants and investment, and significantly expanded urban infrastructure by building new roads and warehouses. Leveraging its exceptional natural conditions, Quanzhou Port rose to become **one of the four great commercial ports in southern China**.

#### (三）宋代：正式开港与繁荣鼎盛

#### (III) Song Dynasty: Official Port Status and Prosperity

**北宋元祐二年(1087年）** 是泉州港历史上具有**里程碑意义**的一年——朝廷在泉州设立 **市舶司**(相当于古代海关），标志着泉州**正式成为开放的国家对外贸易口岸**。

市舶司的设立源于泉州港日益增长的通商需求。此前泉州商人出海须远赴广州或杭州、明州(今宁波）办理手续，诸多不便。市舶司设立后，泉州海外贸易迅速发展，与泉州进行贸易的国家和地区达到**100多个**。

进入南宋，随着政治经济重心南移，泉州港地位持续攀升，**一度超越广州，成为中国第一大港**。1974年在后渚港发掘的**宋代海船**，出土了2350多公斤香料药物，见证了泉州造船业的发达和海外贸易的繁盛。

**In 1087 AD (the 2nd year of Yuanyou, Northern Song Dynasty)** — a **milestone** year in Quanzhou's maritime history — the imperial court established the **Maritime Trade Supervisorate** (Shi Bo Si, equivalent to an ancient customs office) in Quanzhou, officially designating it as a **national foreign trade port**.

The establishment of the Supervisorate was driven by Quanzhou's growing trade demands. Previously, Quanzhou merchants had to travel to Guangzhou, Hangzhou, or Mingzhou (modern Ningbo) to obtain trade permits. After its establishment, Quanzhou's overseas trade expanded rapidly, reaching **over 100** countries and regions.

During the Southern Song Dynasty, as the political and economic center shifted southward, Quanzhou's status continued to rise, **surpassing Guangzhou to become China's largest port**. The **Song Dynasty ship** excavated at Houzhu Harbor in 1974, which yielded over 2,350 kilograms of spices and medicinal products, stands as powerful evidence of Quanzhou's advanced shipbuilding industry and prosperous overseas trade.

#### (四）元代：东方第一大港的巅峰

#### (IV) Yuan Dynasty: The Peak of the "Greatest Port in the East"

元代是泉州港的**极盛时期**。**至元十四年(1277年）** ，元朝复置泉州市舶司。阿拉伯裔商人蒲寿庚凭借其海外人脉与商贸经验，被委任提举市舶司，广招蕃商来泉贸易，推动泉州港与**近百个国家和地区**建立了贸易联系。泉州对外通商的国家和地区由南宋时的58个增至98个，进出口货物在**400种以上**。

意大利旅行家**马可·波罗**在游记中盛赞刺桐港为 “世界最大良港之一” 。**1292年**，马可·波罗从泉州后渚港出海返回威尼斯。另一位意大利犹太商人雅各则誉泉州为 **“光明之城”** 。

The Yuan Dynasty marked the **zenith** of Quanzhou Port. **In 1277 AD (the 14th year of Zhiyuan)** , the Yuan court re-established the Maritime Trade Supervisorate in Quanzhou. Pu Shougeng, a merchant of Arab descent, was appointed to head the Supervisorate. Leveraging his overseas connections and commercial expertise, he actively attracted foreign merchants to Quanzhou, establishing trade relations with nearly **100** countries and regions. The number of trading partners grew from 58 in the Southern Song to 98, with over **400** types of import and export goods.

The Italian traveler **Marco Polo** praised Zayton in his travels as **"one of the world's greatest harbors"**. **In 1292**, Marco Polo departed from Quanzhou's Houzhu Harbor on his return voyage to Venice. Another Italian Jewish merchant, Jacob, called Quanzhou the **"City of Light"**.

#### (五）明清以后：衰落与转型

#### (V) Ming and Qing Dynasties: Decline and Transformation

**明代**推行海禁政策，严禁沿海居民私自出海，泉州港日渐衰落。**成化八年(1472年）** ，泉州市舶司北移福州。尽管**郑和下西洋**期间曾征调泉州海船和水手，但泉州港在海外贸易中的地位已日趋下降，海洋贸易中心逐渐南移至月港。

**清代**，朝廷开放厦门为对外通商口岸，泉州港进一步转型为区域性贸易港口。尽管如此，泉州沿海的私人海上贸易仍保持活跃，不断拓展东亚、东南亚各地的商业网络。

During the **Ming Dynasty**, the government implemented the maritime ban (haijin), strictly prohibiting coastal residents from going to sea, causing Quanzhou Port to gradually decline. **In 1472 AD (the 8th year of Chenghua)** , the Maritime Trade Supervisorate was relocated north to Fuzhou. Although **Zheng He's voyages** to the Western Oceans utilized Quanzhou ships and sailors, Quanzhou's position in overseas trade steadily diminished, and the center of maritime trade gradually shifted to Yuegang.

During the **Qing Dynasty**, the court opened Xiamen as a treaty port, and Quanzhou further transitioned into a regional trade port. Nevertheless, private maritime trade along the Quanzhou coast remained active, continuously expanding commercial networks throughout East and Southeast Asia.

### 三、历史地位与意义

### III. Historical Significance

10至14世纪(宋元时期），泉州港以“刺桐港”之名驰誉世界，成为与**埃及亚历山大港齐名的“东方第一大港”** 。它是联合国教科文组织确认的**海上丝绸之路起点之一**，2021年以 **“泉州：宋元中国的世界海洋商贸中心”** 之名列入联合国教科文组织世界文化遗产名录。

泉州港的繁荣不仅体现在贸易规模上，更体现在**文化包容**上。佛教、道教、伊斯兰教、印度教、摩尼教、景教等多种宗教在此和谐共存，使泉州被誉为 **“世界宗教博物馆”** 。

From the 10th to the 14th centuries (Song and Yuan dynasties), Quanzhou Port was renowned worldwide as Zayton, standing alongside **Egypt's Alexandria as the "Greatest Port in the East"**. It is **one of the starting points of the Maritime Silk Road** confirmed by UNESCO, and in 2021, it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as **"Quanzhou: Emporium of the World in Song-Yuan China"**.

The prosperity of Quanzhou Port was reflected not only in the scale of trade but also in its **cultural inclusiveness**. Buddhism, Taoism, Islam, Hinduism, Manichaeism, Nestorian Christianity, and other religions coexisted harmoniously, earning Quanzhou the title of the **"Museum of World Religions"**.

---

泉州港从唐代初兴、宋元鼎盛到明清衰落，走过了**1300多年的沧桑历程**。它不仅是古代中国对外开放的窗口，更是东西方文明交流互鉴的历史见证，在世界海洋贸易史上留下了不可磨灭的辉煌篇章。

From its early rise in the Tang Dynasty, its peak during the Song and Yuan, to its decline in the Ming and Qing, Quanzhou Port traversed **over 1,300 years** of history. It was not only a window for ancient China's opening to the world but also a historical witness to the exchange and mutual learning between Eastern and Western civilizations, leaving an indelible glorious chapter in the history of world maritime trade.
