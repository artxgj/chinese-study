## 二、成語出處 / Origin of the Idiom

### （一）最早出處 / Earliest Origin

此成語最早見於戰國時期《莊子·山木》：

莊周遊於雕陵之樊，睹一異鵲自南方來者……睹一蟬，方得美蔭而忘其身；螳螂執翳而搏之，見得而忘其形；異鵲從而利之，見利而忘其真。

Zhuang Zhou was roaming in the park at Diao Ling when he saw a strange magpie coming from the south... He saw a cicada that had found a nice shady spot and forgotten its own safety; a mantis, hiding in the leaves, lunged at it, and in its eagerness to catch the cicada, forgot its own form; the strange magpie followed to profit from the situation, and in seeing the gain, forgot its true nature.

### （二）流傳最廣的版本 / The Most Widely Circulated Version

西漢劉向《說苑·正諫》中的典故最為完整，也是後世流傳最廣的版本：

吳王欲伐荊，告其左右曰：「敢有諫者死！」舍人有少孺子，欲諫不敢，則懷丸操彈，游於後園，露沾其衣，如是者三旦。吳王曰：「子來，何苦沾衣如此？」對曰：「園中有樹，其上有蟬。蟬高居悲鳴飲露，不知螳螂在其後也；螳螂委身曲附，欲取蟬，而不知黃雀在其傍也；黃雀延頸，欲啄螳螂，而不知彈丸在其下也。此三者皆務欲得其前利，而不顧其後之有患也。」吳王曰：「善哉！」乃罷其兵。

The King of Wu wanted to attack the State of Chu and told his attendants, “Whoever dares to remonstrate shall be put to death!” Among his retainers was a young man who wished to advise against it but dared not. So he carried a slingshot with pellets and wandered in the rear garden, his clothes wet with dew. He did this for three mornings. The King of Wu said, “Come here. Why do you go to the trouble of getting your clothes so wet?” He replied, “There is a tree in the garden, and on it there is a cicada. The cicada perches high, chirping mournfully and drinking dew, unaware that a mantis is behind it. The mantis bends its body and curves its forelegs, about to seize the cicada, unaware that an oriole is beside it. The oriole stretches its neck, about to peck the mantis, unaware that a pellet is below it. All three of them are intent on obtaining the advantage before them, without regard for the danger behind them.” The King of Wu said, “Well said!” Thereupon he called off his troops.

### 三、完整故事 / The Complete Story

#### 故事背景 / Historical Background

春秋時期，吳王（一說吳王闔閭，一說吳王夫差）準備攻打楚國。他召集大臣宣布決定，並嚴厲下令：「誰敢勸諫我，就處死！」大臣們雖認為吳國實力不足，應先養精蓄銳，但無人敢開口勸阻。

> During the Spring and Autumn period, the King of Wu (some say King Helü, others say King Fuchai) planned to attack the State of Chu. He summoned his ministers and announced his decision, sternly decreeing: “Whoever dares to remonstrate with me shall be put to death!” Although the ministers believed that Wu was not yet strong enough and should first build up its power, no one dared to speak up.

#### 少孺子巧諫 / The Young Retainer’s Clever Remonstrance

大臣中有一位正直的年輕人（「少孺子」——即年輕的侍從），他想勸諫卻又不敢直接說。他思前想後，終於想出一個辦法。

> Among the ministers was an upright young man (a “young retainer”). He wanted to advise the king but dared not speak directly. After much deliberation, he finally came up with a plan.

一連三天清晨，這位年輕人手拿彈弓，懷揣彈丸，在後花園中徘徊，衣服被露水打濕了也不在意。吳王見狀好奇地問：「你為什麼要把衣服弄得這麼濕？」

> For three mornings in a row, the young man roamed the rear garden carrying a slingshot and pellets, paying no heed to his clothes being soaked by dew. The King of Wu, curious, asked him: “Why do you go to the trouble of getting your clothes so wet?”

年輕人回答：「園中有一棵樹，樹上有一隻蟬。蟬高高在上，悲傷地鳴叫，吸飲露水，卻不知道有一隻螳螂在牠身後。螳螂彎曲著身體，伏低前肢，正要捕捉那隻蟬，卻不知道有一隻黃雀在牠旁邊。黃雀伸長脖子，正要啄食那隻螳螂，卻不知道我正拿著彈弓在樹下瞄準牠。這三者都一心只想得到眼前的利益，卻沒有顧慮到身後的禍患啊！」

> The young man replied: “There is a tree in the garden, and on it there is a cicada. The cicada perches high, chirping mournfully and drinking dew, unaware that a mantis is behind it. The mantis bends its body and curves its forelegs, about to seize the cicada, unaware that an oriole is beside it. The oriole stretches its neck, about to peck the mantis, unaware that I am below with a slingshot aimed at it. All three of them are intent on obtaining the advantage before them, without regard for the danger behind them!”

#### 吳王醒悟 / The King’s Awakening

吳王聽後恍然大悟，明白了年輕人的弦外之音：吳國攻打楚國，就如同螳螂捕蟬，卻不知旁邊還有其他國家（如越國）可能在背後伺機而動。吳王稱讚道：「說得好！」於是取消了攻打楚國的計劃。

> The King of Wu suddenly understood the young man’s implied meaning: Wu attacking Chu was like the mantis catching the cicada — unaware that other states (such as Yue) might be waiting in the wings to strike. The king exclaimed, “Well said!” and thereupon cancelled the plan to attack Chu.

## 四、成語寓意 / Moral of the Idiom

此成語諷刺了那些**只顧眼前利益，不顧身後禍患的人**。它告誡人們：

> This idiom satirizes those who **only care about immediate gains and ignore the dangers behind them**. It warns people that:

1\. **做事要瞻前顧後 / Consider both front and back**: 不能只看到眼前的利益，還要考慮可能隨之而來的風險和後果。 / One must not only see the immediate benefits but also consider the risks and consequences that may follow.

2\. **害人之心不可有 / Do not scheme against others**: 當你算計別人時，可能也有人正在算計你。 / When you are scheming against others, someone may be scheming against you.

3\. **目光要長遠 / Be farsighted**: 鼠目寸光、利令智昏的人最終只會害了自己。 / Those who are short-sighted and blinded by greed will ultimately harm only themselves.

## 五、成語用法 / Usage

此成語在句中一般充當**分句**，常用來提醒他人或自我警示，不要因為貪圖眼前的小利而忽視了潛在的巨大風險。

> This idiom generally serves as a **clause** in a sentence. It is often used to remind others or to caution oneself not to be blinded by small immediate gains while ignoring potential major risks.

#### 例句 / Example：

> 他只想著如何擊敗競爭對手，卻不知螳螂捕蟬，黃雀在後，自己早已被人盯上了。
> He was only thinking about how to defeat his competitors, unaware that — like the mantis stalking the cicada — he himself had long been targeted by others.
